    Zenith (GR300+)

    Zenith is the hardest standard content in Frontier Z: powered-up monsters from GR300 here (GR200 in the original), with Zenith armour, weapons, cuffs and skills. This page explains the monsters, their parts and materials, their new status effects and blights, and the gear you make from them.

    What Zenith is

    "Zenith" means a type of monster introduced in Frontier Z, and any equipment or skills you can only get from hunting these monsters.

    Zenith monsters start harder than the existing Exotic monsters (Nargacuga, Zinogre and so on, for example Azure Radiance, Swift Shadow and Swift Thunder in Great Forest; both are under G Rank Quest Counter > G Hunter Quests > ※ G Exotic Quests) at GR300. By GR900 they are the hardest standard content in the game, beaten only by the very hardest event-type quests, such as the Musou Zinogre quests.

    Zenith equipment is almost a flat upgrade over all standard G equipment (Raviente weapons excepted). It has higher defense, more true raw and better skill points, and gives one of the new Zenith skills for each piece, cuff or weapon you equip.

    Zenith skills are always-on skills, mostly designed to buff existing skills or to give a set more skills overall, by adding skill slots. For example the Hermitaur ZX Helm (and the Hermitaur ZX Cap) gives the Zenith skill Quake Res Up (the matching cuff is called Quake Res Up SZ). It raises your Quake Res by 1 tier, including inside combination skills such as Three Worlds. That lets the skill go past Quake Res+2 to become, in effect, Quake Res+3. These extra tiers of resistance are needed to counter Extreme Wind Pressure, Roars and Quakes.

    Zenith monsters often use levels of Roar, Quake and Wind that standard Super Earplugs and similar cannot counter. You either wear a piece of Zenith equipment that counters them, or you dodge the effects. In the same way, Poison, Paralysis, Sleep, Fire Blight and the rest are all raised, and you counter them with high enough resistance to the right element or with a Zenith skill that boosts the standard resistance. Not every status can be nullified. You can never counter Extreme Poison, so try never to be hit by it.

    Different. Zenith monsters, gear and skills are game content and work the same, except the GR they need. Zenith quests are among the 9,152 quest files the server holds (see the quest catalogue). For hitzones, HP and carves see the monster field guide; for the sets and skills see Armour: Zenith armour and Zenith skills. The original guide gives Zenith ★1 to ★4 as GR200, GR400, GR600 and GR800. On our server they are GR 300, 500, 700 and 900 (since 17 November 2026), and this page uses our numbers. The Zenith materials in the Hunting Road shop need GR300. Gear you already made stays yours. Until the next client patch, the 92 older Zenith quests at the quest counter still show and accept the old GR, and the smithy shows its Zenith menu from GR 200, though the materials come from GR 300 on. The G Exotic quests (G Rank Quest Counter > G Hunter Quests > ※ G Exotic Quests) open at GR 100 here. Where Zenith fits in the whole path: Progression: Zenith ★1 to ★3.

    Zenith monsters

    Zenith monsters are versions of existing monsters with very powerful attacks and extra abilities, such as Ultra Wind Pressure, Roars, Tremor, Sleep, Poison and Paralysis. Every Zenith monster has one highly developed part. When you break it, you get a specific material that depends on the rank.

    There are four ratings, ★1 to ★4. Each raises the monster's attack and effective health. Every version of a Zenith gives a defense reduction of 1050, the same as Exotic monster quests.

    Treat ★4 as some of the hardest required content in the game, and do not try it without proper gear. But almost all materials are the same across every tier of a Zenith monster. You only need to kill around 5 of the GR900 monster while breaking its special part to max out any Zenith equipment. You can farm everything else on the GR700 versions without trouble. More on this under Zenith materials.

    The special part

    Every Zenith has a specific extra developed body part. If you break it you get a special item with a 100% break chance. It has four variants, each 100% within one rank band. This table shows them for Zenith Espinas, plus the top one from Khezu (Anomaly Face).

    Breaking the part usually lowers the monster's threat. This varies. Some monsters lose part of their moveset, or lose properties such as Zenith-level Roar, Tremor or Wind Pressure. Others only get slightly easier.

    Is ZX Lv2 to Lv7 worth it?

    There are no decorations or other real benefits from taking ZX armour to Lv7, so you can leave it at Lv1 and miss nothing beyond some defense. The ZX upgrades are not really recommended. They add only around 30 to 50 defense, but use around 70% of the total items needed just to reach ZX Lv1.

    The table below shows how many materials that is. ZX Lv2 to Lv7 needs 142 materials in total. In comparison, Z through ZX Lv1 needs 196 materials in total. It is almost always better to get other armour pieces or work on weapons than to upgrade to ZX Lv7.

    Blights and status

    Apart from new levels of Wind Pressure, Roars and Quake, Zenith monsters bring new levels of any status effect they inflict.

    New status effects

    StatusWhat it doesCaused by
    Super PoisonWorks like other poisons, but can only be cured by picking up the purple shinies dropped on the ground during a Zenith Espinas fight. It drains up to 10 health per second at GR900. The Zenith skill Poison Res Up can reduce its effect, but there is no Super Poison immunity.Zenith Espinas
    Sound AsleepWorks like other sleep, but can only be cured by being hit by other players. It lasts a very long time.     New blights

    You can counter every new blight from Zenith monsters by having the matching resistance at 55 or higher. Fist's numbers (not tested here): the easiest way is to use both the Style Rank skill All Res Up (+14 to +19) and the individual Res Up skills (+25 to +34), plus Guild Food (+30). That can reach up to 83 in an individual resistance, which counters the blights on almost any set.

    BlightWhat it doesCaused by
    Extreme FireblightYou lose both your normal health and the length of the health bar over time. Rolling cancels it.Zenith Rathalos, Midogaron
    Extreme ThunderblightWhile you have it, taking any amount of thunder damage gives you cardiac arrest, and you need a hit to be revived. This mostly means a guaranteed cart. It runs out naturally, and another player's revival can cancel the cardiac arrest. Rolling cancels the effect.Zenith Khezu
    Extreme WaterblightYour stamina recovers extremely slowly, and infinite stamina effects are removed at once. It runs out naturally, and rolling cancels it.Zenith Daimyo Hermitaur, Plesioth
    Extreme IceblightActively drains your stamina, and freezes you in place when it hits zero. Freeze Res cannot cancel the freezing, but being hit after you freeze frees you at once. You can mash out of it both before and after the freeze.Zenith Blangonga
    Extreme DragonblightDisables every recovery item: they give a negative reaction that reduces health instead. It also disables natural healing and cancels other sources, such as vampirism or a Transcend activation. It runs out naturally, or you can cancel it with 13 rolls, but only with your weapon sheathed.Zenith Rukodiora

    Works the same. Blights and resistances are client mechanics and work the same.

    Zenith skills

    Zenith skills work like Burst, Origin or socketed Tower weapon skills, with one key difference: they mostly improve other skills, or let you have more skills overall.

    For example, an armour piece may have Guard Up. While you have any level of the skill Guard, Guard Up raises it by one level. Guard+3 (the equivalent of Zenith Guard) gives new, unique effects.

    Skill you haveNo Zenith skillGuard Up+1Guard Up+2
    No Guard skillNothingNothingNothing
    Guard+1Guard+1Guard+2Guard+3, Zenith Active
    Guard+2Guard+2Guard+3, Zenith ActiveGuard+3, Zenith Active
    Fortification+1Guard+1Guard+2Guard+3, Zenith Active
    Fortification+2Guard+2Guard+3, Zenith ActiveGuard+3, Zenith Active

    Besides buffing skills, some pieces simply give extra skill slots. This goes up to a maximum of 7: 5 Zenith armour pieces, a Zenith weapon and a Zenith cuff. ZP armour also counts towards skill extension, so on a ZP set you could in theory have 19 hand-picked skills.

    In our client the Zenith cuffs are named after the skill with SZ on the end (Guard Up SZ, Quake Res Up SZ and so on). All the Zenith skills and their effects are in the Zenith skills part of Armour, Decorations and Skills.

    Works the same. Zenith skills are client mechanics and work the same.

    Zenith armour

    Zenith armour has four levels: Z, ZF, ZY and ZX. Z and ZF have only 1 upgrade each. ZY and ZX have the usual 7. The upgrades match the GR300, GR500, GR700 and GR900 tiers of the Zenith monsters. All ZX armour is better than standard GX or GP armour, and usually has at least 25 points across 5 different skills. It also has a Zenith skill.

    There are Premium and Lottery Zenith armours with ZP upgrades. In the current sets, the progression goes from GP straight to ZP. They have less defense than a standard ZX piece, but more than a normal GX. They need only materials from a GR300 Zenith Hypnocatrice (for example Hypnocatrice with Extreme Beak), and the Lottery ones also need 2 extra tickets for each piece you upgrade to ZP.

    The skills on Lottery pieces are usually fixed and do not change when you upgrade to ZP. Premium Kits get completely reworked skills, to match the more common skills in the updates that came after their first release (Fencing replaced with Sword God, for example).

    Skill Slots Up

    All Z pieces count towards the skill slot expansion rule for G Rank armour. Five ZX pieces let you equip 12 skills, even if no piece has Skill Slots Up itself. If one piece has Skill Slots Up, that becomes 13, and so on.

Nothing is sold for real money here, and our gachas at the Gacha Cat give consumables, tickets and sigil materials, not lottery armour, so those sets are not available yet (the ZP pieces are marked not obtainable in our armour data).

    Zenith cuffs

    Zenith cuffs are simple. They all need Zenith monster materials to craft, take two slots, and are all rated SZ, which means they do not need a Premium outfit. They give no skill points of their own. Instead they give one extra Zenith skill. With an SZ cuff you could, for example, buff Thunder Clad or add an extra skill slot.

    You craft them at the skill cuff workshop; for example Quake Res Up SZ needs Daimyo Huge Shl x10, Daimyo Huge Claw x5, Daimyo Huge Leg x3 and Daimyo Huge Teeth x1. All of them take two standard Pugi cuff slots, so you cannot get skill points from cuffs while using one.

    Works the same. Zenith cuffs are client content and work the same.

    Zenith weapons

    Zenith weapons are the current standard of weapon power. Each comes with a Zenith skill that varies, and can be very enabling. They also have a Zenith Partbreaker effect that raises damage to parts by 1.50x, which cuts the hits you need on a Zenith part to 66% of what a normal weapon needs. Upgrading a weapon uses materials from all tiers, and the final upgrade needs ★4 Zenith Breaks, ★4 Common Mats, Semi-Rares and Rares.

Fist's table has no column headings, so the headings here were added. Our weapon data also has further Zenith families this table does not list (for example more Crimson Demon weapons with Wind Res Up), so treat it as a guide, not a full list.
