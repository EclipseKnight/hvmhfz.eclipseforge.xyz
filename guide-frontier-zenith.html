Zenith (GR200+)

Zenith is the hardest standard content in Frontier Z: powered-up monsters from GR200, with Zenith armour, weapons, cuffs and skills. This page explains the monsters, their parts and materials, their new status effects and blights, and the gear you make from them.

What Zenith is

"Zenith" means a type of monster introduced in Frontier Z, and any equipment or skills you can only get from hunting these monsters.

Zenith monsters start harder than the existing Exotic monsters (G Rank Nargacuga, Zinogre and so on) at GR200. By GR800 they are the hardest standard content in the game, beaten only by super hard event quests such as Howling Zinogre.

Zenith equipment is almost a flat upgrade over all standard G equipment (Raviente weapons excepted). It has higher defense, more true raw and better skill points, and gives one of the new Zenith skills for each piece, cuff or weapon you equip.

Zenith skills are always-on skills, mostly designed to buff existing skills or to give a set more skills overall, by adding skill slots. For example the Zenith Daimyo Hermitaur Headpiece gives the Zenith skill Quake Res Up. It raises your Quake Res by 1 tier, including inside combination skills such as Three Worlds Protection. That lets the skill go past Quake Res+2 to become, in effect, Quake Res+3. These extra tiers of resistance are needed to counter Extreme Wind Pressure, Roars and Quakes.

Zenith monsters often use levels of Roar, Quake and Wind that standard Super High-Grade Earplugs and similar cannot counter. You either wear a piece of Zenith equipment that counters them, or you dodge the effects. In the same way, Poison, Paralysis, Sleep, Fire Blight and the rest are all raised, and you counter them with high enough resistance to the right element or with a Zenith skill that boosts the standard resistance. Not every status can be nullified. You can never counter Extreme Poison, so try never to be hit by it.

Works the same. Zenith monsters, gear and skills are game content and work the same. Zenith quests are among the 9,152 quest files the server holds (see the quest catalogue). For hitzones, HP and carves see the monster field guide; for the sets and skills see Armour: Zenith armour and Zenith skills.

Zenith monsters

Zenith monsters are versions of existing monsters with very powerful attacks and extra abilities, such as Ultra Wind Pressure, Roars, Tremor, Sleep, Poison and Paralysis. Every Zenith monster has one highly developed part. When you break it, you get a specific material that depends on the rank.

There are four ratings, ★1 to ★4. Each raises the monster's attack and effective health. Every version of a Zenith gives a defense reduction of 1050, the same as Exotic monster quests.

Treat ★4 as some of the hardest required content in the game, and do not try it without proper gear. But almost all materials are the same across every tier of a Zenith monster. You only need to kill around 5 of the GR800 monster while breaking its special part to max out any Zenith equipment. You can farm everything else on the GR600 versions without trouble. More on this under Zenith materials.

Every Zenith has a specific extra developed body part. If you break it you get a special item with a 100% break chance. It has four variants, each 100% within one rank band. This table shows them for Zenith Espinas, plus the top one from Khezu.

Is ZX Lv2 to Lv7 worth it?

There are no decorations or other real benefits from taking ZX armour to Lv7, so you can leave it at Lv1 and miss nothing beyond some defense. The ZX upgrades are not really recommended. They add only around 30 to 50 defense, but use around 70% of the total items needed just to reach ZX Lv1.

The table below shows how many materials that is. ZX Lv2 to Lv7 needs 142 materials in total. In comparison, Z through ZX Lv1 needs 196 materials in total. It is almost always better to get other armour pieces or work on weapons than to upgrade to ZX Lv7.

Blights and status

Apart from new levels of Wind Pressure, Roars and Quake, Zenith monsters bring new levels of any status effect they inflict.

New blights

You can counter every new blight from Zenith monsters by having the matching resistance at 55 or higher. The easiest way is to use both the SR All Res Up skill (+14 to +19) and the individual Res Up skills (+25 to +34), plus Guild Food (+30). That can reach up to 83 in an individual resistance, which counters the blights on almost any set.

You can also buy Cancellation Fruit from the Pink Store NPC. It instantly cures any blights you have, as long as they have not left you unable to use it.

BlightWhat it doesCaused by
Extreme FireblightYou lose both your normal health and the length of the health bar over time. Rolling cancels it.Zenith Rathalos, Midogaron
Extreme ThunderblightWhile you have it, taking any amount of thunder damage gives you cardiac arrest, and you need a hit to be revived. This mostly means a guaranteed cart. It runs out naturally, and another player's revival can cancel the cardiac arrest. Rolling cancels the effect.Zenith Khezu
Extreme WaterblightYour stamina recovers extremely slowly, and infinite stamina effects are removed at once. It runs out naturally, and rolling cancels it.Zenith Daimyo Hermitaur, Plesioth

Zenith skills

Zenith skills work like Burst, Origin or socketed Tower weapon skills, with one key difference: they mostly improve other skills, or let you have more skills overall.

For example, an armour piece may have Guard Up. While you have any level of the skill Guard, Guard Up raises it by one level. Guard+3 (the equivalent of Zenith Guard) gives new, unique effects.

Besides buffing skills, some pieces simply give extra skill slots. This goes up to a maximum of 7: 5 Zenith armour pieces, a Zenith weapon and a Zenith cuff. ZP armour also counts towards skill extension, so on a ZP set you could in theory have 19 hand-picked skills.

All the Zenith skills and their effects are in the Zenith skills part of Armour, Decorations and Skills.

Zenith armour

Zenith armour has four levels: Z, ZF, ZY and ZX. Z and ZF have only 1 upgrade each. ZY and ZX have the usual 7. The upgrades match the GR200, GR400, GR600 and GR800 tiers of the Zenith monsters. All ZX armour is better than standard GX or GP armour, and usually has at least 25 points across 5 different skills. It also has a Zenith skill.

There are Premium and Lottery Zenith armours with ZP upgrades. In the current sets, the progression goes from GP straight to ZP. They have less defense than a standard ZX piece, but more than a normal GX. They need only materials from a GR200 Hypnoc, and the Lottery ones also need 2 extra tickets for each piece you upgrade to ZP.

The skills on Lottery pieces are usually fixed and do not change when you upgrade to ZP. Premium Kits get completely reworked skills, to match the more common skills in the updates that came after their first release (Fencing replaced with Sword God, for example).

Skill Slots Up

All Z pieces count towards the skill slot expansion rule for G Rank armour. Five ZX pieces let you equip 12 skills, even if no piece has Skill Slots Up itself. If one piece has Skill Slots Up, that becomes 13, and so on.

Zenith cuffs

Zenith cuffs are simple. They all need Zenith monster materials to craft, take two slots, and are all rated SZ, which means they do not need a Premium outfit. They give no skill points of their own. Instead they give one extra Zenith skill. With an SZ cuff you could, for example, buff Thunder Clad or add an extra skill slot.

All of them take two standard Pugi cuff slots, so you cannot get skill points from cuffs while using one.

Works the same. Zenith cuffs are client content and work the same.

Zenith weapons

Zenith weapons are the current standard of weapon power. Each comes with a Zenith skill that varies, and can be very enabling. They also have a Zenith Partbreaker effect that raises damage to parts by 1.50x, which cuts the hits you need on a Zenith part to 66% of what a normal weapon needs. Upgrading a weapon uses materials from all tiers, and the final upgrade needs ★4 Zenith Breaks, ★4 Common Mats, Semi-Rares and Rares.
