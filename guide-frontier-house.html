    House and AI Companions

    Your house facilities (the Gook Farm, Pugi Farm and Airou Plaza), and the AI companions that fight with you: Halk, Partnyaa, Fosta, Rasta and Partner.

    House

    Your house holds the facilities in this page. The bookshelf and the dining tables are in the house itself. The Gook Farm, Pugi Farm and Halk and Partnyaa areas are reached by icons at the house entrance.

    Bookshelf and dining tables

    Bookshelf
    Lets you check your monster kills, see information on Elements and Hunting Horn songs, change your house's style, and change the title shown above your name in town. Changing the house style costs GCP. The guide says it is not worth spending if you have anything else to spend it on.
    Dining tables
    A fully upgraded house has three dining tables. They feed two, four and eight people in your house. The guide says the bento function is much easier to manage than using your house for this. This is also where your Partner and your active Partnyaa live. If you talk to them here, you can give them HRP Tickets so both rank up faster.

    Display Gallery

    The display gallery is a customisable room where you set up furniture and boxes. Its main use is getting Gallery Gems and other items. You get these decent gems by asking the Furniture NPC to evaluate your house. Apart from vanity, the room has little purpose, because the prizes for gallery ratings are mostly outdated and have little use. See Gallery Gems.

    Works the same. House interior saves and the bookshelf for visitors were fixed on 2 Nov.

    Gook Farm

    The Gook Farm has a number of cats and an area where you can make a home for gooks (the ducks). Gooks sometimes give free items after quests. They also let you use Gook Cooking, which turns items into other items.

    Farm tools

    The cat sitting on the tree stump in your farm gets you materials after each quest. Talk to the cat and you can use one of the available tools, once per quest.

    You upgrade these tools by making furniture for the Display Gallery. Stamps are given based on the total you spend, and you can redeem them for the option to buy better tools. The guide had an image guide for making furniture for stamps easily.

    The tools are: Plants and Herbs, Tools and Junk, Cook Raw Meat, Ores and Stones, Insects, Mocha Pots and Bargains.

    • Most options give what their names say. For most of them, they are only truly useful before G Rank, as real farming is always better.
    • Mocha Pots and Cooking are always useful. Mocha pots must be bought in the shop. The guide says a pot's stages are counted in quests taken with the pot in the ground rather than in minutes, with rainbow appearing after red. The Monster Hunter wiki has a general guide to how Mocha pots work (page "Pickled Pot Mocha"). Mocha Pot recipes are in Items: Mocha Pots.
    • Bargains only appears at random, under Mocha pots. It gives useful tools and materials such as Mega Pickaxes, Max Potions and rare ores.

    Getting your first Gook

    1. Enter your Gook Farm from your house by choosing the Gook icon.
    2. Talk to the Manager Felyne at the rocky trash heap at the back of the farm.
    3. Talk to the Guild Master, open the Gook Quests section and pick the quest for the gook colour you want. White, Yellow, Black, Brown and Blue are the starter choices.

    For the quest you only need to make the quest monster drop a shiny. After that, bring it back to the base camp and put it in the Red Box. The supply box for these quests has Mega Juices and Sleep Throwing Knives to make the delivery easier. The methods for each quest are:

    Egg questHow to get the shiny
    Yian Kut-Ku in the JungleUse a Sonic Bomb for the shiny.
    Congalala in the SwampBreak its tail.
    Daimyo Hermitaur in the DesertUse a Sonic Bomb while it guards, or interrupt it after a zone change.
    Basarios in the VolcanoMake it leap up out of the ground.
    Tigrex in the Snowy MountainsGet it to charge into a wall in Area 8.

    After you deliver the egg and get back to town:

    1. Go into your farm, talk to your Manager again and mash through his text. Then return to the Guild Master.
    2. Open the Gook Quests section again. You should see only one quest: deliver 1 Blueprints. Although the quest says 1, it is really 5. You get them by beating up the Melynx wandering in any zone.
    3. Enter your farm, talk to the Manager, then leave and enter again. He will shout that he has turned his kingdom of trash into a bed for the child he is fathering for you.
    4. Go up to the egg and interact with it. Choose any option for the first prompt. For the second, choose these: First, Normal Sized Gook. Second, Smaller Gook. Third, Larger Gook. You can do this once per quest taken, so you need about five quests, interacting with the egg after each one.
    5. When the egg is ready to hatch, the Manager shouts to tell you.
    6. After the hatching scene, name the gook and interact with it three times. The first time you pick it up and the second time you feed it. Then talk to the Manager again so your next quest appears at the Guild Master.
    7. That quest is much the same. You deliver 5 more blueprints, from Melynx shinies.
    8. After the quest, talk to the Manager, then leave and re-enter. A cutscene shows the new pond you got for completing the basic quest line. This unlocks Gook Cooking.

    If you want more gooks, repeat the egg process to get new ones.

    Gook interactions

    A board lets you change your gooks' clothing and the look of the bed and pond facilities. You can talk to gooks with the confirm button, like any NPC, to open a detailed interaction menu. From it you can carry them, feed them items, or send them away to die in the wilderness.

    Gook tools

    Once you own a Gook, you now and then get batches of items, from the caretaker or the shinies. These can include materials for Gook Tools. They are special versions of the Pickaxe, Bug Net and Fishing Bait, plus a new Gathering Glove tool. They have a fixed chance of doubling anything you gather with them. For example, with Gook Fishing Bait you have a chance to get 2x of whatever you fish out instead of 1x.

    You can carry up to three Lost Balls. The guide says they can have up to four different effects depending on the situation, and lists three:

    • Effect 1: Randomly a Farcaster or a Nearcaster effect.
    • Effect 2: If the map has a random spawn, it randomly either warps you to a random area or gives the Effect 1 result.
    • Effect 3: If you have a Gook Charm, you are warped to the secret area if it is available, or when you are inside it or it is unavailable you get Effect 2.

    Gook gathering quests and Gook Junk

    The Guild Master has special Gook Gathering Quests. They work like normal quests, with Gook Junk added to the item pool and a chance to meet giant Gooks. Look up Gook Junk in the database to find maps if you want to grind 99 of them for the Gook Charm. Junk is a flat 4% chance from gathering and 100% from finding Giant Gooks. G Rank Sigils can raise the Giant Gook spawn rate.

    Gook Cooking

    Once you have a Gook you can do Gook Cooking (or Stewing). It turns a batch of items, or single items, into other types. Cooking is a simple timing minigame. Watch the pot lid bounce: it makes small bounces, then a larger one that lifts the Gook into the air. If you press confirm when it lands back on the pot after the larger jump, you always succeed.

    A recipe worth using makes Mysterious Stones by cooking 10 Mysterious Shards. You can buy the stone for 2,200 GCP, or the shard for 200 GCP. You can also go to the Island in the Jungle and gather without limit. Any G Rank insect gathering spot has a flat 5% chance of a Mysterious Shard, so you can hold gather and wait until you have enough.

    Even if you buy the shards, having a Gook is worth it. Cooking a stack of 10 costs less than buying the stone (you save 200 GCP). See Mysterious Stones.

    Another useful recipe, for mid to late game grinds, is for the items in the Hiden grind. You can trade HC carves for Hiden Souls, GHC carves for Hiden Ribbons and Conquest Weapon Gems for Hiden Merits. You will naturally collect at least two of these types, so this helps. See Hiden grind.

    Lost Cat

    The Lost Cat is an NPC that spawns in your Gook Farm with a certain chance after quests. He asks you to help him find an item he lost, and gives items in return. These can include Mystery Crowns and their G Rank equivalent, used to craft an armour set that cannot be obtained anywhere else in the game.

    To find the lost item quickly, the guide used an image guide: skip through his text once, talk to him again, and match the leftmost column of text to the image. The guide's author was missing 2 images for this, so you may sometimes need to search those 2 areas if you roll that location.

    Works the same. The farm and Gook Cooking are game content and should work the same; we did not test them in detail.

    Pugi Farm (My Tore)

    The Pugi Farm, or My Tore, opens after you reach HR3. It is a customisable area where you can set up a Pugi, facilities to train it, and Airou (Felyne) Stores that offer unique items and combinations found nowhere else. The icon of a Pugi (pig) at your house opens it. At G Rank, the guide says to spam the Talk option on the Manager to get up to 100P free, which you can use for the G Cuff licence at GR50.

    Pugis are exceptionally important. They are the Frontier version of talismans or charms, and add extra skill points to skills. Almost every endgame set needs a Pugi, so it should be your first priority at the Pugi Garden.

    Points

    After HR5, HR6 and HR7 you can claim a batch of Points by talking to the farm Manager, for a maximum of 90P. You can also claim points you forgot to collect in the same way. Up to 14P are stored. Anything past that is lost for good.

    At first, spend all your points on getting a Pugi (30P), then a Departure Facility (30P). After that, buy either a Premium Outfit or one of the Standard ones (20P), so your Pugi is not wearing only a nappy. After you have the Pugi items, the guide suggests working on the Airou Plaza stores.

    It is also wise to save points until you have 60P spare. When you reach G Rank you must spend 60P on the G Rank Pugi Cuff Licence to use G Rank cuffs. This applies to both Premium and Normal cuffs and cannot be bypassed. You also get 100P just for reaching G Rank (spam the Talk option on your manager), so the licence is no problem.

    • Standard Outfits cost 20P on top of the 60P for a Pugi and the Departure Facility. They have two slots for any 1 or 2 slot Standard Cuffs. Premium cuffs cannot be used with them at all. The default nappy outfit takes Standard cuffs, so a Standard outfit is optional.
    • Premium Outfits are overall the better choice. They have two slots for Premium or Standard cuffs, or a mix. Premium Cuffs only fit Premium Outfits. The best cuffs before G Rank are Premium Cuffs, especially for Blademasters. You can get cuffs with Artisan that enable Sharpness+1 where you would otherwise need several gems.

    Manager requests

    Most of what the Manager asks is simple questions of no real importance. After you get a Pugi, she can also ask for items to heal it:

    • The Eldest Sister requests: 3 Sleep Herbs and 5 Ice Crystals.
    • The Middle Sister requests: 4 Sleepy Fish and 3 Cold Meat G.
    • The Youngest Sister requests: 2 Nutrients and 1 Cold Drink.

    Cold Meat G is made by combining normal Cold Meat with a Universal BBQ Kit, then doing perfect cooking with the spit that results. A Universal BBQ Kit is made by combining a Female BBQ Kit with a Universal BBQ Spit. Cold Meat is made by combining Raw Meat with Ice Crystals. (Grill recipes.)

    Carried Items (GR350)

    Taking a Pugi on quests

    To take a Pugi on quests, spend points on three things:

    1. A Departure Facility within Pugi Facilities (30P).
    2. 1 Pugi within Pugi Expansion, under Increase Pugi Count (30P).
    3. Either the free Standard Outfit, or a 20P outfit within Pugi Expansion, or a Premium Outfit from the Item Mall.

    You can equip a Pugi Cuff by talking to the Manager, at the Blacksmith, or from an equipment box. G Rank cuffs need a licence, bought within Pugi Facilities for 60P.

    After you have the Pugi and the facility (a trampoline on the lowest level of the Pugi Farm), use the board on the lowest level of the farm. It sets which facility each of your Pugis uses. Use it to choose the Departure Facility for a Pugi. The Pugi leaves with an animation, and can then come on quests with you. Only one Pugi can use each facility.

    With your Pugi along on a quest, you can get extra carves at the Pugi Dirt Pile, based on what you carved yourself during that quest.

    Pugi cuffs

    Pugi Cuffs are the Frontier equivalent of charms or talismans. Unlike mainline charms, cuffs always have fixed skill point values. There is no luck involved apart from getting the items to make them. A cuff with +4 Earplugs, for example, will always have exactly +4 Earplugs when crafted. You craft cuffs at the Blacksmith. There are two types:

    • Premium cuffs need items that were paid for, and need a Premium Outfit (also paid for) on your Pugi.
    • Standard cuffs can go on both Premium Outfits and Standard Outfits. You get one Standard Outfit free, and more cost 20P at the Pugi Manager.

    Below G Rank, Standard cuffs are not very good next to Premium ones. At G Rank both are very good, and equally able to make an endgame set. G Rank cuffs need GR50 and a 60P licence bought in the Pugi Facilities menu. You equip a cuff by talking to the Manager, at the Blacksmith, or from an equipment box.

    The guide recommends buying a Premium Outfit, and, if you go the paid route, a pack of G premium skill cuffs when relevant. Non-G premium cuffs are not very necessary but are nice if you have the money. If you buy a non-G pack, use the search function in the crafting window to find a cuff you want and work out whether you want the first or second pack.

    Different. Premium outfits from the Item Mall are not sold here, because nothing is paid for (our gift list has a "Poogie Dressup!" gift; what it holds was not checked). Every account has the Hunter Life and Premium courses, so the subscriber perks should apply (not tested in game). Timers the server handles count from midnight Japan time, not Taiwan time; we did not check the Pugi Farm reset.

    Airou Plaza

    Airou Plaza is part of the Pugi Farm. It has stores that you set Felynes to staff. To use it, you buy Stores and Staff Capacity. Then you talk to the cats that wander your square and try to hire them. Open the Airou Plaza menu with the last option on your Farm Manager.

    Extending the Plaza

    The Extend Plaza option lets you spend points on these facilities:

    FacilityWhat it does
    Staff Capacity: 30P per slot (maximum 5)Lets you hire more Felyne workers for stores.
    General StoreSells general items such as Potions. Also has exclusive items such as Mega Demondrugs, Mega Armourdrugs and Paintballs.
    Combining StoreCombines items. Has a number of exclusive combinations, for items such as Fast Whetstones or Heavy Bowgun Coolant.
    BarGives items to anyone who visits your Pugi Farm. Can give you items at Level 3.
    Clothing StoreLets you buy clothing that changes the items cats gather while out adventuring.
    Adventure HouseLets you send cats to specific places to gather items.

    The guide's table gives the costs for the five stores as 30P, 50P and 50P, which reads as the cost of levels 1, 2 and 3.

    Hiring cats

    To hire a cat, talk to one of the cats wandering your Plaza. You can turn the wandering cats on and off with the last option on the Cat Manager. Talking gives four options: Talk, Listen to Desires, Attempt to Hire and Status. Choosing Attempt to Hire gives three more:

    • Convince with Money
    • Convince with Items
    • Convince with Sweet Talking

    You likely cannot read the text that hints at what each cat wants. The best option is Convince with Items. Giving fairly rare monster materials, or general materials such as Union Ores, has a decent chance of working. Talking has a low chance, and money is likely to fail even if you try to bribe with 9,999,999z. You only get one attempt per cat, so choose wisely. Once you hire a cat, use the manager to put it to work.

    Managing cats

    Talk to the Cat Manager to manage your cats. The second option sets what each hired cat is doing now: General Store, Combining Store, Adventure House, Clothing Store, Bar, Standby or Dismiss. Be careful not to choose Dismiss by accident, or you lose the cat you hired.

    SR Challenge Room

    The Challenge Room is an area where you can take special challenges that raise your stats. You can also take pure vanity challenges, which give special decorations inside the room, and view any of the SR300 Hiden Sets you have crafted for each weapon.

    The stat quests matter a lot toward the endgame, because they raise the maximum attack value you can have active. See Style Rank for the details.

    Works the same. The challenges are quest content in the client and work the same.

    AI Companions

    You have several kinds of AI companions: the Halk (always yours), Partnyaas, Fostas, Rastas and the Partner. Each has its own section below.

    You set the priority of your AI companions in the options, in the NPC section. Press confirm to move the options around. The menu note says the highest number is the one that spawns in quests with other players present, and that Halks do not take a slot.

    You can turn on or off the chat your companions send during quests in the Chat options. More on both screens: Options menus. G Rank advice for these companions: AI companions at G Rank.

    Works the same. The companion options are game content; the sections below say what we checked for each kind.

    Halks

    You are given a Halk from your first log in, and it stays with you in quests unless you or the quest turn it off. After you finish a quest, the Halk may drop an egg for you to collect. It can hold skills for the Halk to use, or items for combining and general use.

    To manage your Halk, go to your house's entrance and choose the Halk icon. There you can feed it and manage its abilities and training.

    • Feeding uses up Fullness, which is how full your Halk is. It raises the Halk's elemental and status stats, and the Halk changes form when a stat reaches 100. For example, 100 in poison and dragon gives a dragon-element Halk that also uses poison.
    • Training covers the five stats your Halk has. When all five bars are full, your Halk levels up. Halks are currently limited to level 3.
    • Skills for Halks are unlocked by picking up eggs dropped at the end of quests. The original guide kept a full list of the Halk skills you can equip in its spreadsheet, which we do not link. At GR150 you can equip a third skill.
    • Halk Pot: once a day you can claim a Halk Pot from the ground near the Halk's rock. It gives a massive damage reduction. You can have one per day and use a maximum of five. A new pot appears and the limits reset at midnight Taiwan time (on the official servers). If you do not claim a pot, you lose it. See Halk Potion.

    Some items are made only with Halk Feathers, which drop after quests. They include a drink that adds a flat 30% affinity to your weapon for 10 minutes. There are also versions for each element, which add a flat 1.1x elemental multiplier to your weapon. The guide said it would add the recipes later.

    Felyne Partnyaas

    Even if you prefer Fostas, make a Partnyaa straight away and send it to the Guild Hall. It helps everyone in the guild, and Guild Partnyaas are the only source of some materials. Partnyaas are Felyne companions like those in MHFU and MHP3rd. You manage them in the same area as Halks. To hire a Partnyaa, talk to the Halk manager and choose his Partnyaa option. This takes you into Airou Creation mode.

    There are five tabs for customising your Partnyaa: Pattern, Eyes, Ears, Fur Colour and Tail. After you confirm the look, you name your Partnyaa and it appears on the island over the bridge. You can start with 1 or 2 Partnyaas (2 if you are in a guild, 1 if not). You can get more once one reaches PNR 300.

    What a Partnyaa is good for

    Partnyaas are not very helpful for actually killing monsters. But an active Partnyaa on a quest gives you a second break roll on every part you break. This is the only way to get extra break rolls on a quest. They also have a chance to steal materials from monsters they attack, and to gather when they are near gathering spots. See also the Guild Hall and Hunter Festival Partnyaas.

    Skill mechanics

    Partnyaa skills work the same as hunters' skills. You put decorations with skill points into armour or weapons, and when you have enough points the matching skill activates. The system looks simple, but to make anything decent you need a Guild Hall, Pugi Farm facilities and many combined decorations.

    For example, a decoration with only Demon Flute is easy. A decoration with Demon Flute, Quake Res, Encourage and Recovery Time is very convoluted. You make several hybrid decorations, combine them into the final one, and get materials from several facilities.

    Partnyaa facilities

    Sending your Partnyaa to different facilities gets you new items.

    • Guild Hall: Your Partnyaa joins Treasure Hunting groups. These get materials for Partnyaa Armours and Decorations, and for player armour and Pugi Cuffs.
    • Pugi Farm: Adds Partnyaa decoration materials to the Combo Booth, Adventure House and Item Shop. The Pugi facilities need you to have spent the points on them first, which can take a long time.

    A Partnyaa can only change facility once a day (it resets at 12:00 Taiwan time on the official servers). You also have to use the booths properly to get the effects. For instance, you must send a cat out with the Adventure House to get the items it brings.

    Fostas and Rastas

    Fostas

    Fostas are the free extra hunters you see on quests when you have a free slot. They are quite strong for the earliest content, but fall off quickly around HR5.

    Rastas

    Access the bar from the stairs in town. The in-house option is only for Private Bars. Any armour or weapons given to a Partner are permanently lost from your own box.

    Rastas are clones of other players, who can be given to up to three people. They use the gear their owner sets on them, and join you on quests. A Rasta's strength depends on two things: your own Guild Loyalty and Reliability and the Rasta owner's armour and decorations.

    The guide says you need HR2 to create or contract a Rasta, and an active subscription to the game. It had an image guide for unlocking and hiring a Rasta, the menus and the mechanics. To change the equipment your Rasta uses in other players' games, you do the same as changing your own equipment, but choose the Rasta option instead of quests or town. See Equipping gear.

    • Guild Loyalty raises Attack, Elemental and Status attack values. On a Rasta you contracted from someone else, the other player must raise this.
    • Guild Reliability raises Defense and allows more skills to be active.

    Legendary Rastas

    On the official servers you needed a Premium (VIP) subscription to contract a Legendary Rasta. These Rastas are very strong.     Partners

    Access the bar from the stairs in town. The in-house option is only for Private Bars. Any armour or weapons given to a Partner are permanently lost from your own box.

    A Partner is close to a paid Premium Rasta in damage, and better at support. The guide strongly recommends putting effort into one. A Partner is much like a Rasta, but it is not a clone of another player. It lives with you, and you gear it up and customise it as you like. See also Partners at G Rank.

    Getting a Partner

    You need to be HR2 and have bought the first house size upgrade from the store (the 10,000z House icon). Then visit the Rasta bar and talk to the Legendary Rasta that holds the weapon you want. Choose Take Partner.

    • The best weapons to give a Partner are Sword and Shield or Dual Blades.
    • Hunting Horn is unreliable in the timing of its buffs.
    • Ranged weapons have terrible AI. If you mainly play a ranged weapon, a Long Sword or Great Sword may be better, to get some tail cuts.
    • Other weapons are usable but generally do worse than the above.
    1. Choose a gender.
    2. Choose a personality. It changes the Partner's looks and the flavour text it uses in quests.
    3. Confirm your choices. The final box lists Gender, Personality and Weapon, and warns that the choice cannot be changed afterwards.
    4. Go to your House. The NPC you talked to is there, and you can customise your Partner.

    If you make a mistake while creating your Partner, talk to the cat next to the bar and cancel them. This completely deletes the Partner, and you start over with name, gender and personality. To change only the face or voice, you can use Premium Restyle Points from the Item Shop (which cost real money on the official servers). You cannot change gender except by dismissing the Partner and hiring a new one.

    Partner weapon stats

    Partner weapons work differently from those on humans or Rastas. A number of things define each weapon.

    • Raw values depend only on the tier of the weapon. For example, a standard G Rank Lance has 736 Attack, at Lv1 or Lv50 alike.
    • Elemental values depend on the Weapon Rank your Partner has for that weapon type. For example, at rank 999 with Tonfa every element has a value of 1500, whether it is a hybrid element like Black Flame or plain Fire or Dragon. This ignores the actual values on the weapon, other than the element type.
    • Status values are the only value taken from the weapon. It is 100% of the value on the weapon, or 50% for Tonfas. If your Partner is meant to use status in a support role, Burst or Origin weapons can be ideal, as they do not need the full 50 levels a standard G Rank weapon needs to reach peak status values.

    Partner skills

    Partners can use many of the same skills a player can, and the effects of the skills are identical to those on players. But the mechanics and limits are quite different. Partner skills are set from a list and bought with GCP. There is a hierarchy to unlocking them, and some of the best (Encourage +2, Strong Attack) need you to rank the Partner up to PR999.

    The Skills menu has four options: Learn Skill, Passive Skills, Equip Skills and Manual.

    Learn Skills

    The Learn Skills option lists the skills you can buy now. Black skills are the ones you can buy, blue skills are ones you already bought, and gray skills are ones you cannot afford yet. All skills are unlocked with GCP. The Caravan Festival is one of the best sources of GCP for ranking up your Partner's abilities. If you finish enough routes it can give many thousands of GCP. See Caravan.

    Passive Skills

    Passive skills need your Partner to have maxed a weapon's rank (999). Then start Kaidan by choosing the last option on the Legendary Rasta, in the same way you first unlocked a weapon or chose a Partner. Passive skills are always on. The basic options are Additional Skill Slots and Extra Cost Budget.

    • Slot upgrades (Equippable Skills +1) add one skill each, up to +2, for a maximum of 12 skills. The guide says you probably never need these.
    • Cost upgrades (Skill Cost +1) add +5 to the maximum Cost each, up to a maximum Cost of 260. You should get all of these, as they are very good.

    Once your Partner reaches Rank 999 in an individual weapon class, you also unlock the option to raise that weapon's maximum attack value. This does not raise the raw values. It gives a higher attack ceiling, which helps with skills such as Adrenaline. It is well worth taking the ceiling for your Partner's main weapon class, as you are likely to hit the ceiling with Strong Attack +5 and similar DPS skills.

    Pushing each weapon to MASTER level adds true raw to all weapon classes. Maxing out Kaidan Rank to 999 adds more true raw to the weapon class you maxed.

    Equip Skills

    Equip Skills shows a list of skills and a list of those you have equipped. In the equipped-skills window, the numbers in the right column are the cost of each skill, and the total cost you can have equipped is shown above them. It is fairly straightforward.

    Skills that share the same effect do not actually stack. Equipping Strong Attack with normal Attack does not give two separate values. It just switches off the weaker skill, while still using up Cost.

    Partner items

    You can only have one type of item in use on Partners. Putting points into a second item type replaces the one you already have.

    The Items option sets your Partner to use items that help it advance and help in quests. The first menu is for HRP/GRP Tickets of various kinds. The second sets your Partner to use Wide Area Powders, Flutes, Throwing Knives or Traps. The guide strongly recommends matching this to what the owner of your Rasta has set. For example, if your contracted Rasta plays Flutes, set Powders on your own Partner for the best result.

    Partners are very useful when ranked up and given the right skills. They can give party-wide buffs and can easily deal as much damage as another human player. The guide recommends investing in one.
