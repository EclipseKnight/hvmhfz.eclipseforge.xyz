    House and AI Companions

    Your house facilities (the Gu-ku Farm, My Tore and the Felyne Square), and the AI companions that fight with you: Halk, Partnya, Fosta, Rasta and Partner.

    House

    Your house holds the facilities in this page. The bookshelf and the dining tables are in the house itself. The Gu-ku Farm, My Tore and the Halk and Partnya areas are reached by icons at the house entrance (our client labels the boards Partnya Board, Poogie Board, Goocoo Care Board and Gallery Board). Our client calls the Gu-ku Farm "My Garden". The server notes below say what we checked.

    Bookshelf and dining tables

    Bookshelf
    Lets you check the monsters you have hunted and how many, the titles you have earned, and Hunting Horn melodies (you buy the melody book at the General Store first). Visitors to your house can read it to see your hunting record. You also set the title shown above your name on it, and see your Poogie and Gu-ku outfits there. You can also buy and switch interior themes there for GCP (the client's Purchase Theme, Change Theme and Change Furniture). The guide says that is not worth it if you have anything else to spend GCP on.
    Dining tables
    A fully upgraded house has three dining tables. They feed two, four and ten people in your house (our client labels them Meal (2P), Meal (4P) and Meal (10P)). The guide says the bento function is much easier to manage than using your house for this. This is also where your Partner lives (our client: after you pick gender and personality, you can meet the Partner in My House). Your active Partnya lives on its own island, see below. The guide says that if you talk to them here, you can give them HRP Tickets so both rank up faster.

    Display Gallery

    The display gallery is a customisable room where you set up furniture and boxes. Its main use is getting the Gallery Atk Deco and Gallery Def Deco decorations and other items. The guide says you get these decent decorations by asking the Furniture NPC to evaluate your house. Apart from vanity, the room has little purpose, because the prizes for gallery ratings are mostly outdated and have little use. See Other kinds of decorations.

    Works the same. House interior saves and the bookshelf for visitors were fixed on 2 Nov.

    Gu-ku Farm

    The Gu-ku Farm (our client calls it "My Garden") has a number of cats and an area where you can make a home for gu-kus (the ducks). Gu-kus sometimes give free items after quests. They also let you use Gu-ku Cooking, which turns items into other items.

    Farm tools

    The cat sitting on the tree stump in your farm (our client calls it the Straw Felyne) gets you materials after each quest. Talk to the cat and you can use one of the available tools, once per quest.

    You upgrade these tools by making furniture for the Display Gallery. Stamps are given for furniture you buy with materials (our client's text: 1 Stamp per 3,000z, or 1 per 2,000z with the Extra Course; zenny purchases do not count), and you can redeem them for the option to buy better tools (our client: collect Stamps to level up the garden tools). The guide had an image guide for making furniture for stamps easily.

    The guide lists the tools as: Plants and Herbs, Tools and Junk, Cook Raw Meat, Ores and Stones, Insects, Mocha Pots and Bargains. Our client has the labels Fish, Bugs, Junk, Bargains and Surprises in its Gu-ku farm text, so the names here may differ; we have not seen the list in game.

    Our client has similar strings in the Gu-ku care screens (Fish, Bugs, Junk, Bargains and Surprises); we did not match them one to one with the guide's list above.

    • Most options give what their names say. For most of them, they are only truly useful before G Rank, as real farming is always better.
    • Mocha Pots and Cooking are always useful. The guide says Mocha pots must be bought in the shop. That holds here: the General Store (Tools tab) sells the Mocha Pot, listed at 300z and 90z here with the Premium course. The guide says a pot's stages are counted in quests taken with the pot in the ground rather than in minutes, with rainbow appearing after red. The Monster Hunter wiki has a general guide to how Mocha pots work (page "Pickled Pot Mocha"). Mocha Pot recipes are in Items: Mocha Pots.
    • Bargains only appears at random, under Mocha pots. It gives useful tools and materials such as Mega Pickaxes, Max Potions and rare ores.

    Getting your first Gu-ku

    1. Enter your Gu-ku Farm from your house by choosing the Gu-ku icon (our client calls it My Garden).
    2. Talk to the Manager Felyne at the rocky trash heap at the back of the farm (our client's name for the garden's Goocoo NPC is the Felyne Caretaker).
    3. Talk to the Guild Master, open the Gu-ku Quests section and pick the quest for the gu-ku colour you want. White, Yellow, Black, Brown and Blue are the starter choices. Not on Hunsterverse: our client's quest menus have no Gu-ku Quests section, and none of the egg quests below is listed at any quest counter, so this route to a first gu-ku may not work here (see the note at the end of this section).

    The guide says that for the quest you only need to make the quest monster drop a shiny. After that, bring it back to the base camp and put it in the Red Box. The supply box for these quests has Mega Juices and Sleep Knives (the guide says) to make the delivery easier. The methods for each quest are:

    1. Go into your farm, talk to your Manager again and mash through his text. Then return to the Guild Master.
    2. Open the Gu-ku Quests section again. You should see only one quest: The Stolen Blueprints (Jungle, not offered on Hunsterverse). The guide says it asks to deliver 1 blueprint but really needs 5; our quest data says 5. You get them by beating up the Melynx wandering in any zone.
    3. Enter your farm, talk to the Manager, then leave and enter again. He will shout that he has turned his kingdom of trash into a bed for the child he is fathering for you.
    4. Go up to the egg and interact with it. Choose any option for the first prompt. For the second, choose these: First, Normal Sized Gu-ku. Second, Smaller Gu-ku. Third, Larger Gu-ku. You can do this once per quest taken, so you need about five quests, interacting with the egg after each one.
    5. When the egg is ready to hatch, the Manager shouts to tell you.
    6. After the hatching scene, name the gu-ku and interact with it three times. The first time you pick it up and the second time you feed it. Then talk to the Manager again so your next quest appears at the Guild Master.

    8. After the quest, talk to the Manager, then leave and re-enter. A cutscene shows the new pond you got for completing the basic quest line. This unlocks Gu-ku Cooking. Our client's G Rank letter says only that Goocoos raised by G Rank hunters can cook items, and to talk to the Felyne Caretaker in My Garden for more.

    If you want more gu-kus, repeat the egg process to get new ones.

    Gu-ku interactions

    A board lets you change your gu-kus' clothing and the look of the bed and pond facilities. You can talk to gu-kus with the confirm button, like any NPC, to open a detailed interaction menu. From it you can carry them, feed them items, or send them away to die in the wilderness.

    Gu-ku tools

    Once you own a Gu-ku, you now and then get batches of items, from the caretaker or the shinies. These can include materials for Gu-ku Tools. They are special versions of the Pickaxe, Bug Net and Fishing Bait, plus a new Gathering Glove tool. They have a fixed chance of doubling anything you gather with them. For example, with Gu-ku Fishing Bait you have a chance to get 2x of whatever you fish out instead of 1x.

    • Effect 3: If you have a Gu-ku Amulet, you are warped to the secret area if it is available, or when you are inside it or it is unavailable you get Effect 2.

    Gu-ku Cooking

    Once you have a Gu-ku you can do Gu-ku Cooking (or Stewing). It turns a batch of items, or single items, into other types (our client has a "Goocoo Pot" cooking screen where you pick the rank and species of the items, and the result can be Good Job, Half-Baked or Charcoal). The guide says cooking is a simple timing minigame. Watch the pot lid bounce: it makes small bounces, then a larger one that lifts the Gu-ku into the air. If you press confirm when it lands back on the pot after the larger jump, you always succeed.

    A recipe worth using makes Mysterious Stones by cooking 10 Mysterious Shards (the guide's names; we could not match them to items in our item list). On the official servers you could buy the stone for 2,200 GCP or the shard for 200 GCP; on Hunsterverse the GCP shop sells only Diva Defense items, so neither is for sale. You can also go to the Island in the Jungle and gather without limit. Any G Rank insect gathering spot has a flat 5% chance of a Mysterious Shard, so you can hold gather and wait until you have enough.

    Having a Gu-ku is worth it: cooking turns the shards you gather into stones (on the official servers, cooking 10 bought shards also cost 200 GCP less than buying the stone). See Mysterious Stones.

    Another useful recipe, for mid to late game grinds, is for the items in the Hiden grind. You can trade HC carves for Hiden Souls, GHC carves for Hiden Ribbons and Conquest Weapon Gems for Hiden Merits. You will naturally collect at least two of these types, so this helps. See Hiden grind.

    Lost & Found Felyne (the guide's Lost Cat)

    The Lost & Found Felyne (the guide calls him the Lost Cat; our client has a "Lost & Found Felyne" who gives gifts) is an NPC that spawns in your Gu-ku Farm with a certain chance after quests. He asks you to help him find an item he lost, and gives items in return. The guide says these can include Lost Crowns and their G Rank equivalent (Lost Luxury Crown), used to craft an armour set that cannot be obtained anywhere else in the game.

    To find the lost item quickly, the guide used an image guide: skip through his text once, talk to him again, and match the leftmost column of text to the image. The guide's author was missing 2 images for this, so you may sometimes need to search those 2 areas if you roll that location.

    Different. The farm and Gu-ku Cooking are game content (Cooking not tested in detail). Our server stores your Gu-ku: since 24 Nov a released Gu-ku keeps its place, so hatching a new one no longer overwrites one you kept (Gu-ku lost before the fix cannot be brought back), and character backups include your Gu-ku. Gu-ku that wander in town do not exist in this client.

    My Tore

    My Tore opens at HR4 (our client: "You can go to My Tore if HR4 or higher"; the client code checks for HR 51 or higher). It is a customisable area where you can set up a Poogie, facilities to train it, and Felyne Square stores that offer unique items and combinations found nowhere else. The icon of a Poogie (pig) at your house opens it. At G Rank, the guide says to spam the Speak option on the Manager to get up to 100P free (our client caps your points at 99), which you can use for the G Rank License at GR50.

    Poogies are exceptionally important. They are the Frontier version of talismans or charms, and add extra skill points to skills. Almost every endgame set needs a Poogie, so it should be your first priority at the My Tore.

    Points

    The guide says that after HR5, HR6 and HR7 you can claim a batch of points by talking to the farm Manager, for a maximum of 90P. We found no such rank grant in our client code or on our server. You can claim points you forgot to collect by talking to the manager: our client code gives up to 14 missed days of points as a talk topic (once per character as far as we could tell). Your points are capped at 99. Anything past that is lost.

    At first, spend all your points on getting a Poogie (30P), then a Departure Facility (30P). After that, buy one of the Standard outfits (20P each; Premium Outfits are not sold on Hunsterverse, see the note after Skill cuffs), so your Poogie is not wearing only a nappy. After you have the Poogie items, the guide suggests working on the Felyne Square stores.

    It is also wise to save points until you have 60P spare. When you reach G Rank you must spend 60P on the G Rank License to use G Rank cuffs. This applies to both Premium and Normal cuffs and cannot be bypassed. The guide says you also get 100P just for reaching G Rank (spam the Speak option on your manager), but we found no such grant here: our client text for GR50 only unlocks the G Rank Poogie, and no gift on our server gives Tore Points. If you are short, the Hunting Road shop's Special tab sells the 30P Poogie Tkt for 30,000 Road Points and the 5P Pugi Tkt for 5,000 (GR 1).

    • Standard Outfits cost 20P on top of the 60P for a Poogie and the Departure Facility. They have two slots for any 1 or 2 slot Standard Cuffs. Premium cuffs cannot be used with them at all. The default nappy outfit takes Standard cuffs, so a Standard outfit is optional.
    • Premium Outfits (not sold on Hunsterverse) are overall the better choice, the guide says. They have two slots for Premium or Standard cuffs, or a mix. Premium Cuffs only fit Premium Outfits. The best cuffs before G Rank are Premium Cuffs, especially for Blademasters. You can get cuffs with Artisan that enable Razor Sharp +1 where you would otherwise need several gems.

    Manager requests

    Most of what the Manager asks is simple questions of no real importance. After you get a Poogie, she can also ask for items to heal it:

    • The Eldest Sister requests: 3 Sleep Herbs and 5 Ice Crystals.
    • The Middle Sister requests: 4 Sleepyfish and 3 Chilled Meat+ (the guide's Cold Meat G).
    • The Youngest Sister requests: 2 Nutrients and 1 Cool Drink.

    The guide says Chilled Meat+ is made by combining normal Chilled Meat with a Universal BBQ Set, then doing perfect cooking with the spit that results. A Universal BBQ Set is made by combining a Female BBQ Spit with a Universal BBQ Kit. Chilled Meat is made by combining Raw Meat with Ice Crystals. (The guide's "Universal Grill Tools" and "Universal Grill" are our Universal BBQ Kit and Universal BBQ Set.) (Grill recipes.)

    Taking a Poogie on quests

    To take a Poogie on quests, spend points on three things:

    1. A Departure Facility within Poogie Facilities (30P).
    2. 1 Poogie within Poogie Extension, under Increase Poogie Count (30P).
    3. Either an outfit from the Poogie Dressup! gift (outfits 1 to 30, free at Claim Items on the Gacha Felyne or the plaza Guide) or a 20P outfit within Poogie Extension. Premium Outfits from the Item Mall are not sold here.

    You can equip a Skill Cuff by talking to the Manager, at the Blacksmith, or from an equipment box. G Rank cuffs need the G Rank License, bought with 60P from GR50 (client code; our client calls it "G Rank Poogie").

    After you have the Poogie and the facility (a trampoline on the lowest level of My Tore), use the board on the lowest level of the farm. It sets which facility each of your Poogies uses. Use it to choose the Departure Facility for a Poogie. The Poogie leaves with an animation, and can then come on quests with you. Only one Poogie can use each facility.

    With your Poogie along on a quest, you can get extra carves at the Poogie Dirt Pile, based on what you carved yourself during that quest.

    Skill cuffs

    Skill Cuffs are the Frontier equivalent of charms or talismans. Unlike mainline charms, cuffs always have fixed skill point values. There is no luck involved apart from getting the items to make them. A cuff with +4 Earplugs, for example, will always have exactly +4 Earplugs when crafted. You craft cuffs at the Blacksmith. There are two types:

    • Premium cuffs need items that were paid for on the official servers, and need a Premium Outfit (also paid for) on your Poogie. Neither is sold on Hunsterverse (see the note below).
    • Standard cuffs can go on both Premium Outfits and Standard Outfits. The guide says you get one Standard Outfit free, and more cost 20P at the Manager (the 20P is confirmed in our client code).

    Below G Rank, Standard cuffs are not very good next to Premium ones. At G Rank both are very good, and equally able to make an endgame set.     Partnya skills work the same as hunters' skills. You put decorations with skill points into armour or weapons, and when you have enough points the matching skill activates. The system looks simple, but to make anything decent you need a Guild Hall, My Tore facilities and many combined decorations.

    For example, a decoration with only Demon Flute is easy. A decoration with Demon Flute, Quake Res, Encourage and Recovery Time is very convoluted. You make several hybrid decorations, combine them into the final one, and get materials from several facilities.

    Partner weapon stats

    Partner weapons work differently from those on humans or Rastas. A number of things define each weapon.

    • Raw values depend only on the tier of the weapon. For example, a standard G Rank Lance has 736 Attack, at Lv1 or Lv50 alike.
    • Elemental values depend on the Weapon Rank your Partner has for that weapon type. For example, at rank 999 with Tonfa every element has a value of 1500, whether it is a hybrid element like Black Flame or plain Fire or Dragon. This ignores the actual values on the weapon, other than the element type.
    • Status values are the only value taken from the weapon. It is 100% of the value on the weapon, or 50% for Tonfas.     Partner armour stats

    Partner armour is simpler. It ignores every value of the armour except its class. Any full set of normal G Rank armour at Lv1 gives the maximum defense that standard G Rank armour can. The guide says it is not worth making Burst or Origin pieces for a Partner, but G Exotic can be worth it.

    Partner skills

    Partners can use many of the same skills a player can, and the effects of the skills are identical to those on players. But the mechanics and limits are quite different. Partner skills are set from a list and bought with GCP. There is a hierarchy to unlocking them, and some of the best (Encourage +2, Strong Attack) need you to rank the Partner up to PR999.

    The guide says the Skills menu (our client: Skill Settings, with a Skill List and a "Choose Skill to learn" screen showing Point Cost and Cost) has four options: Learn Skill, Passive Skills, Equip Skills and Manual.

    Learn Skills

    The Learn Skills option lists the skills you can buy now. Black skills are the ones you can buy, blue skills are ones you already bought, and grey skills are ones you cannot afford yet. All skills are unlocked with GCP. The Caravan Festival is one of the best sources of GCP for ranking up your Partner's abilities. If you finish enough routes it can give many thousands of GCP. See Caravan.

    Passive Skills

    Passive skills need your Partner to have maxed a weapon's rank (999). Then start Kaidan by choosing the last option on the Legendary Rasta, in the same way you first unlocked a weapon or chose a Partner. Passive skills are always on. Our client calls this Weapon Mastery: once the weapon's Proficiency is 999, talking to a Legend Rasta turns it into Weapon Mastery, and the auto-activating skills need that Mastery plus their prerequisite skill, bought with GCP. The basic options are Additional Skill Slots and Extra Cost Budget.

    • Slot upgrades (Equippable Skills +1) add one skill each, up to +2, for a maximum of 12 skills. The guide says you probably never need these.
    • Cost upgrades (Skill Cost +1) add +5 to the maximum Cost each, up to a maximum Cost of 260. You should get all of these, as they are very good.

    Once your Partner reaches Rank 999 in an individual weapon class, you also unlock the option to raise that weapon's maximum attack value. This does not raise the raw values. It gives a higher attack ceiling, which helps with skills such as Adrenaline. It is well worth taking the ceiling for your Partner's main weapon class, as you are likely to hit the ceiling with Strong Attack +5 and similar DPS skills.

    Pushing each weapon to MASTER level adds true raw to all weapon classes. Maxing out Kaidan Rank to 999 adds more true raw to the weapon class you maxed.

    Equip Skills

    Equip Skills shows a list of skills and a list of those you have equipped. In the equipped-skills window, the numbers in the right column are the cost of each skill, and the total cost you can have equipped is shown above them. It is fairly straightforward.

    Skills that share the same effect do not actually stack. Equipping Strong Attack with normal Attack does not give two separate values. It just switches off the weaker skill, while still using up Cost.

    Partner items

    You can only have one type of item in use on Partners. Putting points into a second item type replaces the one you already have.

    The Items option sets your Partner to use items that help it advance and help in quests. The first menu is for HRP/GRP Tickets of various kinds. The second sets your Partner to use Wide Area Powders, Flutes, Throwing Knives or Traps. The guide strongly recommends matching this to what the owner of your Rasta has set. For example, if your contracted Rasta plays Flutes, set Powders on your own Partner for the best result.

    Partners are very useful when ranked up and given the right skills. They can give party-wide buffs and can easily deal as much damage as another human player. The guide recommends investing in one.
