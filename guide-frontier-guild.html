    Guild and Guild Hall

    How to join a guild, and what its Guild Hall offers: a shop, Clan Poogies, a Legendary Rasta, Special Cooking, the Felyne Treasure Hunters and the Adventure Boat.

    Guild chat first

    To talk to your guild you must set your chat to the right mode: green, with a pink flag next to the input box. If you see anything else, you are in the wrong mode. Local chat is only seen by people in the exact same area as you, so a message to someone in the Blacksmith in local chat will not reach them.

    The guide's author preferred a separate chat server over guild chat for general questions and conversation.

    Different. The "English Guild" of the original guide was its author's own guild on the official servers. Here, browse our guilds on the guilds page (there is no official English guild); our Discord is a place to find players and parties. The chat colours and keys above were not checked in game.

    Joining a guild

    On the official servers new players started in a rookie guild and had to leave it (in the Guild menu) before they could join another. Our server never puts anyone in a rookie guild, so you can apply to a guild straight away.

    The game lets you apply when you are not in a guild and the guild you pick is recruiting and not full.

    1. Talk to the guild counter NPC in town (behind the blue-covered counter in the picture).
    2. Open the guild search and search by guild name: the row just above the search by leader ID. The original guide's route was the first option, the first option again, then the third option.
    3. A box opens. Type the guild's name to find it.
    4. Confirm the correct guild. On the next page choose the middle option to apply. The guild's leader (or a member the leader lets handle applications) must accept you before you are a member.

    Guild chat should appear automatically. To talk in it, press Shift+Up/Down until the chat icon becomes a flag and the text turns green. If you were logged in when the guild accepted you, you may need to log out and in again before you see guild chat.

    Works the same. Guilds work (over 500 exist; see Guilds). Member limits depend on guild rank: 30 members up to rank 2, 40 up to rank 6, 50 up to rank 9 and 60 from rank 10. Rookie guilds are not built here: joining one always fails. Leaders and sub-leaders can also accept applications on the Account page.

    Guild requirements and meeting place

    These were the house rules of the guide author's guild, which are typical of an active guild:

    • Log in now and then. A G Rank hunter who has not been around for about 30 days may be kicked to make space.
    • Anyone still in HR may be kicked after about 10 days away, as a long absence suggests the player left the game.
    • Festivals (see Hunter Festival) used to need at least 5,000 souls from each participant. The guild removed this rule, but it is still polite to collect as many souls as you can, and here the guild prizes go up to 5,000 souls (the personal prizes up to 1,000).

    Different. These were the house rules of one guild, not game rules: each guild here sets its own, within the size limit for its rank (30 to 60 members). The Hunter Festival does run; its prize table is on Events.

    The Guild Hall

    To enter the Guild Hall, walk up to its entrance and press confirm. You go into the Hall. If someone is already inside, the game asks you to change to the server the Hall is currently active on.

    Quest givers

    The quest counter by the quest board opens the same menu as the General Quest Counter in town.

    The guild's own quests are under General Quest Counter > Clan Quests: Quest Orders and Training Support Quests, which need a Guild Ticket, and Add Request. The original guide says you can add extra sub-goals to these quests, such as hunting with no armour or no weapon, and that those sub-goals pay a ticket you get nowhere else (not checked here).

    Guild Store

    The store Felyne in the Hall (our client names it "Gacha Felyne", although it is the guild's shop, not the gacha) sells some food you cannot buy in the town's food shop. Its food list, the Hunter Bento menu, opens at guild rank 4. The most useful at first are Demon Mushroom (980z) and Fatty Tomato (450z). As a bento with Fist Rice (550z, from the town's food shop) each gives +50 HP and +50 Stamina in one season: Fist Rice with Demon Mushroom in the Warm Season, Fist Rice with Fatty Tomato in the Cold Season. Every pair and season is on Food and Bentos; how food works is in Quests, Food Buffs and Bentos.

    Works the same. The Hall, its quest counter and the Clan Quests are game content: 75 Quest Orders and 10 Training Support Quests are offered here. We did not test each guild quest.

    Clan Poogies

    You can only have one Clan Poogie buff active at a time. Feed again only when you want to change your active Clan Poogie skill, or when the buff has run out.

    The Guild Hall has three Clan Poogies, and each gives a buff when you feed it. The guide says each outfit grants a different skill; its picture lists four buffs (above) and says only the Guild Leader can swap the outfits.

    • Feed a Clan Poogie Poogie Crackers (10z each, from the store Felyne). A successful feed shows as the Poogie jumping up and down in a white flash.
    • If you feed more than one Poogie, only the last one fed stays active.
    • The guide says all Poogie buffs stack with similar skills: you can use the Luck Poogie even with Great Luck or a Premium Course active and still gain from it.

    How the Luck Poogie works, as the guide gives it: it has a 12.5% chance to reroll a failed reward roll and a 12.5% chance to add extra reward slots. With Great Luck and the Poogie active you can reach a maximum of 24 standard reward slots. It only affects the top 24 Main Reward slots. It does not change other rewards (Sub Objectives, Additional, Breaks, Partner and so on).

    Different. Clan Poogies are game content. With our client patch (v1.21 and later) the first feed always gives the buff (on the old servers it often took many crackers), and the buff lasts through a crash, disconnect or relog for 12 hours after you take it (10 Nov), so you do not need to feed again at every login. Every account has the Premium course, so the Luck Poogie stacking should apply (not tested in game). Which Poogies and outfits your guild has was not checked in game.

    Guild Legendary Rasta

    The Guild Hall has a single Legendary Rasta. Which one it is changes every day.

    Talk to the Legendary and choose Contract. This lets you take them on any one quest you can sign in the Guild Hall, once a day. You can set all the usual options (weapons, items they use). Unlike a Premium Legendary, they do not have an infinite health pool. They do completely ignore all quest requirements, which makes them useful for quests that need specific gear that makes the quest more tedious than it should be.

    Unknown, not checked. We did not check the daily Guild Legendary Rasta in game. Our server answers the game's daily Legendary Rasta request with placeholder entries (choice 0 for every day). In that case the client picks the day's Legendary Rasta itself from its own list by day number, so the daily change should work. Whether Contract works was not checked. Rasta borrowing and contract problems were fixed on 2 Nov, and no account has the Assist course (the Legend Rasta course).

    Guild Special Cooking

    Special Food gives you a skill when it is cooked successfully. Each skill has three levels, and there is a failure result that applies a debuff instead of a buff. The finished food goes to the Felyne Chef next to the facility, who lists the guild's meals and lets you eat one.

    How cooking works

    1. Choose four ingredients and start. The facility has four cooking spots, so up to four hunters can cook at once.
    2. In the minigame, press confirm to keep the icon on the meter inside the blue or green bars. Blue gives the best result, green a good one.
    3. Cat stamps in the top right show how you are doing until the cooking time runs out.
    4. When finished, the food goes to the Felyne Chef. You are told what buff it gives, or asked to replace an existing meal if all six meal slots are in use.

    Cooked food is available to all guild members at the Felyne Chef. On the official servers it stayed there for one hour; here it stays for 7 days. The buff itself lasts one hour and thirty minutes from when you eat.

    If a food has random results, keep eating it at the Felyne Chef until the skill you want triggers: our client says such a meal activates a different skill each time you eat. The guide adds that the skills in the pool depend on the success level, and that +20 resistance is in the normal success pool.

    Results

    There are four results: Great Failure, Failure, Success and Great Success. The stamps you collect in the minigame decide it: no stamps is a fixed Great Failure, red stamps give Failure, green Success and blue Great Success.

    Cooking modes

    You can cook without help, or first borrow one of six kinds of Chef's Wisdom from the Felyne Chef, which change the minigame or the result. The guide says normal cooking is free and the Chef's Wisdom costs Guild Tickets.

    ModeCostEffect
    Secret of Success10 TicketsWidens the normal green success area.
    No Failure Allowed10 TicketsRemoves the red large failure area.

    Recipes

    As with normal food, there is a recipe for each effect you might want. The recipes below are the cheapest combinations in materials; the original guide kept more in its document, which we do not link. Examples of strong results are Unaffected+3 (the Three Worlds skill), Adrenaline+2, Great Luck and Wide-Area+3.

    For Zenith monsters, use the resistance recipes too. The guide says you need 55 of a resistance to avoid the extreme version of a blight, and that you can reach +83 resistance from SR Skills combined with Guild Cooking (see Zenith).

    In our client you first pick one of 23 dishes (Food Choice), then its ingredients: Clan Yaminabe, Spicy Bakudandon, Pioneer Exciting Meal, Lucky Pancakes, Ultimate Sashimi, Brothers Grill, Fiery Tongs Feast Pot, Medicinal Porridge, Heaven & Earth Rice, Goddess Gem Sweets, Shellfish Iso-Yaki, Holy Seafood Banquet, Motivated Noodles, Hunter's Freaky Pot, Skewer Dumplings, Crimson BBQ Meat, Sweet Wrappings, Dawn Sky Toast, Five Colored Soup, Bloody Sala-Salad, Mori Mori Meat Pie, Fried Buns and Latchberry Cutlet. For every dish but Clan Yaminabe the screen colours the ingredients: yellow is the basic ingredient and pink the three extras. The guide's rows below list ingredients only, so which dish each row is was not matched.

    The guide's advice: unless you are very sure you can hit Great Success every time, use only recipes made of red ingredients, and cook with the option that forces a Success to rise to a Great Success (the 20-Ticket mode above).

    Felyne Treasure Hunters

    Guild members' Partnyas can be sent out treasure hunting, and every guild member can send them. A good number of items and materials come from this system, including the C.Base G cuff materials for G Rank Poogie cuffs, which can be vital for building many G Rank sets. Every reward is listed on Sources (hunt levels 0, 1 and 2).

    Among the ordinary items you can get are Quick items, such as the Quick Potion. The guide says they work like the normal consumable but at 200% speed, as if Speed Eating were active. They are separate item stacks and you can take them alongside normal ones (for example 10 Potions, 10 Quick Potions, 2 Max Potions and 2 Quick Max Potions).

    Starting a hunt

    Choose the first option at the treasure hunt counter (the Treasure Felyne) in the Guild Hall to send a hunt to one of the locations (six in the picture) with up to 5 of the guild's Partnyas. After picking the area, assign cats to the slots. Match the colours to the two shown on each slot, put the highest-level cats in the right slots and confirm.

    The guide says each hunt takes 30 minutes. In our client's data it is 30 minutes for four of its eight destinations and 45 minutes for the other four (the picture shows six areas; which is which was not matched). On the official servers a cat sent once was tired until the next daily reset, and sending it again the same day cost 1 RP per tired cat. Here every send is free: see the note below.

    Treasure types

    The guide lists three kinds of loot: Rewards, Treasures and Souvenirs. The type is mostly luck. The reward pools depend on the location you sent the hunters to.

    • Rewards are given straight away when you talk to the treasure hunt counter after a hunt ends.
    • Treasures are also triggered by talking to the counter, but a player must actively find them after the hunters have located them (see below).
    • Souvenirs arrived on the official servers when someone else returned from finding a treasure: a guild-wide notice told everyone, and they claimed the rewards at the reward basket in the Guild Hall. Souvenirs are off here.

    Getting treasures

    When a Treasure is found it is added to the guild's list of found treasures under the counter's second option. Every guild member can join any treasure on the list.

    Joining gives you a stamp on the treasure's card. When you back out, a cat confirms the location you must visit.

    You get a hint and must find the treasure within the given area. The spot is not marked, so check every area of the map to be sure you find it.

    You can gather the treasure on any quest that takes place in the area where the hunters found it. The simplest is a map delivery. The treasure appears as a giant shiny in one of several preset spots. Pick it up and clear the quest; the treasure then opens for every member who joined, to claim in the Guild Hall.

    Claiming treasures

    Treasure rewards appear in the reward basket in the Guild Hall. It works almost like a quest rewards screen, except that you first choose a party that has rewards for you, and then use the send all to box option. Each member claims a cleared treasure once.

    Different. Guild treasure hunts work, but sending cats is always free here, also the same day, so the RP cost for tired cats does not apply. Hunts are kept for 7 days. Souvenirs are off here: the reward basket never lists any. The hunt length and the six areas were not checked in game.

    Felyne Adventure Boat

    The Adventure Boat sends a Felyne to a chosen destination. On the official servers it was the only way to get some of the items for Guild Special Cooking recipes, such as the ones used in the Caring recipes (the guide says Caring stops you hitting other players with attacks).

    Sending the boat costs RP (Clan Points), which you earn by being logged in. The rewards depend on several factors, and you can improve them by putting Guild Tickets in. To do this, talk to the Adventure Felyne after the boat departs.

    When the boat returns, claim the rewards directly in front of it. The success level of the trip decides what you see, from a pile of plain boxes and debris to shining treasures.

    The boat is out for 6 hours. All members of the guild can contribute and claim rewards (each member once), so if you plan to use guild cooking, it is worth pooling resources here for the best results.

    Different. The boat works as in the game: it is out for 6 hours, the game itself rolls the rewards and takes the RP cost, and each member collects once. RP here is 1 for every 15 minutes logged in (it was 1 per 30 minutes without the Net Cafe course), added when you log out, up to 65,535 (see Clan Points). In our client's data a trip costs 2 RP on the six standard routes and 3 RP on the six routes that need a key item (the guide's Diva Defense routes), and the ingredient lists agree with the table above. The odds were not checked in game.
