Getting started and progression

From the tutorial bracket to Zenith: ranks, styles and what unlocks when.

Getting Started and Progressing

Progression runs in four stages: a short tutorial bracket, a long Hunter Rank climb, a parallel Style Rank track, then G Rank and Zenith.

What Frontier Is

Frontier Z is Capcom's PC Monster Hunter MMO, built on the second-generation games but developed for over a decade. It has its own roster of Frontier-exclusive monsters and elders, its own weapon types such as Tonfa and Switch Axe F, and a progression ladder far longer than any handheld entry.

It also keeps old-school rules: no combining items once a quest is posted, and no restocking mid-quest.

The difficulty curve is not smooth. HR1 through HR4 is a tutorial bracket — the game hands you an HR5 weapon, every monster in those ranks is available again at HR5, and gear effectively resets there. HR5 is where the real game begins. Live servers were subscription-based: the trial ran to HR4 and HR5 onward required a paid course, which also gated store stock and daily quests.

On Hunsterverse: there is no cash shop and no paid service, so those subscription gates do not apply. Capcom's calendar events — festivals, Diva Defense, Caravan, seasonal events — are not scheduled here; content exists only when it is part of the server's own quest set.

How a Hunt Works

You post quests at a counter: the guild girl in black at the middle counters early on, the G Rank guildie with the umbrella later. Quests sort by star rating, key quests always first with a star icon, and an urgent appears once the keys are done, flagged by a red exclamation mark over the NPC.

Before posting you confirm party size and password; the final screen also toggles Hardcore mode. A party holds up to four hunters; empty slots can be filled by AI companions — Halk, Partnyaa, Partner or another player's Rasta.

The quest gives you a supply box at camp and nothing else. From HR3 onward — High Rank by the old rating — you stop receiving supplies and can spawn at a random point. You cannot return to town for items, change gear mid-quest, or combine after posting, so leaving is a commitment. The end screen lists rewards, HRP gained and monster stats.

Carting is not free: on Supremacy-class quests, carts and Halk Pot use shrink the reward pool. Halk Pots cut damage taken by a flat 70% but are limited to five a day, so treat them as a crutch.

On Hunsterverse: quests come from the server's own quest set with English text; titles, objectives and descriptions are translated.

The Basic Loop

  • Open the Hunter Guide. Shift+C or R1+R2. Enter each section, press Z or Square for details, then confirm; most early tasks are "read this" and pay potions, meat, whetstones and cheques.
  • Run keys, then the urgent. Three keys at HR1, the urgent, then back to town to rank up.
  • Cash the cheques. At HR2 you get hundreds of yellow cheque items. Sell about 150 for 7.5 million zeny, then buy the Powercharm (36,000z), Armor Charm (24,000z), both Book of Storage volumes (1,500z and 5,000z), and box upgrades.
  • Eat before you leave. Fist Rice with Demon Mushrooms gives +50 HP and +50 stamina in warm season; with Frost Tomatoes, the same in cold; with Power Lard, +50 HP and +25 stamina in breeding season.
  • Claim the daily. The pink store guildie's last option gives a Daily quest, five Halk Pots and five N Points, resetting at 12:00 Taiwan time.
  • Use your stamps. A completed stamp card pays 1x 4,000 HRP ticket, the next 3x, repeating forever; each adds 4,000 HRP to the quest.

On Hunsterverse: the site tracks playtime, hunts, weapon usage, quest outcomes and Road placings, and the Discord bot links accounts and shows who is online.

Hunter Rank

HR1 to HR7 is gated by keys and urgents, not point totals: clear the keys, an urgent unlocks, clear it and you rank up on returning to town.

  • HR1 — Taikun Zamuza, Yian Kut-Ku, Hypnoc; urgent Abiorugu.
  • HR2 — Rathian, Dyuragaua, Volganos, Gurenzeburu; urgent Espinas.
  • HR3 — Pariapuria, Rathalos, Nono Orugaron, Rajang; urgent Rukodiora.
  • HR4 — White Hypnoc, Orange Espinas, Kuarusepusu, Doragyurosu; urgent Akura Vashimu.
  • HR5 — Baruragaru, Diorex, Toridcless, Gasurabazura, Toa Tesukatora; urgent Supremacy Odibatorasu.
  • HR6 — Supremacy Aruganosu and Goruganosu, then Supremacy Unknown, as repels. No urgent: clearing the 6★ keys promotes you to HR7 automatically.
  • HR7 — Shantien repel, which opens G Rank.

HR5 is the hinge: it unlocks Style Rank, three of the four weapon styles per weapon, Hardcore monsters and the SP, Gou and Exotic gear trees. HR6 adds 6★ repels and slays, with Supremacy variants of Pariapuria, Unknown, Doragyurosu, Teostra and Odibatorasu; Lavasioth's Supremacy form is Aruganosu and Goruganosu, a pair that must both be brought to 1 HP before either dies. HR7 adds the last SR skills, and Affinity Up is the pick for almost every weapon.

The cap is HR999; HRP comes from quests, with 4,000 HRP tickets as the shortcut.

Style Rank

Style Rank starts automatically the first time you approach the Guild Master at HR5 and ranks on SRP. It gives three things: SR Skills, two extra movesets per weapon type, and Hardcore monsters.

Styles swap movesets. At HR5 you have Earth, Heaven and Storm, freely changeable at any equipment box under Change Equipment → Secret Book → Weapon → Style; Extreme unlocks at G Rank and is best in slot there. Storm is the default at HR5 for most weapons, but Storm Bow is a downgrade, Storm Long Sword only usable, and Sword and Shield and Gunlance good in all three.

SR Skills are permanent buffs that cost no slots, equipped through the Book of Secrets, which must itself be equipped. You get one, and a second at GSR100: Defense +170, a single resistance +34, all resistances +19, Health +50, Affinity +20-24%. From HR7, Affinity Up is the standard pick.

Attack Up adds +17 true raw at HR5, +45 at HR6, +50 at HR7, and caps at +100 at GSR999. Attack Ceiling is the stat people neglect: attack is capped at 800 true raw by default, and each of the 180 My Mission levels raises the cap by 40, to 8,000. Aim for about 50 levels early and 110-130 once your set is built.

Hardcore is a toggle on the final quest screen, marked by a flame icon. It makes every large monster in the quest its HC variant, with new moves and higher roar, wind and quake levels. HC pays 3x HRP, 4x SRP, 1.5x zeny and 1.5x GCP while your weapon is within the rarity limit (Rare-11 at HR5, Rare-12 from HR6), and drops Weapon Souls by band: 序 for HR1-2, 中 for HR3-4, 極 for HR5 non-Gou, 天 for HR5 Gou. HC carves are a 5% carve outside the normal pool; GHC carves are 2%.

Hiden is the long payoff: five decorations for a weapon type grant its Weapon Tech skill, always including Super High-Grade Earplugs and at least 1.2x melee / 1.3x ranged attack. Expect 775 hunts and 30+ hours for the first, and it cannot be finished before GR500.

G Rank

G Rank starts at HR7. The G Rank guildie posts a Shantien repel on an airship — ballista binder ammo from the deck box, the gong, and it goes down. Then talk to the Guild Master: she sets every weapon to GSR and unlocks Extreme Style for all of them. Do that immediately, on every weapon type.

G Rank is free-form: everything is open except G Rank Exotics and Zeniths. Quests are sorted ★1 to ★7, and the star rating subtracts defense — full at ★1, -150 per star to -900 at ★7, and -1,050 at ★8 and on every Zenith. Lowering a quest's star rating when confirming cuts rewards and carves.

  • GR100 — required by the G Rank Hunter Guide; a milestone, not a gate.
  • GR200 — Zenith quests open at ★1.
  • GR500 — G Rank Exotics at fixed ★8, Merits, and the point Hiden sets can be completed.
  • GR800 — G Finesse upgrades, and ★4 Zeniths.
  • GR899 / GR999 — the original cap was GR899; a later update raised it to GR999 at 13,716,950 GRP total, 2,331,451 more from GR899. GRP quests pay 3,000-5,200; the Gogomoa GRP ship pays 8,000.

G Rank weapons have 50 levels, armour runs G Lv1 to GX Lv7, and sets run 12 relevant skills early and up to 16 late, so full monster sets are mostly unusable. Sigils replace decorations, G Break Jewels come from parts of same-star monsters, and GSR is per weapon: GSR1 gives +50 true raw, GSR999 +100.

Zenith Content

Zenith is the Frontier Z endgame tier: variants of existing monsters with one overdeveloped part, new patterns and much larger health pools. One rating per 200 GR — ★1 at GR200, ★2 at GR400, ★3 at GR600, ★4 at GR800 — and every Zenith quest applies -1,050 defense.

G Rank Espinas at GR1 has about 29,090 effective health and G Rank Barioth at GR100 has 93,333; ★1 Zenith Espinas has 99,800, then 119,800 at ★2, 149,800 at ★3 and 249,600 at ★4.

You do not need the top tier for materials. Almost everything is shared across a Zenith's tiers and only the special break item is band-specific — breaking the developed part gives a 100% drop for that band. Farm ★1 to ★3 for the bulk, then kill roughly five GR800 ★4s with the part broken to finish a set. Breaking it also usually reduces threat; some Zeniths lose their Zenith-level roar, tremor or wind pressure.

Zenith gear is a flat upgrade over standard G Rank gear, and each piece carries a Zenith Skill that mostly boosts an existing skill a tier or adds a slot. Slots are the real prize: five armour pieces plus a Zenith weapon and Cuff give up to seven extra, and a ZP set can reach 19 skills. Zenith weapons add 1.5x part damage and up to 580 base true raw.

Do not rush ZX upgrades. Armour runs Z, ZF, ZY and ZX at GR200, 400, 600 and 800. Z to ZX Lv1 costs 196 materials; ZX Lv2-7 costs another 142 for 30-50 defense. Leave it at Lv1.

Zeniths also bring statuses standard gear cannot fully answer: Extreme blights need about 55 resistance — SR All Res Up, one Res Up and Guild Food can reach 83 — and Super Poison, Sound Asleep, Crystal Paralysis, Lingering Paralysis, Bleeding and Lightning Rod Needle each need specific handling. Extreme Poison cannot be countered at all.
