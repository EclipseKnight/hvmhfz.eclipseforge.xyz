    Basics and Getting Started

    A friendly start to Monster Hunter Frontier Z: what the game is, how to get going, your first steps from Hunter Rank 1 to 7, daily routines, controls and chat, and where to read next.

    Welcome to Frontier

    Monster Hunter Frontier Z (MHF-Z) is an online Monster Hunter game with a long history and a lot of content. Its main parts are:

    • Hunter Rank (HR) 1 to 7. HR1 to HR4 are very easy compared to the rest of the game and mostly work as a tutorial. The game ramps up at HR5. HR6 holds the old "endgame", and HR7 leads to G Rank.
    • G Rank (GR). A free-form rank where you hunt for better gear. Zenith quests open at GR 300 here (GR200 on the original servers).
    • Style Rank, Hardcore monsters, Transcend, Hiden, the Caravan, Raviente and the Hunter's Road are some of the systems that give Frontier its own flavour. Each has its own page in this guide.

    This page walks you through the early game in order. Frontier has some old-school limits: you can only equip things in your house or at the Blacksmith, you cannot combine items after you sign for a quest, and you cannot restock or change equipment during a quest. In general you commit more to each quest than in newer Monster Hunter games, so think before you leave.

    If you only know Monster Hunter: World, remember that this is a much older style of game. Take your time and read the other pages when you need them.

    Different. These systems are part of the game client, and Raviente and the Hunting Road run here; the Caravan's server side (its rewards, shop boxes and Pioneering) is not built. The Road is different from the official servers: players vote on its design (see the Hunting Road guide). Zenith quests start at GR 300 here, not GR 200 (★1 to ★4 at GR 300, 500, 700 and 900).

    Getting started: install, account and character

    The main menu

    When the game starts, you see a menu with four options:

    • Quick Start picks a Land for you from your HR (below HR5 a Novice Land first, from HR5 a Normal Land). You cannot choose the Land, and if none fits you go to the World Select.
    • Start Game (Choose Server in the picture) opens the World Select, where you pick the world and Land yourself.
    • Options
    • Quit

    Character creation happens after you choose either Quick Start or Start Game. On your first launch you have no character yet: log in with your Hunsterverse account in the launcher and the game takes you to character creation.

    Character creation

    The options are in English. Each section has a master selection option, with filters for individual sub-types (for example dark skin, long hair, light skin). Select randomises your character. The right stick, or K and ; on a keyboard, turns your character around.

    After you design your character, you choose which weapon type you start with. This is only what you carry at first and you can change it later. As in mainline Monster Hunter, every character starts with the basic version of each weapon in the box (every type except Tonfa, Switch Axe and Magnet Spike).

    You start with the basic weapons (Bone Blade, Bone Katana "Wolf", Hunter Knife and the like), not an HR5 weapon. Hunsterverse The original guide says new hunters start with an HR5 (Gou) weapon; on Hunsterverse they do not, and Gou weapons are made at the Blacksmith from HR 100 (HR5). The guide's author found HR1 to HR4 very easy compared to the rest of the game, with content ramping up harshly from HR5 onwards, and calls HR1 to HR4 about the equivalent of Low Rank at best. The Urgent quests are scaled down so you can solo them. You can switch off your AI companions (Options > NPC Settings, see Menus) if you want to stretch out the earliest stages, but that is not generally recommended: almost every monster from the first four rank bands comes back at HR5, gear essentially starts over there (like High Rank in other games), and at HR5 Style Rank adds Heaven Style and Storm Style to the Earth Style you start with, so you have three of the four styles for every weapon (Extreme Style comes at G Rank, and Magnet Spike only has Extreme Style).

    Your first servers

    After you confirm the news posts, you are put on a server or you choose one, depending on how you started. On the official servers only the first and fifth tabs were available at the start: the first tab held the standard low-rank servers and the fifth a special set of newbie servers (see the newbie server below). Our own worlds, including a Newbie world, are listed on the worlds page.

    Different. The guide's own setup chapters (Windows locale and time zone, Locale Emulator, PlayStation versions, Taiwanese and Japanese account sign-ups, installers, VPNs) were written for Capcom's official servers and do not apply. Start at the Play page (Windows, Linux / Steam Deck, or bringing a character from another server), then create your account in the Mezeporta launcher: press REGISTER and choose a username and password (case-sensitive: always type it the same way). No time zone or locale change is needed; our Windows guide says Locale Emulator is only needed if the game refuses to start.

    First login and settings

    When you are in a server, you see your character walk into town and an instructor NPC greets you. Mash through what he says: it is not important. Then you get a message about the Hunter Navi, which works as both a set of achievements and a basic progression guide.

    1. Press confirm to open the Hunter Navi. Its topics are Novices, HR, HR4, Old SR, GR, GR 2, GR 3, Sigils, Conquest, Diva, Longplay, Ravi and Various; the G Rank topics cannot be chosen before G Rank.
    2. Open the HR section with confirm (E, left click, or the right face button on a controller). It has two tabs of tasks.
    3. In the first tab, press Z (or the left face button) for Task Info, the details of the tasks. This automatically completes five of them and lets you claim a basic set of starter items (potions, meat, whetstones) for your first quests.
    4. Do the same in the second tab and in the Other section for items that are useful later.
    5. Dismiss the pop-ups with back or confirm. You end up where the long string of prompts began.

    Options worth changing now

    • Control type: the game offers Portable Type (attacks on the buttons, like the console Monster Hunter games) and Classic Type (attacks on the right analogue stick). Change it under Options > Input Options > Key Setting.
    • Display (Gen, Quests): turn off the Travel Arrow Display (the mouse-control direction indicator) and the Control Guide overlay if you do not want them. HUD Bar Length sets how long the health bar is drawn, as you prefer.
    • NPC chat: switch off Rasta Chat, Partner Chat, Partnya Chat, Fosta Chat and Mercenary Chat (and the Raviente guides) if you do not want companions talking in chat.

    The tutorial quest

    When you talk to the instructor you may end up on a Gogomoa quest. Skip through the text prompts as they appear (press whichever button is cancel) and choose No when asked a question. Then:

    1. Use the Blue Box and take the coin from it.
    2. Deliver the coin to the Red Box. This lets you leave to the north.
    3. Kill the Gogomoa, which ends the quest.

    You then see a normal quest end screen where you can take rewards, followed by a list of the HRP you gained, monster stats and so on.

    Works the same. The Hunter Navi tasks are client content, and Hunter Navi is switched on here (older Hunter Navi saves were fixed on 2 Nov). Settings are client options.

    The town and Hunter Rank 1

    The town layout is simple. The map above shows the key locations, including the places named later on this page.

    HR1 quests

    Weapons: you start with the basic weapons in your box. Hunsterverse The original guide describes stronger starter weapons (very early HR5 weapons); new hunters here do not get them. If you want to craft a weapon from scratch and learn to gather, see Items and Materials. Finding materials is then entirely on you, with no hand-holding.

    1. Go to the General Quest Counter: the receptionist in black at the middle counters of the town square. She may prompt you to return to the Hunter Navi; you can ignore this and keep talking to her until you get the quest menu.
    2. Choose Hunter Quests > ★1. Key quests are sorted to the top and have an obvious star icon.
    3. Finish the six key quests (listed with links on Progression). The Urgent Quest then appears under Hunter Quests > Urgent Quests: [Urgent] Unknown Species, Fierce Wyvern (Abiorugu). Complete it to progress to HR2.

    The instructor NPC shouts a lot during these quests and makes whistling sounds even if you switched messages off. He stops appearing in your quests after you reach HR2.

    Hunter Navi tasks

    Hunter Navi tasks work like most modern achievement systems: you complete a task and get items. Most tasks are simple and most are worth doing. Open the Hunter Navi tasks window in town with Shift+C on keyboard or R1+R2 on a controller (the guide's keys), or from the Start menu. The challenges are split into sections ordered by progression.

    Open every section you can, press Square (or Z on keyboard) for Task Info, then press your normal confirm button (Claim Rewards). Confirm on a task can also Mark this task: the quests it needs then show at the quest counter. This claims a batch of tasks that are just "Read This", which gives you free items, including the cheques you sell below. The guide recommends working through all the tasks, because the rewards are very useful and most do not take you far out of your way. The Google Document has the details on each task.

    Works the same. The town, the HR1 quests and the free rewards from the guide tasks are game content and work the same.

    Hunter Rank 2

    Zeny and box space

    When you finish the HR1 Urgent, you move up to HR2 automatically when you return to town. The instructor greets you, upgrades your house so you can get a partner, and gives you a stack of GCP tickets.

    Caravan Gem

    The Caravan Area (also called Pallone) is next to the town entrance. Go there and get your Caravan Gem as soon as possible after reaching HR2. Walk in and talk to the Caravan's leader (the Boss), the woman near the bonfire. A new icon of a random colour then appears next to your name: that is your Caravan Gem.

    The gem adds a new option to your equipment box (in the Equip menu, Caravan Skills). At first it holds only Recovery Items Up. Equip it when you can: it raises the healing of Potions and Mega Potions to 1.10x their normal amounts. For more on the Caravan see the Caravan section.

    Your Partner

    At HR2 you should get a Partner. Enter the Rasta bar up the stairs of the town and talk to the waiter in blue at the counter. This lets contracted NPCs go on quests with you and lets you start your own Human NPC companion.

    1. Talk to the Legendary Rasta who uses the weapon type you want your Partner to use. Tonfa and Switch Axe are unlocked separately at G Rank. If you are unsure what weapon a Legendary uses, talk to them and choose the second option to see their gear.
    2. Choose Partner (Take Partner / Unlock Weapon) to start creating one.
    3. Choose a gender, then a personality.
    4. Confirm your choices. After this, go to your House. The NPC you talked to greets you there and you can customise your Partner as you did your own character.

    The best choices for AI and actual damage are Dual Swords or Sword and Shield. Great Sword is also not too bad. The other weapons have AI that is pretty stupid and spends more time doing nothing than attacking monsters.

    The personality types are listed below (male on the left, female on the right). Personality only decides the manner of speech and does not matter much.

    Partners have much more to them, but you can ignore it until G Rank unless you plan to spend a long time at HR5. At G Rank, read Partner on the House and AI Companions page.

    Felyne Partnyas

    Partnyas are Felyne companions like those in MHFU and MHP3rd. You manage them in the same area as the Halks (go to your house and choose the heart icon). To hire one, talk to the Halk Instructor, the only human NPC in the zone (he manages your helpers), and choose Hire Partnya. This starts Airou creation mode.

    There are five tabs for the look: Pattern, Eyes, Ears, Fur Colour and Tail. After you confirm, you name your Partnya and it appears on the island over the bridge. You can have 1 to 2 Partnyas at first (2 if you are in a guild, 1 if not), and you can get more when one is at Rank 300. More is on the Partnya section.

    For the fastest clearing of the first quests, open Options > NPC Settings and move your Partnya to the lowest priority. This stops it joining your quests and replacing a normal NPC.

    HR2 quests

    The HR2 keys are straightforward (all ten are listed with links on Progression). The one thing to know: in the Rathian hunt (Land Where the Queen Dwells), go to Area 6 and wait, because Area 10 is closed to hunters at night in the jungle.

    Different. You do not need to sell a big stack of cheques here: Low and High Rank armour upgrades are cheap since client patch v1.19 (one Armor Sphere a level). Keep the cheques and sell one when you need zenny. Our General Store does sell the Powercharm, the Armorcharm and the Storage Guide books (the game's own shop list), and your box also grows with your rank (the "More Storage!" gift, claimed once from a Gacha Felyne > Claim Items, also adds box pages). The Caravan Gem and its skills are game content, but the Caravan's server side (its rewards, shop boxes and Pioneering) is not built here (see Caravan). We did not test creating a Partner at the Rasta bar. Restyle points were bought with real money on the official servers: nothing is sold here, but our gift list holds some "ReStyle your Chara!" items (not tested). The path we advise is on Progression.

    Hunter Rank 3 and 4

    House and Transcend

    At HR3 the books tab of the General Store adds House Expansion 2 (9,000z here; House Expansion 1, 6,000z, appears there at HR2). The original guide buys this second house expansion now, next to the Storage Guide+ book. You also unlock Transcend: a customisable, time-based buff that augments your hunter's abilities. Like Wystones in MH4U, Transcend appears as a usable item among your consumables. Unlike a Wystone, the Transcend state starts with little charge and has to be powered up over time on quests.

    It starts as a slight increase to elemental and raw damage and becomes fairly powerful over the game. Full details are on the Transcend page.

    The HR3 quests are simple. HR3 is technically High Rank by the old rating system, so you no longer get supplies and you spawn at random spots in quests that support it.     Item Presets

    Item presets save your pouch for a quick restock. Set up your inventory exactly as you want it saved, open any box, choose Item Sets then Save Item Set (the guide wrote Item Presets > Register Preset), pick a slot and optionally name it. Slots 1 to 4 have shortcuts: L1/L2/R1/R2 or F/Ctrl/C/Shift while you interact with a box. So after a quest, run to a box, interact and press L1 or F to equip the preset from slot 1 at once.

    The client has 24 item sets: Item Set 5 to 24 are the extra 20 slots (the guide says subscribers got them under Item Presets > Equip Preset; every account has the courses here). These slots have no keyboard or controller shortcuts and must be equipped by hand. More in Equipment and Item Boxes.

    HR4 quests

    The HR4 key quests are standard. The Urgent, [Urgent] Desert Crystal Scorpion, is against Akura Vashimu, who uses the Frontier-exclusive Crystallisation status. The eight Key Quests are listed on Progression.

    • Crystallisation coats you in crystals and instantly drops your stamina bar to 25.
    • After a short time the crystals explode and hurt you and anyone near you.
    • You can shatter them by mashing and waggling the analog sticks, or by taking a hit from another hunter.
    • NPC companions automatically try to break you out if they are not crystallised themselves.

    Different. Transcend, bentos and My Tore are game content. Joining the newbie guild always fails here: join a player guild, whose Guild Store Cat sells the bento foods from guild rank 4. The General Store sells the Storage Guide books (Storage Guide 900z, Storage Guide+ 3,000z and Storage Guide・Extreme 15,000z) and both House Expansion books (Buy > Books (Combo, Box, Monsters)); your box also grows with every rank. The 20 extra preset slots were a subscriber perk; every account here has the Hunter Life and Extra courses, so they should be there (not tested in game). Where the guide says "midnight Taiwan time", timers the server handles count from midnight Japan time instead; we did not check each one.

    Hunter Rank 5: where the game starts

    HR5 is where the game really starts, a step up like reaching High Rank in the mainline games. All the Frontier exclusives added in recent years are HR5 or higher, so there is plenty of variety if you want to stay a while before G Rank. Monsters have bigger health pools, adjusted hitboxes and hit harder. The basic weapons you started with are far behind now, so make new ones (see Gou equipment below). From here, expect Keys and Urgents to be much closer to Online Quests in a typical Monster Hunter game. Hunter Rank and Style Rank goes deeper.

    Weapon styles

    HR5 unlocks Style Rank, which gives your weapon new styles, and lets you fight Hardcore versions of monsters. Many of these are big changes beyond what you see even in Sub or Rare species, and are worth trying.

    1. After reaching HR5, walk up to the Guild Master to begin Style Rank. You interact with her automatically after talking to her, which grants SR and turns it on.
    2. To enable the new movesets, use any large box that lets you change gear (such as the one at the Blacksmith). Our client's help text says to equip the book with Change Equipment > Equip Hiden Book (our client calls the Book of Secrets the Hiden Book); the box's equipment list also has SR Settings as its last option.
    3. Pick your weapon type, then the second option, then your preferred style.

    At HR5 you have Heaven Style and Storm Style. Storm Style is the best for most weapons at this rank, with some variation. You can swap styles at any time and are not locked into any of the three styles available at first. The table rates the three styles for each weapon.

    Charms, talons and claws

    On the original servers you had GCP tickets worth 300 GCP at HR5, saved for the Lao-Shan Claws. Hunsterverse There is no HR5 gift here. A Guild Point Tkt (3 in the New & Returning Hunter Pack, 1 in each Weekly Supply Drop) trades for 10 GCP at the General Store: GCP Exchange opens the GCP shop and the item-to-GCP list (the original guide calls this step Obtain GCP); see Currencies.

    The original guide spends that GCP on 2x Lao-Shan Claw (90 GCP each in its GCP shop). Combine each with a charm (Powercharm + Lao-Shan Claw = Powertalon, Armorcharm + Lao-Shan Claw = Armortalon), combine each talon with a second charm (Power Taloncharm, Armor Taloncharm), and combine the two Taloncharms a final time into the Hunter's Taloncharm. It gives the effects of all four items in one item slot: Attack +15, Defense +40. Hunsterverse Our GCP shop does not sell the Lao-Shan Claw (see the note at the end of this chapter); its item page lists where it drops.

    Armour upgrades

    You may not have needed to upgrade armour yet. For HR armours the game uses Armor Spheres, as the mainline games do. You get them as fixed extra rewards that depend on the tier of quest, from mining, and from several daily quests that give batches of 30 of two tiers each.

    Claim your Daily by talking to the General Store and choosing Daily Special (the fifth of its eight options; the last is Farm & Furniture). It gives your Daily Quest Right and 5 N Points. The original guide says it also gives some Halk Potions, which reduce damage taken during a quest but have a daily use limit; our server's daily claim gives no items.

    The ZZ update: Magnet Spike, Zenith Sigils, GR999 and more

    The ZZ update adds a new weapon class, new sigil types, a new monster mode and raises the G Rank limit to GR999. It was released like a "Z3": no new Exotics were added. The original guide kept a translated changelog, which we do not link.

    Magnet Spike

    Magnet Spikes have two modes you can morph between: cutting and impact. They deal the damage type they are named for and use those hitboxes. The name comes from being Magnets in a game full of Spikes; they are not spikes.

    Unlocking it

    Hunsterverse The three unlock quests are in our data (Magnetic Twin Stars: Gougarufu, Magnetic General: Rebidiora and Magnetic King: Rukodiora), but none of them is offered at any counter here: the G Rank Quest Counter's New Weapon Type Quests list is empty. We could not confirm how a hunter unlocks the Magnet Spike here, so treat the steps below as the original guide's story.

    A short story unlocks the weapon with a number of solo-only quests. At the end you unlock Magnet Spikes and get the materials for a Prototype MS for both you and your partner. The quests are not very hard, but being G Rank you will want at least your first free GR set. Short guide:

    1. Talk to the smithy and ignore what he says.
    2. Enter the Rasta bar and talk to the new NPC, Graham.
    3. Go out to the entrance and talk to the cat you have ignored many times.
    4. Go back in, talk to Graham and do the G Rank Lolo Gougarf and Ray Gougarf in the top section.
    5. Talk to Graham, then the Combiner in the town square.
    6. Hunt the G Rank Rebidiora in the same section as the Gougarfs.
    7. Go back and talk to Graham, the Smith, Graham again and the Road NPC.
    8. Post the new G Rukodiora in the topmost section and kill it.
    9. Talk to Graham after returning, then the Smith.

    Stats

    Magnet Spikes have no phials and no length. Their attack multiplier is 5.4: divide the displayed attack by 5.4 and round up to get the true raw.

    Using them

    • Both modes can be swapped to and unsheathed into at any time from idle, and neither depends on a meter. From any horizontal attack in one mode you can swap into a horizontal in the other.
    • Cutting mode has fast attacks and evasions like Dual Swords. Impact mode is slower with heavy single attacks, but still has quick swipes and a very fast charge.
    • In either mode you can toggle aiming mode. It lets you shoot a Magnetic Marker at a monster, or attract towards or repel away from a placed marker.
    • Attract or repel depends on distance. Too close allows only Repel (blue). Too far allows no action (white). The right distance allows Attract (red). Attraction chases a monster as long as you follow a straight path towards it and can be cancelled into an evade or a powerful falling attack.
    • Both modes charge as you use them. When the icon turns red you can magnetise the weapon: all attacks get 1.1x, like a power phial, with extra hits on some attacks, for 80 seconds. After it ends you cannot gain progress towards it again for a short time. Both modes charge separately and can stay at red charge indefinitely.
    • Both modes have a gauge used for evasions, attracting, repelling and the Magnetic Pin attack. It recharges over time and while attacking. Each action uses a single bar.

    Magnetic Pin

    The pin builds up like a normal status effect: hit the monster enough and a moving magnetism effect on its body shows it is ready to be pinned. You can pin out of Magnetic Double Slash or Double Swipe. You leap and slam your Magnet Spike down, which pins the monster and starts a QTE that holds it in place until you either perform the pin or fail the QTE.

    The QTE shows which directions and buttons to mash. If you mash well enough, the monster icon's face turns red and you can perform a Pin Finisher. It has an incredibly high motion value, makes the monster fall over, and holds it for a bit longer because the finisher animation is long. The finisher targets the spot where your magnetic marker is attached, so you can choose the best hitbox or the part you want to break. On tails it deals cutting damage but can only reduce a tail to 1 HP, so any single cutting hit then severs it.

    Zenith Sigils

    Zenith Sigils, new in ZZ, are used actively for a base duration of 15 to 25 seconds, rather than being always-on passives like standard Sigils. There are two kinds: Standard and AoE Zenith Sigils. They drop from doing any rank of Zenith. The overall drop rate is not known, but it is at the low end and probably follows the rare carve reward rate for each Zenith tier (1%, 3%, 5%, 7%). On your item bar a new sigil-shaped item appears. You can use it any time you are not mid-action, with the use button. Using one makes you do the Smoke Bomb animation and applies the buffs. More Sigil information is in Sigils.

    Standard Zenith Sigils

    A Standard Zenith Sigil gives you a number of buffs. The duration is a base of 15 seconds plus the sigil's duration value (for example +4 duration is 19 seconds). The recharge is a base of 2 minutes (120 seconds) minus the sigil's value (for example +14 recharge is 1:46, or 106 seconds). The sigil values do not map directly to the buffs and are far stronger than low rolls look: +15 Attack is really +330 attack. With good management a decent roll ends up as good as a top roll on a normal sigil.

    Area of Effect Sigils

    An AoE Zenith Sigil makes a dome on the ground where you use it, for a set duration of about 25 seconds and a recharge of 2 minutes. It can buff Attack, Elemental, Status, All Resistance, Affinity and Stun output, and give passive healing. You can roll several buff types on one sigil, and the dome alternates between colours to show which buffs are active. The values do not match the buff directly either: a sigil with +1 elemental and +14 to Resistances actually gives Elemental +100 and All Resistances +28. Higher rolls give much more.

    GR999 and Unlimited Mode

    GR999 is the new maximum rank, reached with a total of 13,716,950 GRP. From the start of GR899 you need 2,392,200 GRP. You can stack many buffs to multiply the gain: Diva Song, Log In Boost, Hardcore Mode and any Premium Courses. A GRP quest against a Gogomoa for 8,000 GRP shipped with ZZ (Mega GRP: Gogomoa, G Rank Quest Counter > Event Quests; it needs GR 900 here since 24 Nov). Reaching GR999 adds item and equipment box pages (the Hunter Navi reward Storage Guide・Illusion expands both boxes as soon as you take it), and the usual shops add SR ingredients and past event armour that only GR999 hunters can buy. The game does not say which counter sells which; the original guide puts the SR food at the food shop and the old event armour with an NPC at the Blacksmith. These sets are of little use unless you rushed to GR999 on premium without building a real set.

    Unlimited Mode (UL)

    GR999 unlocks Unlimited Mode, a per-quest toggle on some monsters that works much like Hardcore. It sets the monster's Attack, Health and similar values to around those of 3-4 star Zenith. Monsters with a hardcore variant use their hardcore moveset; monsters that never had one (such as Pokaradon) use their base moveset. Quests you can toggle are marked with a blue mark (the guide's Blue Flame icon) and cover all standard G star ratings. You switch a quest to Unlimited when you post it (the GR999 letter sends you to the G Rank Receptionist first), you need SR gear equipped for it, and the Always Post UL option makes it the default. There are no Burst, Origin or Exotic monsters in UL mode.

    New ZZ monsters

    Bogabadorumu

    A blast monster that comes with the new skill Furious and the new Zenith skill Rush. Cuffs for both use his materials, not any standard G monster's. His weapons have Blast Element, which post-rework still does not seem very good, but they have Rush: free infinite stamina and +70 true on top of the usual Rush buffs, which makes them good Raw choices. He has Ultra Quake and Roar, Blast, and can stun you into an on-your-knees stance even with stun immunity. His Zenith Parts are his forelegs, which only become Zenith parts midway through the fight; both must be broken. His tail can be cut and his head and back can be broken.

    Zenith Gravios

    Comes with the new skill Trained, a hybrid skill with Focus, Martial Arts and the equivalent of Caring +3. Its Zenith Part is the chest. The head can also be broken and the tail severed. It can inflict Extreme Fireblight and has Ultra Roar and Ultra Quake. The weapons are Fire and Blast with different Zenith skills per weapon class; they are not very good. The armour is mediocre, with points in Trained but needing more pieces than is reasonable to reach +2.

    Zenith Baruragaru

    Can pin you, cause sharpness loss and defence down, cause extreme water blight and has an Extreme level Roar. Its Zenith Part is the head, extending to the tongue. The armour is mostly mediocre, with standouts in the Blademaster Chest (+10 Charge Attack Up, +6 Trained) and the Gunner Helmet (+10 Mounting). More about Zenith monsters is on Zenith.

    PZ cuffs

    ZZ adds PZ cuffs: upgraded versions of the SZ cuffs, with a single Zenith Skill and up to 5 points in a meta skill. Unlike earlier Premium Cuffs they are only in a Lucky Box style gacha that costs 30 coins up front and rolls for 10 cuffs. You cannot roll duplicates, since each cuff is removed as you roll it, so 150 coins (about 90 USD) guarantees every cuff. There are a fair number of useful cuffs but more mediocre and brick rolls, so you could easily spend 90 coins before seeing one you want. Five series of PZ cuffs have launched since ZZ. Using a PZ cuff is always the best choice, especially with Hiden Cuffs launching in the same update cycle.

    New Tower weapons

    New Tower weapons arrived a couple of weeks into the ZZ update, just before Zenith Gravios. They are extremely strong, with very high potential Raw, Element and Status values. They only have Tower Sigil Slots (no decorations), but their stats outclass any weapon that is not a Raviente or Road weapon, and they are best in slot outside a handful of Raviente weapons, even for the hardest content. You can craft one weapon a week, and upgrade them with standard tower gems from the Hunting Road shop plus a small number of Zenith Materials. They remove almost any reason to grind normal weapons: all new Zenith monsters have launched with bricked weapons since this series. Their one downside: Tower weapons do not have the Zenith Partbreaker effect.

    ZZ Update 1

    • New monsters: Zenith Taikun Zamuza, Zenith Harudomerugu, Musou Zerureusu. The weapons from the new Zeniths are useless on day 0 because of Blue Tower Weapons, but the armour pieces are good.
    • New Zenith skill Ceaseless: relaxes hit requirements, makes the skill's buff stages go down one at a time instead of resetting to zero, and adds a third stage with more Affinity and Crit Multiplier.
    • New Road and Tower weapons: GS and Hammer get Very Long versions of the Blue Tower series, and Lance gets a Long version (more usable than the very long one). Crimson Demon Road weapons are added, one weekly from the Hunting Road shop; they are globally pretty bad, worse than the Burning Zero Series and the Festival Chroma Series.
    • Other changes: UL Mode adds Zenaserisu, White Hypnocatrice, White Espinas, Silver Rathalos and Gold Rathian. Legendary Rastas completely ignore all Zenith Blights, Ultra Roars, Wind and Quake. New options rebind the interact-with-NPC key and let you always run in town. The Hunter's Road has new equipment and lets you earn medals more easily, including a new skill for extras. Diva Defense has new weapons, including a Magnet Spike.

    New Hiden Cuffs

    New cuffs give Hiden points and stop the Hiden using a skill slot. They are separate from normal skill cuffs and take no actual outfit slots. You can equip a single Hiden cuff alongside any other cuff. There are three power levels:

    • Basic Cuffs: +6 points in a Hiden and the Hiden does not use a skill slot.
    • G Cuffs: +12 points in a Hiden and the Hiden does not use a skill slot.
    • True Cuffs: +12 points in a Hiden, +2 points in a Meta Skill and the Hiden does not use a skill slot.

    Hunsterverse The Hunting Road shop sells them: Basic Hiden cuffs from GR 50 (10,000 RdP), G cuffs from GR 150 (20,000 RdP) and the top (True) cuffs from GR 350 (35,000 RdP); True Hiden decorations from GR 500 (50,000 RdP). See Progression.

    The first two levels use standard Hiden materials; you cannot exchange HL Hiden tickets for them. A True cuff needs a single True Hiden decoration. There are 8 versions of these cuffs for each weapon type, and any True Hiden decoration can make any of them. The guide says it cannot be understated how enabling these are for building sets, and how much power creep they bring.

    Zenith nerfs

    This update nerfs all GR200 to GR400 Zeniths considerably, which changes the difficulty of entry-level Zeniths a lot.

    • GR200 monsters (GR 300 here): standardised to about 86% of their original health and 80% or less of their attack.
    • GR400 monsters (GR 500 here): standardised to about 80% of their original health and 90% or less of their attack.
    • At both GR200 and GR400, weaker outliers such as Tigrex only lost about 93% of their health but took the same attack cut as the others.

    As a result, some standard G Rank monsters now have more HP and Attack than their "Zenith" versions, and a GR100 Shagaru Magala has more HP than every GR200 Zenith except Taikun. On the plus side, you can ignore the old non-Zenith content more easily. The guide's last words on the update: Monster Hunter Frontier as a whole was being shut down on the official servers.
