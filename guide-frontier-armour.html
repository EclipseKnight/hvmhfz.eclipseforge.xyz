    Armour, Decorations and Skills

    How to craft and upgrade armour, what each armour family does, how to unlock armour colours and transmog, and a full reference of decorations and armour skills.

    Making armour

    When you have a Partner, the Blacksmith first asks whether you are making equipment for them or for yourself. You can tell which you chose at once: your Partner takes your place on screen when you craft for them.

    At the Blacksmith, open the Armour section and pick the kind of armour to make. The main Blacksmith menu is Weapons, Armour, Decorations and Pugi Cuffs. Menu names may differ slightly in our client.

    Upgrading armour

    All armour made before G Rank works like the current mainline games. You craft the base armour from materials, then upgrade it with Armour Spheres. At level 7 you may be able to move to a second tier of the same armour (F, FX, HS and so on). That tier uses items again, but levels 2 to 6 always use Armour Spheres. You find Armour Spheres by hunting or by mining.

    Armour stats and skills

    Each piece shows its rarity, defense and the five elemental resistances (fire, water, thunder, ice, dragon).

    To see a piece's skills while crafting, press Square on a PS controller or Z on a keyboard, then change pages with L1/R1 or F/C. The skills are on about the third page. The number of decoration slots is on the second page of an armour or weapon's information.

    To work out what a skill tree does, look it up in the skill reference below. The original guide's author also made a Skill Hunter tool that identified most skills from a screenshot, which we do not link.

    Works the same. Crafting works the same. Premium kit and lottery armour came from the Item Mall and the gacha, which are not available here.

    Armour types

    Standard armour

    Standard armour is the same as in the mainline games, except that you both craft and upgrade it with materials instead of Armour Stones.

    SP armour and SP gems

    SP armour is mostly out of date. Its main use now is collecting sets. At HR5 you can craft SP armour and SP gems. They are meant to be used together: SP armour has no natural skill points, so it needs SP gems to be useful. It has a single decoration slot for these gems.

    SP gems usually give many points in a few skills, such as +10 Hearing Protection and +15 Psychic, which switches on Autotracker and Earplugs at once. You craft both from the normal armour and gem menus. They need materials that only drop in HR5 quests.

    Each SP piece comes in several colours. The colours are cosmetic and need coloured grasses to make. You can trade grasses to the Veggie Elder (in the Gorge) for colours you are missing.

    HC armour

    HC (Hardcore) armour is an upgraded version of standard or Premium armour. It is mostly the same, but on any HC quest it regenerates health while your health bar is above 50%. It also needs some stamina, depending on how many pieces you wear.

    HS armour

    HS (Hunting Safeguard) armour is an upgraded HC armour. It has every HC effect. On an HC quest with either an HC weapon or a weapon of Heavenly Storm or higher, it also adds defensive buffs.

    G Rank armour

    G Rank armour is the standard armour of G Rank. It works much like standard armour, but gives two bonuses based on how many G Rank pieces you wear. A higher skill limit is how you fit 11 or 12 skills on one set.

    PiecesAttack bonusSkill limit
    1-2 piecesnone10
    3-4 pieces+30 true raw11
    5 pieces+30 true raw12

    G Rank armour is also the main source of G Rank decorations. When a piece reaches level 7 in its upgrade tier (G, GF or GX) you can convert it into a decoration. See G Rank decorations below and G Rank.

    GS and GP armour

    GS, GP or G Hunting Safeguard armour is the G Rank upgrade of Premium armour. Only Premium armour can take this path, such as armour from Premium Kits or lotteries. These pieces keep every advantage of HS armour (same table as above) and also count as normal G Rank armour pieces for the G Rank bonuses.

    GP armour is usually a little worse than standard GX armour in defense, but it makes up for it with a wider choice of skills and is easy to fit into "clownsuits" (sets that mix pieces from many families).

    Gou armour and its upgrades

    Gou (Goushu) armour opens at HR5. Every piece needs proofs from its monster (a ticket you get each time you slay a Gou monster) and 1x Gou Elder Dragon Gem (the Chinese name is 古龍種的稀玉). The gem comes from Gou Rukodiora breaks and tail carves, at 10% each for tail carves and horn breaks. Some quests that cycle in give the gem 100% of the time, which is ideal for making Gou armour. You also get 3 of the gems when you make your first Gou weapon.

    Gou armour gives a Boost (or Muscle) effect. While it is active, certain skills go up one full level. For example, 50 points in Attack give Attack Up (Very Large); with the Boost it becomes Attack Up (Absolute), worth 30 free points of Attack. Not every skill can be boosted. A skill that can be boosted shows a light blue arrow next to its name on your Guild Card. See also Muscle Boost.

    The Boost needs a certain amount of health (and 26 stamina), depending on how many Gou-line pieces you wear.

    • Supremacy opens at SR300. It is the same as Heavenly Storm, but the Boost needs much less health.
    • G Supremacy opens at GR1. It works like Supremacy, with higher defense, and its upgrade needs G Rank materials.
    • Burst is the second G Rank upgrade of Gou armour and opens at GR1. Its upgrade needs materials from G Rank Burst monsters and GHC materials.
    • Origin is the third G Rank upgrade and opens at GR1. Its upgrade needs materials from G Rank Origin monsters and GHC materials.

    Tenrou (Tower) armour

    Tenrou (also called Customisable Tower armour) is made and upgraded with items found only in the Tower (Sky Corridor). It works much like standard armour and upgrades all the way to GX. The big difference is that you can slot special Tower decorations into it. They give the properties of other armour types.

    Exotic armour

    Exotic armour is imported from the mainline games. Each piece has a skill built in that is always active, whatever your skill limit or other factors. You need GR500 to upgrade an Exotic piece to G or higher, because Exotic monsters are GR500 in G Rank. When upgraded to G or higher, Exotic armour counts as a standard G Rank piece for the skill limit and for the extra raw.

    Zenith armour

    Zenith armour comes in two kinds, Standard and Premium. Both carry a Zenith skill (see Zenith skills).

    • Standard Zenith armour is made from a specific monster (such as Khezu Z Series) or from certain events (such as Katante Z Series from Diva Defense). It has four tiers. Z and ZF have one upgrade each, and ZY and ZX have 7. The letters match the GR rating of the Zenith monsters you hunt to make them: GR200 for Z, GR400 for ZF, GR600 for ZY and GR800 for ZX. It has its Zenith skill from Z on, but only reaches maximum skill points at ZX.
    • Premium Zenith armour is made by upgrading Premium pieces past GP to ZP. Every ZP upgrade needs Zenith Hypnoc parts. Lottery armour needs 2 extra tickets, which you must have kept from earlier lottery rolls or win again. The gain varies from a complete rework (Lien GP to ZP changes every skill and becomes one of the best kits in the game) to simply adding a Zenith skill while keeping the old stats (Cielo ZP).

    Premium Zenith armour cannot be turned into decorations. Both kinds also give the biggest Origin benefits for gunners: with 2 or more pieces, Critical Distance multipliers go up on all ranged weapons, and bows get Power Coatings of 1.7x, up from 1.5x.

    Different. Armour stats and effects work the same, but Premium armour (kits, lotteries) needs the Item Mall and the gacha, which are not available here. Diva Defense armour needs that event, which runs since 4 Nov (new, not much played yet; its armour rewards were not checked).

    Transmog

    MHF-Z lets you apply the look of one armour piece to another. Talk to the Blacksmith and give the items for the exterior you want. What you need varies. Standard armour wants monster materials (G1% items, normal carves and so on). Some exteriors are tied to events, and lottery armours are tied to lottery rolls.

    An armour in the "Billing" list is not necessarily for sale. Some premium exteriors are limited to events, and others came only from timed lotteries that may never return (for example the Nier 2B exterior).

    Armour and icon colours

    In Frontier you unlock armour colours by doing various tasks. The colour is also the one shown behind your weapon icon in town. Colours other than Green, Red and White need at least HR3 to use.

    The colour menu itself unlocks when you make or get any armour piece that lets you set its colour. The easiest is the T-shirt sold by the NPC with glasses in the Blacksmith.

    Short unlocking guides

    White, Red and Green. Green is easy while you work on your Caravan Gem: delivering 3 iron ore counts the same as killing a monster and takes seconds, and all deliveries count, so about an hour is enough. Red you will almost certainly unlock just by playing. It is likely your first colour. White is hard now, because newer content is all monsters you cannot capture. The best route is to buy 2x Capture Gem G (捕獲珠Ｇ) from the Road store. They give Capture Guru, which makes monsters blink on the mini-map when they can be captured. You can also get Autotracker from 3x Insight Gem G (洞察珠Ｇ), but that is much less practical. Paintballs and psychoserums work just as well.

    Blue. The friend list is one-sided and does not send a prompt, so you can add 50 random players and delete them afterwards. Do delete them, or you will spam them with messages whenever you sign in.

    Dark Blue. You must craft 20 Rare-7 weapons of each type to unlock the Weapon Maniac titles. Duplicate weapons do not count. G Rank weapons do not count as over Rare-7, and the tonfa and switch axe titles are not considered. Pure raw SP weapons upgrade to Rare-7, and bowguns can be made at Rare-7 or higher, unlike most other weapons. Make whichever type you have the most materials for.

    Light Green. Make 35 of the Chef chest piece (廚師服飾). It needs only raw meat and items from the Pugi Farm store:

    Purple. You need a pet monster. Talk to the Blue NPC in town and choose Hunter Dojo, then Select Training, then Arena Training (獵人道場 > 選擇演習 > 鬥技演習). This lists Arena quests with preset gear. Do the first 3 to unlock pets: hunt 2 Velocidromes, hunt 1 Gypceros and hunt 1 Rathian.

    Decorations

    As in the mainline games, decorations give points in armour skills and fit in any armour piece or weapon with a free slot. You create them at the Blacksmith. The decoration menu has Create Decorations, Insert Decorations and Remove Decorations.

    Standard decorations

    Standard decorations are the most basic kind. You make them at the Blacksmith from normal materials. They usually give about 5 points in total and may have negative points in some skills. For example, the standard Artisan Deco (匠珠) gives +2 Artisan, +2 Auto-Reload and -1 Adrenaline.

    Many have more than one recipe. The Artisan Deco can be made from 1x Rajang Fur and 3x Monoblos Horn, or from 2x Lunstra Horn and 3x White Monoblos Horn. You will rarely need standard decorations beyond HR7.

    G decorations

    G decorations are stronger standard decorations. You make them by improving existing decorations, or by collecting special materials made just for them.

    G Rank decorations

    Most G Rank decorations can be crafted directly from a handful of materials: usually a couple of lower-percentage standard carves and a GHC carve. They are almost always available from sources such as the Road, Festival or GCP stores. A few need you to craft and upgrade armour, such as White Fatalis, Keoaruboru or Hiden decorations, but they are a small minority. Even event decorations now use only their base materials. G and GF decorations can still only be made from armour pieces, but you are unlikely to use them in a modern G Rank set.

    The G decoration crafting menu has sections to make a specific decoration easier to find: Direct Crafting, Show All, Monsters, Hunter Festival, Regular Event, Event and Other.

    Making G Rank gems from armour

    The older way to get G Rank gems is to refine level 7 G Rank armour. Each piece gives a single gem and is destroyed. You will only rarely need to do this, as most gems can be made from the menus above.

    • A level 7 G piece refines into one G decoration.
    • A level 7 GF piece refines into one GF decoration.
    • A level 7 GX piece refines into one of GX1 to GX5, depending on the piece: helmet GX1, chest GX2, arms GX3, waist GX4, legs GX5. This matches the order at the Blacksmith.

    A few armours have more than one G or GF gem. They use the same numbering, so a GF3 decoration needs level 7 GF arms of that armour.

    Building sets

    Set building matters mostly in G Rank. Sets you use before then are not very important or long-lasting.

    Unlike the mainline games, skills in Frontier are very important. You are expected to carry a certain core set of skills to clear normal content in a decent time. The original guide had a separate page on picking skills for each weapon type, which we do not link; our armour set builder finds sets for the skills you pick.

Bows always want Auto-Reload, which is terrible on bowguns, so never use a bow set with a bowgun.

    Skills

    Skill basics

    Skills switch on when the points on your armour pieces and decorations reach a threshold. The default limit is 10 active skills. In G Rank it can rise to 11 or 12, depending on the armour you wear (see G Rank armour).

    Every skill has a hidden priority that decides whether it activates. You can check what a set will really give in the MHSX2G Set Generator. Priority matters late in the game, because it is easy to switch on skills you do not want. This is especially true of sets with Focus (Quick Charge), which has the lowest priority of all skills: nearly anything else pushes it out.

    Hybrid skills

    As in the newest mainline games, Frontier has skills that combine other skills. If you have one of the skills that a hybrid gives, it shows grey on your Guild Card. A grey skill is disabled but still uses one of your 10 to 12 skill slots. At G Rank you normally run 11 to 12 active skills, so a wasted slot is very bad: avoid it.

    Hybrid skillContains

    Sometimes a skill is grey in town but still works on quests. That happens when you have the real skill and a boost on it, and also have it inside a hybrid. For example, with Sword God +1 and Fencing +1, Fencing +1 shows disabled in town. On a quest the Muscle Boost raises Fencing to +2, so it is active. Boosts are explained below.

    Exotic, Tower and weapon skills

    Exotic, Tower, Burst and Origin skills are not shown on the Guild Card. They are always active, and they can make skills on the list look greyed out. From Z1 on they appear in a new section of Hunter Status.

    • Exotic armour pieces always activate a certain skill, with any number of pieces equipped. A Zinogre Helmet always gives Thunder Attack Up (Large), ignoring skill limits and every other piece, and wearing all 5 pieces still gives it once.
    • Burst and Origin weapons have fixed skills that work the same way. They are fixed versions that higher versions replace, and extra skill points do not add to them. A weapon that gives Issen +2 simply gives the skill, not 20 points, so you still need 30 points in your skills for Issen +3: you cannot just add 10.
    • Tower weapons take decorations that add armour skills in the same fixed way. You choose which skills to slot instead of the base weapon deciding. These skills still add no points. Even with Good Luck slotted, you would still need 20 points in Fate to get Great Luck.

    Other sources of skills

    Outside equipment there are two sources: Skill Fruits and Guild Cooking.

    • Skill Fruits are a premium item. Eating one activates a skill for the whole quest, and it ends when you finish or fail the quest. Retiring returns the item, as with all others. You buy them with Lottery G Coins from the Pink Shop NPC at 30 fruits for 3 coins. Events and other purchases can also give them. They are not worth going out of your way for, unless you are a speedrunner at a fully maxed endgame who wants to shave more time.
    • Guild Cooking opens to guilds once they reach a certain level. You use ingredients in a cooking minigame to make food that gives the skill you cooked for, on every quest you take during its duration. Easy recipes include Wide-Area +3, Adrenaline +2 and Goddess' Embrace. See English Guild and Guild Hall.

    Muscle Boost

    Gou armour and its upgrades cause a Boost (or Muscle) effect. It raises a skill by one level. With Evasion +1 under the Boost you would have Evasion +2, saving 10 points. If you wear the right armour type, a skill that can be boosted has a light blue arrow next to its name on your Guild Card. It goes up a level on a quest while you meet the armour's conditions, and the skill's name turns blue while it is really boosted. In the example, Martial Arts and Fencing are boosted. Fencing was grey because another skill gave +1, but it becomes active on the quest as +2 overwrites it.

    The conditions are health and stamina amounts that depend on the armour type; they are in the Gou armour table above. The Boost needs 26 or more stamina.

    Its upgrades also add, per piece, Attack +15, Attribute +2% and HC rarity requirement -1 (Origin: Attack +20, Attribute +3%, rarity -1). For the sharpness, Critical Distance and Power Coating bonuses, see Gou armour and its upgrades.

    Works the same. Skills and their priorities work the same. Skill Fruits are bought with Lottery G Coins in a coin shop that has no entries on our server, and no account holds coins, so expect them to be unavailable.

    Zenith skills

    How Zenith skills work

    You get a Zenith skill by equipping a piece that carries it. Zenith skills mainly buff existing skills. Apart from Skill Slots Up, they have no effect of their own. They are added on top of the normal skill points: a maxed Zenith armour piece has its usual points in 5 skills and a Zenith skill as well.

    Zenith skills appear on Cuffs, weapons and armour pieces. Both Premium (ZP) and normal Zenith (Z, ZF, ZY, ZX) armour have them, and you always have to hunt real Zenith monsters to get them. Cuffs are standard cuffs, so you can use them without a Premium Pugi outfit.

    Having more than the limit of a Zenith skill adds nothing. Dissolver Up has only 1 level, so having it on 5 pieces is the same as on 1. Hybrid skills count as their parts: Fortification +2 counts as Guard +2 for Guard Up, and Three Worlds Protection +3 counts as the maxed Wind Pressure, Hearing Protection and Quake Resistance. There is no limit on how many different Zenith skills you can have: with 7 different ones across your cuff, weapon and armour, you get all 7 as long as the skills they buff are active.

    Example: Guard Up +1 raises Guard by one level, and with Guard +2 already active it adds the extra Zenith effects shown below. With no Guard skill at all, Guard Up does nothing.

    Skill Slots Up

    Skill Slots Up is always active, on top of any other source of extra slots. Pieces of Z, ZY, ZX or ZP level count towards the G Rank piece requirements for slots. You can find it on armour pieces, weapons and cuffs, for a maximum of 7 extra slots. It stacks with the +1 or +2 slots from having 3 or 5 standard G Rank pieces (Z, ZF, ZY, ZP and ZX included).
