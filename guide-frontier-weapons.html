    Weapons and Sigils

    The 14 weapon types, how weapon families differ, element and bowgun references, daily Active Features, and the full list of sigils for G Rank weapons.

    Weapon types

    Tonfa and Switch Axe F are only available at G Rank. The Switch Axe F unlocks as soon as you reach G Rank. The Tonfa needs a string of specific quests first. The guide has a page for each weapon with more detail (and one each for the Tonfa and Switch Axe F). This page covers what they share.

    Making weapons

    When you have a Partner, the Blacksmith first asks whether you are making equipment for them or for yourself. You can tell which you chose at once: your Partner takes your place on screen when you craft for them.

    The Blacksmith's main menu has four parts: Weapons, Armour, Decorations and Pugi Cuffs. Menu names may differ slightly in our client. Under Weapons you choose a family:

    MenuEntries
    WeaponsStandard Weapons, Upgrade Weapons, Gou Weapons, G Supremacy Weapons, G Rank Weapons, Upgrade G Rank Weapons, Exotic Weapons, Raviente / Evolution Weapons, Zenith Weapons, Premium Weapons
    Premium WeaponsPrem Kit Weapons 1, Prem Kit Weapons 2, Lottery Weapons, NetCafe Weapons, HL/EX Weapons, Event/Promo Weapons, Exotic Weapons

    Works the same. Crafting works the same. The Premium kit and lottery weapons came from the Item Mall and the gacha, which are not available here, so those entries will mostly lead nowhere.

    Weapon families

    Weapons are grouped by where they come from and what they add. Each family below is a step up in power or a special case.

    Standard weapon
    Normal weapons with no special properties. They are the baseline for every other family.
    SP weapon
    HR5 weapons that are reskins of other weapons. They get +10 true raw and +20% affinity automatically on HR5 or higher quests. They have had little purpose since their original launch.
    HC weapon
    HR5 weapons with a low rarity but decent power for that rarity. Like SP weapons, they have little purpose now.
    Master's Mark weapon
    Mostly the same as normal weapons, but they give the skill Weapon Handling for free, with no skill slot and no points. Unless they are one of a handful that upgrade to G Finesse, they have little purpose.
    Evolution weapon
    These weapons need you to hunt Raviente to upgrade them. They have 300 levels across three tiers (HR2, HR5 and GR) and end up among the best weapons in the game, and the best true raw weapons outright. They take hundreds of hours of grind, so ignore them unless you are very serious about getting them and already have endgame skills. Each weapon type gets its own extra tool:
    • Melee weapons can use Raviente Power Crystals (Power Sword Crystals), which boost raw damage by 1.2x. You can carry 30.
    • Light Bowguns can use Acid Shots, raw or one of the five elements. They add +10 to the weakness value of that type for 30 seconds. You can carry 5.
    • Heavy Bowguns can use Bomb Shots: lingering shots that damage over time inside a radius. You can carry 5.
    • Bows can use Impact Coatings. They make your shots use Impact hitbox values and cause KO, but the shots still deal Shot-type damage, so they cannot break things limited to Impact, such as Daimyo shells. You can carry 30.
    See Raviente for how to get them.
    Gou weapon
    The core HR5 weapon type. Their attack grows with the number of Gou (or higher) armour pieces you wear (see Gou weapon boosts).
    • Melee weapons gain ESP while you are at maximum HP and stamina.
    • Light Bowguns can have Ultra Rapid Fire, which lets you keep feeding ammo into one rapid-fire volley.
    • Heavy Bowguns have a Heat Cannon. It turns the heat built up by steady shooting into a giant beam, like a Gravios.
    • Bows can use Bomb Coatings, which deal bomb damage.
    Heavenly Storm weapon
    On HR5 or higher quests, wearing 2 or more pieces of Heavenly Storm armour (or better) raises Blademaster sharpness by one level (purple to cyan, and so on). Gunners get higher critical range multipliers and stronger power coatings.
    Supremacy weapon
    The same as Heavenly Storm, with a different glow effect and higher base stats.
    G Supremacy weapon
    The same as Supremacy, with a different glow effect and higher base stats.
    Burst weapon
    Mostly the same as G Supremacy, with a different glow effect and much higher base stats. It also gives a skill just for being equipped, shown in its description. This is often something like Vamprism, Exploit Weakness or Evade Distance.
    Origin weapon
    Mostly the same as Burst, with a different glow effect and much higher base stats. Like Burst weapons, it gives a skill just for being equipped, often Vamprism, Exploit Weakness or Evade Distance.
    G Rank weapon
    G Rank weapons have sigils instead of decorations, and a level system that goes up to Lv50. A Lv50 weapon is usually called "G50" or similar. See Sigils.
    G Rank Finesse
    Upgraded forms of various event, festival and normal weapons. They have the faster sheathing and unsheathing of Weapon Handling, plus Movement Speed +1. From G8 you can upgrade normal Lv50 G Rank weapons into Finesse versions with better stats.
    Tower weapon
    A customisable weapon made from materials you find in the Sky Corridor. You can customise sharpness, raw, element, status, shelling type, shot type and more, so each one is unique to its player. Fully upgraded, it can rival a Lv50 G Rank weapon, depending on several factors. The decorations you put into it give skills outright, with no slot use, which is very strong when built well.
    Exotic weapon
    The weapons of the mainline import (Exotic) monsters. They start at HR5, or at GR for the Switch Axe and Tonfa, and have 2 or 1 upgrades after crafting. At maximum they have the stats of a G50 weapon, take both decorations and sigils, and give Quick Eating without using a slot or skill points. To finish them you need access to G Rank exotics, and each needs its monster's lowest-percentage carve (such as a Jasper or Mantle).
    Prayer weapon
    Tied to the Diva Defense event and given as daily or personal rewards. By default they have the stats of a low-end G50 weapon. But with a Prayer active during the Song of War chapter of the event, they get a huge boost, enough to beat even Lv100 G Raviente weapons. The boost is:
    • True raw +45
    • Doubled element
    • Weapon Handling and Movement Speed +2
    • Turns on the weapon type's Active Feature
    • Sharpness colours raised by one tier
    • Turns on the special Dual Hiden skill
    • Interception Points raised to 1.2x
    See Diva Defense.
    Zenith weapon
    G Rank weapons that open at GR200 and are finished only after GR800. Their stats go beyond a standard G50 weapon, and each has a unique ability and one Zenith skill. The ability is better Zenith part breaking, which makes it easier to destroy the parts of Zenith monsters that reward the unique items all Zenith equipment needs. Zenith skills either upgrade an existing skill or add skill slots. For example Thunder Clad Up lengthens Thunder Clad when it is on a Zenith weapon or armour piece. See Zenith.

    Different. Weapon stats work the same, and Raviente runs (see Events). The Diva Defense weapons need that event, which runs since 4 Nov; it is new and not much played yet, and whether the weapons are among its rewards was not checked.

    Gou weapon boosts

    If you use a weapon from a Gou line that is at least Heavenly Storm, its stats grow with the armour you wear. Every piece of Heavenly Storm, Supremacy, G Supremacy or Burst armour adds +15 Attack, +2% Status and +2% Element. It stacks per piece, to a maximum of +80 Attack, +10% Status and +10% Element.

    With two or more of those pieces you also get Sharpness Level Up, Critical Distance Up +0.3x and Power Coatings Up +0.1x.

    • Sharpness Level Up raises the sharpness colour by one full level.
    • Critical Distance Up greatly improves well-spaced shots for ranged weapons.
    • Power Coatings Up improves power coatings further (for example 1.6x becomes 1.7x).

    These effects only work on Gou, Supremacy or G Rank quests. That is where you will spend most of your time anyway.

    On a Gou-line weapon, every piece of Origin armour adds +20 Attack, +3% Status and +3% Element, up to +110 Attack, +15% Status and +15% Element. Any single Origin piece gives Sharpness Level Up on the right Gou weapon types.

    Origin pieces matter most to gunners. Their Critical Distance Up and Power Coatings Up buffs apply to all G Rank weapons, not just Gou-line ones. That is a huge buff for standard weapons. It is almost never worth wearing a standard armour piece over an Origin one, even with Zenith armour added in the Z update. Note that the extra Attack, Element and Status do not apply to non-Gou weapons.

    For the other armour families and their Boost conditions, see Armour: Gou armour.

    Works the same. Weapon and armour effects live in the client and work the same.

    Melee weapons

    Sharpness

    Sharpness in Frontier has 8 levels at present, from red to cyan. The picture shows each level with its raw multiplier on top and its element multiplier below. Blue sharpness or higher also adds 10% affinity.

    The melee weapon screen

    The information window shows attack, length, sharpness, element and status, weapon type, rarity, affinity and slots. Some terms appear in the Blacksmith and weapon lists:

    • Weapon length: Very Short, Short, Normal, Long, Very Long.
    • Gunlance shots: Normal Shot, Spread Shot, Long Shot, plus the Shelling Level.
    • Sword crystals: Explosive, Power, Poison, Paralysis, Sleep, Fire, Ice, Thunder, Ice (the guide lists this one twice) and Dragon.
    • GR200 sword crystals: Inferno, Tsunami, Storm, Glacial and Elder Dragon.

    Weapon length

    Weapon length is a mechanic unique to Frontier. It changes the weapon's total reach and its sweet spot to match. On a very short Great Sword, almost the whole blade becomes the sweet spot, at the cost of a lot of range. On a very long one the opposite is true.

    Sword crystals

    Sword crystals need their own skill to use. Each has three tiers, and +3 gives the most damage or status per crystal. To apply a crystal, hold R2 and press the right analog stick in (R3), or press Shift plus the Minus key (not on the numpad), or use the touchpad on the Vita.

    GR200 sword crystals are purely elemental, and do about as much elemental damage as three hits from the normal ones. You can only buy them for GCP from the Pink store after reaching GR200.

    Works the same. Sharpness works the same.

    Bowguns

    A bowgun's window lists its ammo, reload speed and recoil, and its upgrades. Each bowgun's special abilities are covered in the guide's detailed weapon pages.

    • Reload speed: Very Slow, Slow, Normal, Fast, Very Fast.
    • Recoil: Medium, Small, Smaller.
    • Ammo: Normal, Pierce, Pellet, Crag, Cluster, Recovery, Poison, Paralysis, Sleep, Fire, Water, Thunder, Ice, Dragon, Tranq, Paint, Demon and Armour Shot, plus Rapid Fire and Ultra Rapid Fire.

    Critical Distance

    Critical Distance is shown when the shot expands fully and makes a circle. The Heavy Bowgun has a much larger impact animation in the first half of critical distance on standard shots, so it is very obvious when you are properly spaced. This adds an extra 0.3x to damage in that zone (for example 2.0 in the first half of Normal Shot range, 2.3x with an Origin piece and a G Rank weapon).

    Sigils

    Sigils are like decorations, but only for G Rank weapons. You craft them at the Cat Smith, who makes random Gou weapons.

    Sigil slots are triangular. They either replace the decoration slots on standard G Rank weapons, or they are hybrid slots that take either a decoration or a sigil. A weapon can have up to three sigils. Sigils with a variable value generally stack. Sigils that add or improve abilities and motions have a fixed effect, however many you slot.

    Sigil tables below show the skill name, the range of points (for example -10 / +15) a sigil can roll, and its effect. A dash means the sigil has no value. "Does not stack" means a second one of the same sigil adds nothing.

    Choosing sigils

    Most weapons have dedicated sigils, but many are far from optimal. Think about how often you will really use the attack. For example, the Dual Swords sigils for Rush Slash and Frontflip Slash are of very low value for Extreme Style. Extreme Demon Mode cannot use them, and good play uses that mode almost all the time.

    In the same way, the Great Sword's Guard Slash is not available in Extreme Style, and Upswings are mostly charged (which the sigil does not help), so sigils for those moves are mostly useless, especially compared with just adding raw.

    For the best results, aim almost entirely for Attack or Element on your sigils and one or more of your weapon's specific sigils. For a Gunlance, always aim for Lv9 shelling if you use shelling and Wyvern Fires.

Does not stack and does not work on bows with an element.
    Strengthen SnS Raw-Adds extra raw damage based on attack. Formula: Attack x 0.025 x Sharpness Multiplier x Hitbox. Sharpness multiplier: Blue 1.0625x, White 1.125x, Purple 1.15x, Cyan 1.20x. It ignores motion values, and Fencing+2 adds a further 0.2x of the extra damage. Does not stack and does not work on a Sword and Shield with an element or status.
