What there is to do

Quests, festivals, Diva Defense, Hunter's Road, Caravan and Raviente.

What There Is To Do

Frontier's content divides into permanent rank progression and event content that Capcom ran on a calendar. This chapter covers both, plus the Hunter's Road, the Caravan, its gem and Raviente.

Taking Quests

Quests are signed at NPCs. The Story and Item Trading Guildie handles the first ranks and story event quests; the Standard Quests Guild Girl carries the normal lists to G Rank; the Guild Master gives Gook Quests, manages Style Rank and trades Medals for charms. Guild Quests are taken in the Guild Hall and pay Guild Tickets.

Ranks run HR1 to HR6 as ★1 to ★6. Clear a star's list and an urgent unlocks the next; urgents appear only when needed. There is no HR6 urgent — clear the two key quests, then speak to the G Rank NPC for the Shantien urgent. G Rank replaces HR with GR, climbing to GR899, plus a per-weapon GSR capping at 999.

Beginner servers only allow HR1–4 quests, so HR5 and G Rank work needs a standard server. Sub-objectives add up to four reward slots and let you finish at the basecamp bed.

One hunter posts and three join. A bento packed at the food NPC or Guild Store Cat joins a quick list called up with X or Z while posting.

Event Quests

Event quests sit under Scheduled Quests, whose Active Events page holds whatever is running; Exotics cover mainline imports such as Zinogre, and Dailies come from the Pink Guild Girl. New content arrives first as a time-limited scheduled quest before joining the permanent rotation, and unique event weapons and armour come only from scheduled quests. An event, on live servers, was largely a schedule entry that made a set of quests available for a week.

The Live-Server Event Calendar

Four things counted as events: Conquests and the Hunter's Road were always available, while the Hunter Festival and Diva Defense cycled.

Doing all four gave event cycle equipment: the Law, Sage and Truth weapons and the Ninfa, Uiru and Anju armour and decoration series, at 40 of an event's material per piece.

Hunting Festival

The Festival ran over three weeks: sign-up, soul collection, Winners' Week. The guild leader or second or third in command enrolled the guild, which was assigned red or blue at random.

The Hunting Competition ran from 14:00 Friday to 14:00 Monday, posted at the Official Hunting Assembly. Entries used preset armour with your choice of Origin Weapon and three decorations, split into a solo and a guild quest. The solo gave materials for Pugi Cuffs and Partnyaa decorations. The guild quest added roughly a guild's worth of souls to your colour.

Souls were the currency of the middle week. Each monster had a fixed value, typically 1 to 17, paid on a successful hunt. You carried 500 at a time, handing them to the Festival Manager or auto-contributing. Three secret quests a day were open to the team that was behind, in two-hour windows, and to both teams at weekends. Soul Gems added 20, 40 or 80, one per quest.

Winners' Week turned guild souls into Festival Points, divided by contributors and multiplied by your tier: 3.0x at 5,000 contributed, 2.0x at 2,000–4,999, down to 0.5x below 100. Winners' Quests came from the cat beside the Festival Manager, using Tickets for HR and Gems for G Rank; 50 Gems and 5 Emblems completed a decoration, and a Zenith festival weapon needed 5 key materials and 10 Festival Marks.

On Hunsterverse: the Festival does not exist. There is no sign-up, colour, soul collection or Winners' Week, so festival gear is present only if its quests are in the server's set.

Diva Defense

Diva Defense ran in two chapters. In the Prayer Chapter you picked one of up to four Prayer Gem colours each day from the Blue Guild Girl and were locked to it until noon the next day. Hunting and breaking parts earned Song Spheres, scaled from 0.125x at HR1–2 to 2.0x at G Rank, and daily bonus tasks tripled yields.

Server contributions levelled the prayers, with all colours together capped at 7 levels. In the Song of War Chapter you activated a prayer for 60 minutes using the previous week's spheres, gaining its buff and the Prayer Weapon buff. Guild Interception ran alongside: huntathon quests fought for hexes on a guild board, and Keoaruboru appeared as an urgent after 35,000 points.

On Hunsterverse: Diva Defense is not run. There are no prayer weeks, server song spheres or interception board, so its buffs and GCP rewards are gone; the Diva questline exists only if its quests are in the server set.

Hunter's Road

The Road replaced the Tower and Sky Corridor event. It is a branching huntathon: commit to a run, pick a branch at each stage, and see how deep you get. On live servers it was always open, with only limited-quantity rewards resetting.

You start at an NPC standing where the Tower NPC used to be and cannot bring items in. The staging area resembles Guanzorumu's arena and sells supplies for Gm before you choose a path. Choices draw on most G Rank content — ordinary G monsters, GHC, Exotics, Zeniths, Burst, Origin — with a lot of randomness. Kills pay Gm and Hunt Points. Some branches are bonus quests such as Shakalaka slaying: fewer points, but Tower Medals and often far easier.

Attack rises sharply with depth while health barely moves, so a monster survivable at floor 5 can one-shot you at floor 30.

There are no carves on the Road. Runs pay Hunt Points by kills and depth, spendable on a store covering most of the game's items, G Rank and Zenith materials included. Stock unlocks by consecutive stages reached — 40 steps unlocks everything and never resets once met — while White Fatalis and Tower Weapon sigil materials are weekly-limited.

Landmarks: every tenth floor offers White Fatalis, floors 12, 14, 16 and 18 offer ★1 to ★4 Zeniths, and Zenith choices otherwise come in pairs of the same tier. Bonus stages have pink names, are worth few points, and always give 5 Tower Medals; even with the frequency skill you see them in roughly 5% of stages at best.

Road skills use Hunt Rank, Cost and SP: Rank gates what you may equip, Cost is the loadout budget, SP is spent per skill. Attack level 5 costs 35 Cost and 12 SP at Hunt Rank 100 for +70 attack. Resurrection Knowledge adds one faint before failure, Advancement Knowledge adds attack every fifth floor up to +100, and Last Stand trades a guaranteed faint for affinity and attack.

Survival beats damage. Transcend, every four to six minutes, grants +800 defence, +100 to all resistances, super armour, movement speed, 113% raw and doubled element for two minutes. Guild Cooking gives a free skill that persists until you leave, the Diva buff adds Divine Protection, and Caravan's Bonus Art keeps food buffs after a cart. A lance — guard cancelling, infinite stamina while guarding, built-in support — is the most forgiving weapon for a deep run.

On Hunsterverse: the Road exists, but as the server's own rotating system rather than a permanent open event. Rotations change every two weeks, and a quest party chooses which Road it plays with !road. The party holds up to four hunters and everyone in it ends up on that Road. Old Roads stay playable and keep their own boards, both original and revisit versions.

Caravan

The Caravan area, Pallone, sits beside the town entrance. Caravan quests follow the mainline structure: clear a star rating's list, then an urgent to advance. Each large monster gets its own zone, and you keep the same equipment and item pool throughout, restocking from the balloon with CP. Monsters have less health but hit harder; carving, gathering, killing and breaking all pay CP.

The Fatalis Trio, a limited route with all three HR Fatalis, was the best CP grind at 100,000–150,000 per ten-minute run; G Rank routes averaged 50,000–90,000.

On Hunsterverse: Caravan is not run as a scheduled event. The rotating routes and Fatalis Trio windows do not exist, and unless the Pallone and its quest line are in the server's quest set, none of the area is reachable.

Caravan Gems

The Caravan Gem, also called a Hunter Gem or Pearl, is taken the first time you enter the Caravan area at HR2 and approach the bonfire. It is a colour, rolled daily at noon along with the colours assigned to monsters. Hitting a monster whose colour matches yours pays double; there are eight colours, only your own can be rainbow, and a rainbow gem pays into every colour.

Gem skills are equipped from the equipment box: three skills, ten points total. Levelling means filling every colour to cap, then paying the Gem Smith — 100,000 CP for level 2 rising to 2,000,000 for level 7, then 2,500,000 at GR700 and 3,000,000 at GR800. A gem needs free maintenance every 30 days or its skills decay.

On Hunsterverse: the gem is tied to Caravan, so it exists only if the Caravan area and quests do. There is no premium shortcut to a rainbow gem here — there is no paid service at all.

Raviente

Raviente is a siege, not a hunt. It is a big snake with a single HP pool per server, fought in groups of four: two groups at HR2 and HR5 — eight players — and eight groups at G Rank, thirty-two.

Raviente is posted at Kieru in the Caravan area and cannot start until enough hunters sign up: 4 to 8 at HR2 and HR5, 24 to 32 at G Rank. Only the organiser starts it.

Slayers hit the snake; HR2 and HR5 have nine hunting quests across three islands. Support players deliver items to a Felyne by the equipment tent to power five abilities: Resurrection, Destruction, Sedation, Poison and Recovery. Rage lowers every hitbox's weakness by 10, which is why Sedation matters. Phases pay 50,000 and breaks 20,000 — 40,000 at G Rank — with 1,000 deducted per faint.

G Rank is the organised version: four supporters and everyone else hunting, in parties built around one Hunting Horn, one impact, one cutting and one shot weapon, because the snake builds resistance to damage types it takes repeatedly. After the kill comes the carving phase, a separate quest open for 24 hours with three minutes to carve; every spot holds nine carves, and full teams earn more.

Gems convert to g and Gg points, and a G Rank weapon needs 2,795,000 Gg — roughly 117–140 premium runs — with the final form locked once you commit. A full armour set takes around 400 Berserk runs, mostly for Determination.

On Hunsterverse: Raviente is not a calendar event, so nothing about it depends on Capcom's schedule; it exists here if the server's quest set includes the Raviente quests. The 24-to-32 player requirement is the real constraint — it needs an organised group rather than a scheduled event.
