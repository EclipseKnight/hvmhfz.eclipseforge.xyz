    Hunter's Road

    The Hunter's Road is a branching huntathon that replaced the Tower. This page covers how a run works, the stores and Road skills, Tower weapons and armour, White Fatalis and Duremudira, and using Road points for Hiden and Raviente grind.

    How the Road works

    On the original servers the Hunter's Road replaced the Tower, or Sky Corridor, event (on Hunsterverse both are open: the Sky Corridor is the Tower menu of the same counter). It is a very fancy branching huntathon that borrows from both the Caravan and the Tower.

    The Road is always available. Only some limited-quantity rewards reset over time, so you can run it whenever you like. You start it at the H.Road/Sky Corridor counter in town: Hunting Road > Hunting Road Quests. You need GR 1 or higher and cannot take items in (the client's own help text). You start at Fort Ruins, an area very like Guanzorumu's arena, where the Hunter's Road Felyne lets you swap Gm (Gems) for items and pick one of two destinations each time.

    Most monsters are tuned to end up around a normal G Rank monster, whatever their tier was before Zeniths. The multipliers below hold up to floor 40. Many monsters use non-standard rates for their Road versions, so they are actually noticeably lower in health. These are the original servers' figures; they were not checked on Hunsterverse's Roads.

    The choices have a wide range. They can include almost any standard G Rank content: normal G monsters, GHC, Exotics, Zeniths, Burst and Origin. There is some logic to it and a few are fixed difficulty, but mostly it is random. Each monster has an amount of Gm and Road Points (RdP) it gives when slain.

    There are also bonus quests, such as Shakalaka slaying. These give fewer points but give Tower Medals, and they can be much easier than the other choice, so you can progress without using items.

    After you choose a branch, a Target pops up showing the name of the next monster.

    Different. Hunsterverse runs its own Road. Players design Roads in the Road builder and vote every two weeks on the next live Road (Road vote). Every Road keeps its own board, and a quest party picks one with !road (the solo Road with !road solo). Each Road pays prizes by place for its design vote and its floor board, claimed on your account page. See Hunting Road on Hunsterverse and Road data.

    Stores and points

    Gm store

    The Hunter's Road Felyne at Fort Ruins swaps Gm (Gems) for items, like the shop in the Tower. You get some Gm at the start of the quest and a set amount as a bonus for each stage you clear, and spend it on Potions, ammo, Bottles and other utility items. There is no limit on swaps, and leftover Gm turns into a small amount of RdP on the results screen (the client's own help text).

    Road points shop

    There are no carves on the Hunter's Road. Instead, after each run you receive Road Points (RdP) based on what you hunted and how far you went. You spend them at the Point Exchange (the Road shop) on items from most monsters in the game.

    Some items need a number of floors in one Road quest, or a number of monsters slain, before you can buy them. Solo Road progress does not count toward it (the client's own help text for the shop). On the original servers 40 steps unlocked all monster items; here the floors needed run up to 100 (see the note below). The client's help text names two kinds of gate: kills of certain monsters in a period, or your best progress (how far one run got).

    The store can stock most items in the game, including G Rank items and Zenith materials. You still need the floors (and the Guild Rank, here) each row asks for.

    On the original servers some items had weekly limits (the client's Weekly Limited tab). You had to repeat the requirements every week to get more of them. Examples were White Fatalis materials and the Tower Weapon Zenith Sigil materials. Hunsterverse does not count them (see the note below).

    HC and GHC carves are easy to get here, so the Road is a good alternative to the standard Hiden grind. You can buy HCs, cook them in Gu-ku and get Souls and Ribbons (see Road as a Hiden alternative).

    The shop has nine tabs. The client labels them Basic Items, Gatherables, HR1-4 Mats, HR5-7 Mats, Decorations, Other Items, GR1+ Items, Weekly Limited (items with a purchase limit) and Super Special. The Sources page lists the same nine as Basic, Gatherables, HR1-4 materials, HR5-7 materials, Decorations, Other, G materials, Limited and Special.

    Surviving a long run

    The Road rewards one long session, and its consumables are limited to what you buy with points. So do not play it like a normal hunt. After the first step you cannot be sure what items you will have or what you will fight next. Some stages always hold a fixed type of monster, but not always the same monster.

    As you go deeper, monster attack multipliers rise a lot while their health stays about the same. You need to play better in later stages, because hits that were not one-shots before may become one-shots.

    Fixed stages

    On the original servers' Road (Hunsterverse's Roads are designed by players and change with each vote, see the note at the end of this section):

    StepWhat appears

    12 stepsZenith ★1
    14 stepsZenith ★2
    16 stepsZenith ★3

    Nothing else is truly fixed. You will almost always get Zenith choices in pairs of the same tier (such as a choice of two ★2 Zeniths), unless you roll a Bonus Stage.

    You needed to complete 50 stages to unlock everything on the Road on the original servers (on Hunsterverse the top shop rows need 100 floors, see Stores and points). These things help you last that long.

    Things that help

    Transcend
    A very large buff to several stats that you can turn on roughly every 4 to 6 minutes, depending on how far you have levelled it. Maxed Transcend lasts two minutes and gives +800 Defense, +100 to all Elemental Resistances, Super Armour, Movement Speed Up, 113% Raw Damage and 200% Elemental Damage (113% for ranged). It makes you immune to all blights and lets you survive almost any single hit if your base defense is high enough. See Transcend. Caution: the client's own help text lists "Hunting Road Quests (Special Slay)" among the quests where Transcend cannot be used, so check it in game before you count on it here.
    Guild Cooking
    A meal prepared in the Guild Hall gives a skill without using a skill slot. On the Road, the food effects last until you leave the Road.
    Diva Buff
    Gives Divine Protection, which helps you survive harder monsters.
    Guild Pugis
    As in any content, a fed and happy Guild Pugi applies an extra minor skill. The best Guild Pugi for the Road is usually the fluffy sheep pugi, which halves stamina use on actions such as evading. The watermelon status-attack Pugi can also help if you use a status build.
    Caravan skills
    The most useful is Bonus Art, which keeps your food buffs after you cart. Lesser useful ones: Recovery Items Up (1.10x healing from Potions, Mega Potions and Lifepowders), KO Technique (a flat 1.10x stun), and quality-of-life skills such as Unstable Defender, Perfect Defense or Weapon Art. The guide's author does not know whether skills with trigger limits reset for every monster, but thinks they should. See Caravan skills.

    Any skills you have when you start the Road quest last the whole run. Guild Food and the Diva Song normally last only an hour and a half. But if you spend 8 hours on the Road without returning, you keep both effects for the whole time.

    Good skills to have

    Vampirism
    Lets you skip most healing while boosting your attack. It makes small mistakes easy to recover from and stops lesser monsters from whittling your health down.
    Vigorous
    Gives 1.15x attack while you are above 100 Health. It is a big boost on the Road, without the risk that even lesser monsters bring when you use Adrenaline. The Zenith skill Vigorous Up adds +100 True Raw for melee weapons and +50 True Raw for ranged weapons. You can get it on Zenith Midogaron's Waist, on Tower Weapon Zenith Sigils, or on the Vigorous Up Cuff.
    All Res Up
    You can gain +20 to every resistance with enough points or with Guild Food. Add a decent base set (5 in each element), SR skills (+19 All Res) and the Road All Res skill (+15), and you get an extra 49 resistance to every type. That can make you immune to all Extreme Blights on the Road.
    Hunger Negated
    Stops your stamina going down. You can get it with Guild Cooking.
    Divine Protection
    Gives you a chance to take no damage. The Diva Buff gives it.
    Wide-Area +3
    Lets you heal others without carrying Lifepowder. You can get it with Guild Cooking or an FP Exotic piece.
    Encourage
    Gives everyone evasion and stun immunity. Having it in your party lets people take more DPS skills and saves lives. Encourage Up also gives the whole party Stamina Recovery (Large) and Peerless, which helps everyone manage stamina.

    Weapon choice for a deep run: Lance

    Of all weapons, the Lance gives the best survivability on the Road. Guarding gives you infinite stamina, Lifepowder effects and attack buffs. With Guard Up you can chain guards even when attacked from behind. This blend of survivability and support means 4 Lance players can go deep without much risk.

    Tower Lances can use Guard Up or Reflect Up sigils while also giving Guard+2 or Reflect+3. They are weaker than a maxed Zenith weapon, but a Raw Lance will be within a few points of the raw value of a Zenith one.

    Works the same. Surviving the fights works the same, because combat is client content. What the floors hold changes with each Road, so check Road data for the current one.

    Tower Medals

    On the original servers Tower Medals came only from Bonus Stages on the Hunter's Road (the client's help text calls them Guild Pin Medals). You use them in place of points for items sold only in the Tower Medal store. A Bonus Stage always gives 5 medals, but they have low spawn rates. Even with the Road skill that makes them spawn more often (Bonus Encounter), you will see about 5% Bonus Stages at most. The Rewards Up Road skill adds to the medals you earn.

    Bonus Stages are easy to spot: the destination shows in a different text colour (pink, the guide says) and the target monsters are easier than usual. They are worth very few points.

    White Fatalis

    White Fatalis can be a target on the Road, and he appears as an option every 10 floors. On the original servers you had to kill three White Fatalis in a week to unlock all his materials, and spend 19,800 P to buy them all.

    The fight is harder than a normal monster and about as hard as a Zenith. He has Ultra Roar, Ultra Quake and Ultra Wind Pressure, and attacks that one-shot you through any amount of health or survivability skills.

    The biggest one-shot is chained from a roar with a long start-up. If you are too far away you are very likely to die. If you stand next to Fatalis' legs, you avoid the one-shot completely and make him stagger himself.

    On the original servers there was a weekly limit on the materials you could claim from White Fatalis, so you could not finish everything he has in one week. You could complete a single decoration or armour piece in a week.

    White Fatalis' weapons all have the Emperor's Roar element, a hybrid of Thunder (150%) and Dragon (50%).

    Different. The fight is game content. The three kills a week, the 19,800 P and the weekly limit do not apply here. The Hunting Road shop sells the ten White Fatalis materials (GR1+ Items tab, 500 RdP each) to anyone who has killed White Fatalis on the Road (not tested in game), with no weekly limit, and the White Fatalis GX decorations for 30,000 RdP each (Super Special tab, from GR 1). Whether the current Road offers him was not checked; the floors change with each Road.

    Hunter's Road skills

    Like the Tower, the Road has special skills you can switch on and manage: more revivals, more attack, more Gm and other utility skills. They are one system with the old Tower skills: the Hunting Road Skills menu (see Status in the menu, Hunter's Road Status) holds both. Three things matter: Road Rank, Road Skill Points and Cost.

    • Road Rank (RR): it goes up with Road Rank Points earned in Road quests. If you are below the required rank, you cannot learn the skill.
    • Cost: the total amount of skills you can equip (the client's Road Skill cost cap). Each skill has its own Cost, and you have a total Cost you can allocate. You must equip a skill to get its effect.
    • Road SP (Road Skill Points, RSP): like Cost, but used for learning the skill. Each Road Rank you gain gives some.

    Example: the Lv5 Attack skill costs 15 Cost to equip and 12 SP in total to learn, and needs Road Rank 100. In the tables, the number in brackets is the total SP you have spent on that skill up to that level.

    You raise your total Cost, SP and Rank over time by playing the Road and using special items: the Road shop sells the SP Book, the Cost Book and the Road Respec (10 RdP each, Super Special tab). The guide's note that old Tower points convert to Road points was for the original servers and was not checked here. The numbers in these tables are the guide's; where our patch data differs, the Cost column below is ours (Attack, Care, Pharmacist, Virus Res., Frost Res. and the Guardian skills), and Starting Gm Up Lv2 gives +2000 Gm here. Our patch also puts the 13 old Tower skills back in the menu (Vitality of Hope, Stamina, Flame, Shock, Blunt and Cut Protection, Technique, Negotiation, Gunner, Partbreaker, Treasure, Encounter and Trap Evasion); they are not listed here. Not tested in game.

    Tower weapons

    The basics

    Tower Weapons are customisable weapons you build from the ground up, starting from a template weapon with very low stats. Broadly there are two kinds: Light and Dark. Most upgrades to these weapons cannot be undone.

    • Light versions have two sigil slots and one decoration slot.
    • Dark versions have one sigil slot and two decoration slots.

    (Later the guide says all Tower Weapons have 2 Tower Sigil slots and 1 Tower Decoration slot. Check your own weapon in game.)

    Tower Sigils grant Affinity, Status, Elemental or Zenith skills, or Weapon Abilities. You can only have as many of these properties as you have sigil slots, and you cannot have two of the same type. Two Elemental, Status or Affinity sigils do not stack.

    Tower Decorations grant skills outright. If you have a Tower Decoration for Expert+5, it gives you that skill the moment you slot it into a weapon and equip it. A weapon can hold up to two such skills. Removing a decoration is very grindy: you need a decoration removal item, the Tower Catalyst, that costs 500 Tower Medals (about 100 Bonus Stages on the Road). Here the Road shop also sells it for 1,000 RdP (Super Special tab).

    On the original servers all Tower Weapons were on sale in the Road's point store, along with all the materials needed to upgrade them. Upgrades cannot be undone, so build the weapon carefully in the Weapon Sim. On Hunsterverse the shop sells the upgrade gems and the Tower books, not the weapons (see the note below). You craft Tower Weapons by talking to the Cat wearing a backpack in the blacksmith.

    The cat's menu

    Craft Weapon
    Needs a special item. On the original servers you got it for completing the first floor of the Tower, and later found it at random in Treasure Chests in the Sky Corridor. Here the Road shop sells the Tower books (300 or 500 RdP) and the Entrusted Secret Text (1,000 RdP, Super Special tab).
    Upgrade Weapon
    Uses the Gems you find in the Tower to upgrade the weapon. The gems depend on the sigils inserted in the weapon. Upgrades also need SR Weapon Souls for that weapon type. You get these by fighting Hardcore SR1-31 (Prelude), SR31-99 (Middle), SR100 (Ultimate) and Gou (Heaven) monsters. For example, the Dual Swords soul for Gou comes from an HC Gou monster fought with Dual Swords.
    Change Type
    Lets you change the properties of Tower Hunting Horns, Bows, Bowguns and Gunlances. It uses a Pulsating Liquid (1,000 RdP at the Road shop's Super Special tab here).
    Tower Decorations
    Lets you make and slot Tower Decorations. Most are found only by fighting Duremudira. Some stronger G Rank versions of skills come from 2nd District Duremudira's carves, and from base decorations combined with Duremudira materials. For example, Fencing+2, Sharp Sword+2 and Artisan decorations combine with Duremudira materials to make a Sword God+2 decoration. A full index of Tower Decorations is in the guide's Google Document. You can only remove a decoration with the Tower Catalyst (500 medals, or 1,000 RdP at the Road shop's Super Special tab here), but you can replace it directly at the cost of losing the decoration that was in the slot.
    Tower Seals (Sigils)
    Lets you make and insert Tower Sigils, which adjust things like the weapon's element, affinity and status, and Bowgun shots. You craft each sigil with Ancient Gold Shard. You can change a sigil's value by upgrading it in the standard upgrade menu. A full index of Tower Sigils is in the guide's Google Document.

    Weapon simulator

    A Tower weapon simulator calculates the stats and cost, so you can plan a build before you commit. On Hunsterverse use our Tower weapons page.

    Zenith Sigils affect any skills granted, including Exotics, Hybrids and Tower Decorations. They do not turn the weapon into a Zenith weapon, so it does not get the Zenith part breaker effect even though it has a Zenith skill.

    Different. Tower weapons are client crafting and should work the same, but the Hunting Road shop sells the Courage, Glittering and Divine Gems and the Tower books, not finished weapons (from our shop data); plan one with our Tower weapons page. Resetting tower skills is saved (fixed 2 Nov). The Sky Corridor is open since 3 Nov 2026, with our own 3rd and 4th Districts (Zenith-strength bosses). Clears count for every hunter, with or without a guild; a cleared tower quest also counts toward your guild's tower missions, the town's Duremudira progress follows everyone's clears and floors, and floors, clears and Duremudira kills go on the boards. The tower present box gives Tower Medals and Ancient Gold Shards at set numbers of floors and clears (our own list, once per character). On the Duremudira floor, walk to the broken wall to Return to Mezeporta, which counts as a clear and starts your next run from the next floor (3 Nov). See Sky Corridor for the boards and Game systems for what is Test land only.

    Tower armour

    Tower armours

    This is a standard armour that you craft at low rank and upgrade all the way to GX. Players use it widely in mixed endgame sets. You make it from items found at a low chance in Tower Treasure Chests, or given outright for clearing a number of floors. Once maxed, it counts as standard G Rank armour for G Rank Skill Limits.

    You craft these at the normal blacksmith, in Special Armour Production under Event Armour.

    Tenrou armours

    Tenrou, or customisable Tower armours, can be set up to act like other types of armour, in terms of the buffs they give. You craft and upgrade them only with Tower items, and you can fit them with special gems to get those buffs. The armour must be at the upgrade tier of the armour type you want it to copy. For example, to act as an Origin or G Rank piece, it must be at G level.

    The armour glows according to the Special Decorations in it. If two slots hold the same colour, that colour is the glow. If there is one of each, it glows white.

    Duremudira

    Urgent quests

    As you go along the Road, you have a chance to trigger a Duremudira quest. Both first and second district Duremudiras can appear at any point. Like most urgents, they are posted individually. If you are in a party of four hunters, you will probably get four Duremudiras to do at once.

    The urgent quest takes place in a repurposed tower with a single floor for Duremudira. You can buy items with Gm from the cat, as on the Road, and you spawn with more Gm in the second district. Unlike White Fatalis and the Road, you can simply idle in the main hall if you do not want to fight him.

    Fighting Duremudira with less than a proper G Rank Hiden set is almost impossible, especially solo. So use any Duremudiras you get before you are fully geared as a way to learn the fight.

    Fighting Duremudira

    You have 20 minutes from the time the first person enters the arena. There are no carts. Instead, you lose 60 seconds every time a hunter is completely defeated. You can avoid this with:

    • Revival Balls, which you throw at a hunter to revive them at once, or
    • Revival Drugs, which you use while standing over the downed hunter.

    NPCs can also revive you a number of times, but they are inconsistent and can cause problems with some of Duremudira's attacks, because they do not know how to avoid them. By default you can carry 2 of each revival item. The Pharmacist Road skill raises this (see Duremudira-only skills).

    Duremudira inflicts two statuses:

    • Deadly Poison cuts defense massively and does very high damage over time. Cure it with the Deadly Poison Cure item sold at the cat store.
    • Freezing is only done by second district Duremudira and is literal. Road skills reduce the effects of both, but you cannot become immune. Vampirism with resistance may be enough to leech through the damage over time with some weapons.

    First District Duremudira has no equipment or items of its own. He gives a pool of Tower items, including decorations and weapon materials. You gain a title on your first and fiftieth kills. The fight has two phases: the first is around 33% of his HP and the second around 66%.

    Second District Duremudira has two low% carves (Red and Black Fluids) used to make G Rank Tower Gems and Sigils. He gains a third phase after he first collapses as if dead. You get a larger pool of Tower items than from the first district, and titles on your first and fiftieth kills. His HP is split about 20% for the first phase, 40% for the second and 40% for the third.

    Road as a Hiden alternative

    You can buy HCs and GHCs from the Road shop, so the Road is a direct alternative to the real Hiden grind. Time-wise it is probably worse than grinding with Premium, but you can work on it passively for extra decorations.     Raviente materials

    On the original servers Berserk Raviente materials were added to the point store by the Fourth Content Update for Z2 (the guide was written before it, and said to save points in advance). All of them had weekly limits, so every decoration took at least four weeks from this source. The time investment is similar to that of the original decorations. The difference is that you can grind them solo or with small groups. See Raviente for the fights.

    The tables show the original servers' materials, store costs, weekly limits and the number of weeks needed for each type of Raviente decoration. They do not apply here (see the note).

    As the tables show, you need to be able to earn 547,500 points a week. It takes 20 weeks to get Determination this way. This is meant as a complement to real Ravientes, but it works for players who cannot or will not join Raviente trains for decorations.

    Different. The Road shop sells the Raviente, Violent Raviente and Berserk Raviente GX decorations for 20,000, 30,000 and 40,000 RdP each with no limit, the G Raviente materials for 200 RdP (a GX armour piece needs about 150 to 175, so 30,000 to 35,000 RdP, about one siege's worth of party Road), the Raviente Ruby and Sapphire for 1,000 and Divine Power Gems, from GR 1, and the Extreme Raviente GX decorations for 60,000 RdP each from GR 900, so the weekly-limit tables above do not apply here (from our shop data; not tested in game). Its full stock is on Sources.
