    Starting G Rank

    What to do the moment you reach G Rank: claim your Style Ranks, follow the Hunter Navi tasks to your first G sets and weapons, and set up Poogie, Rasta, Partner and Gou gear.

    First steps in G Rank

    In the original guide the HR7 Urgent is Shantien, on an airship: the gong makes Shantien collapse unless it is doing certain actions, and ballista binder ammo from the box on the ground reels it in when it flies off the ship. Hunsterverse Our client lists that quest, Legend Soaring the Skies, at the G Rank Quest Counter under Clan Requests once you are HR 999 and before G Rank (read from the client's code; not yet seen in game; see Progression: HR7). If it is not there, ask in our Discord.

    When you reach G Rank, do these things at once.

    1. Claim every GSR and every Extreme Style. There is no reason to wait. Walk up to the Guild Master and she moves any weapon you have not yet claimed to GSR and unlocks all the Extreme Styles.
    2. Switch every weapon to Extreme Style. It is the best style for all weapons at all times, except for niche or gimmick use.
    3. Start the Hunter Navi tasks (below).

    To turn on Extreme Style, open the Equipment Box and choose Change Equipment, then Equip Hiden Book (our client calls the Book of Secrets the Hiden Book), the weapon and the Extreme Style. Do this for every weapon type in one go so you always have the most flexible movesets. Change Equipment > Equip Hiden Book is our client's own path (its help text); the words after it are the guide's. More on styles is in Style Rank.

    Works the same. The Guild Master's GSR and Extreme Style unlocks are client and save mechanics and should work the same (not tested in game). The guide's tip about an NPC at the town entrance with a new G Rank reward was left out: it could not be checked.

    Hunter Navi tasks

    The Hunter Navi tasks are the best start to G Rank. They teach G Rank and mechanics that HR never taught properly, and their rewards and materials really help you find your feet.

    Open the Hunter Navi from its menu option, with Shift+C or with R1+R2. A window opens where you choose HR, GR or Other tasks. When you first reach G Rank you unlock the first set of GR tasks, plus the Sigil and Conquest tasks. The most important are the ones in the GR Tasks section, which cover your first steps.

    For any task with a quest, select the task and confirm. The arrow icon disappears and the task is tracked. Then go to the G Rank Quest Counter: the Navi quests are listed under Gear Acquisition Quests, and may also show in the Hunter Navi row of its My Missions menu (not confirmed).

    G Rank Series 1

    Tasks 1 to 3: the Entora set

    Task 1. Open the Hunter Navi and press X/Square on a pad (XB360) or Z on the keyboard to see the details. The rewards come at once, and you claim them by confirming. They are 1k Gz Cheques, worth 1,000 GZeny each (they look like purple tickets). Sell them right away with Sell Items at your box or a shop NPC. All G Rank equipment and content runs on GZeny. It works like Zeny, but you start with only what you are given.

    Task 2 is to craft the Entora G set and equip it. Turn on tracking and take First G Rank Armor: Entora G at the G Rank Quest Counter (Gear Acquisition Quests). Each run of the quest gives you all the materials to make one piece and push it to G Lv7. Run the quest five times, then upgrade and equip the pieces. The smith's menu has Create Armor for making and Upgrade Armor for upgrading (the original guide says G armour has its own entry for making it; we did not check that in game).

    Task 3 is to upgrade the pieces to GF Lv7. Like the last task, track it and run the quest it pulls up (First G Rank Armor: Entora GF) five times at the G Rank Quest Counter. Use Upgrade Armor. The G★1 quest First G Rank Armor: Entora GX (same counter) gives the materials to max out the Entora set.

    Tasks 4 to 9: Gurenzeburu decorations

    Task 4 only asks you to view the description (Z or X). The smith's Refine option turns any armour that can become a decoration into one once it is at Lv7 of any tier (G Lv7, GF Lv7 or GX Lv7). You will do this in a later step.

    Task 5 is to hunt the G★7 Gurenzeburu, the same monster as your HR2 Key Quest. G★7 Gurenzeburu quests you can take here are Fierce Howl Rocking the Highlands and Raging Horn Piercing the Storm (G Rank Quest Counter > G Hunter Quests > G★7 Quests); which exact quest the Navi task tracks was not confirmed. G Rank hunts reduce your Defence: you lose 150 for every star above ★1. A ★7 quest is 6 stars above ★1, so you lose 900 Defence (6 x 150). That can easily leave you on 0 Defence.

    To counter the Defence loss, lower the difficulty rating of your first Gurenzeburu. On the screen that asks you to confirm spending GZeny on the quest, press F on the keyboard or L1 on a pad. Lowering the star rating sharply reduces your rewards and can leave carves with nothing in them. Only use it for quests you need for Hunter Guide tasks or the Diva Quests storyline.

    Even with the difficulty lowered, Gurenzeburu is strong and hits hard. Do not be discouraged if you cart. You can also use one of the Halk Potions that the first task gave you: it cuts all damage by 70%.

    When you slay it, claim Task 5's rewards: everything you need to make a Gurenzeburu chest piece.

    Task 6 is making the chest piece, at the smith (Create Armor > Chest Armor). Armour sorts by rating and type, so his armour is near the last page. Make a Blademaster chest even if you are a gunner. You will not wear it: you will turn it into a decoration.

    Do NOT upgrade the Guren chest to GX Lv1 or higher! You would be stuck and have to waste time on arena quests.

    Task 7 is upgrading that piece to GF Level 7. Task 8 is converting it (at GF Level 7) into a decoration, with the smith's Refine option (the client says a G Rank armour can be refined into a decoration).

    When all of that is done and claimed, you have 10 Guren BM GF decorations. Slot them into your starter set and you complete Task 9, which gives more Gz Cheques and the materials to make basic G Rank weapons.

    This is one of the very few times you convert armour. Every standard monster's armour has GX decorations you can craft directly, with only White Fatalis, Keoaruboru and Raviente as exceptions. So you do not need to max armour for most decorations, which makes full sets much faster than they look.

    Tasks 10 to 13: a weapon and sigils

    G Rank weapons use levels: each has 50, and the stats climb gradually to the maximum at G Lv50. Task 10 is to craft the weapon of your choice. Task 11 is to read the description, which tells you about levelling past Level 11 (see G Rank weapons). The smith's menu options are Create Weapon and Upgrade Weapon. You must identify and gather the materials for the weapon's first tier yourself: see Items and Materials.

    Task 12 is making a Sigil. Sigils are like decorations but fit only the triangular slots found in G Rank weapons. They can add to your weapon's Raw, Element or Affinity, or have odd effects such as gestures giving store discounts or making your Halk more likely to drop things. The full list is under Sigil skills on the Weapons page.

    To make one, talk to the Blacksmith Cat and choose Sigil Production (it becomes available when you reach G Rank; the cat's list also has Premium Sigils, Zenith Sigils, Brand Sigil and Remove Sigil). That opens a recipe list. Around the 5th page you find the G level-up commemorative recipe. Choose it and spam the create option to make some basic Sigils. The most useful sigils are the Attack UP, element and Affinity ones. Made sigils go to your item box. To put them in a weapon, choose Brand Sigil (at the cat or in the box). It is probably best to wait until you have made the free weapons from G Rank Series 2, which will likely be better than anything you have now.

    Task 13 unlocks once you have made a Sigil. With that, every task in the GR Tasks section is done and the GR 2 tasks unlock.

    The Entora set's skills

    The Entora set gives the same skills at GF Lv7 or GX Lv7. These are the final skills with 10 Guren BM GF decorations in it:

    SetSkills
    Blademaster GF/GXStrong Attack+3, Issen+1, Buchigire, Razor Sharp +1, Critical Eye +3, Super Earplugs, Dragon Wind Breaker, Evasion+2
    Gunner GF/GXStrong Attack+3, Issen+1, Steady Hand+1, Buchigire, Critical Eye +3, Reload Speed+3, Super Earplugs, Evasion+2

    You will not use this set for long: replace it with the Samurai set later. What the skills do is in Armour, Decorations and Skills.

    G Rank Series 2

    Tasks 1 to 3: free weapons and decorations

    Task 1 is again just reading the description. It gives you three kinds of weapon orb and some materials:

    • Black Orb makes a Sword and Shield, Gunlance, Bow, Tonfa or Switch Axe.
    • Blue Orb makes a Hammer, Lance, Long Sword, Heavy Bowgun or Switch Axe.
    • Yellow Orb makes a Great Sword, Hunting Horn, Light Bowgun, Dual Swords or Switch Axe.

    All these weapons are strong, about the lower-middle of G Lv50 weapons. Make whichever you like. You can get all the materials again from fairly easy G★1 quests (see Easy equipment below), which are offered under Gear Acquisition Quests, so there is no reason to hoard them.

    Task 2 triggers when you equip one of the weapons you made. It gives some Legendary Rasta Tkts and more Cheques. Holding Legendary Rasta Tkts makes the Rasta bar's manager appear at the steps in town, telling you to pick a Legendary Rasta if you want to use the tickets. Enter the bar, approach any legendary Rasta and choose Contract.

    Task 3 is to get and equip 5 G Attack・Resist decorations (Issen+2, Three Worlds+2 and Status Res+3). These are a type of G Rank decoration that is not made by refining armour. Turn on tracking and take the Bulldrome and Velocidrome quest that gives them as rewards, Obtain It! G Rank Flash Decoration (G Rank Quest Counter > Gear Acquisition Quests). For that quest you may want to buy Psychoserums from the General Store. Both monsters spawn at the start and wander the jungle, so seeing where they are cuts a lot of frustration.

    If you do not mind hunting hard monsters, you can instead collect Guanzorumu materials and make them at the smith (Create Deco). You need 25 Guanzorumu Scales, 25 Guanzorumu Pelts and 5 GR1 Gems. Be sure to craft the right decoration, because four different types use the same materials. Slotting five of the right type into your gear completes the task at once.

    Tasks 4 to 7: the Furogada set

    These tasks get you your second early G Rank set, the Furogada set. Turn on tracking for each task in turn. The quests are Second G Rank Armor: Furogada G, Second G Rank Armor: Furogada GF and Second G Rank Armor: Furogada GX (G Rank Quest Counter > Gear Acquisition Quests). The first quest gives the materials to make one G piece and upgrade it to Level 7. Making the headpiece completes the task, and gives all the materials to make and upgrade four more pieces to G Level 7. The other two quests and tasks work the same way: Task 4 gives the materials for GF and Task 5 for GX. You must make a full set of GX Lv7 pieces and equip it to complete Task 7.

    Furogada set, GX Lv7Skills
    Blademaster GXStrong Attack+5, Unaffected+3, Buchigire, Fencing+2, Critical Eye +3, Taijutsu
    Gunner GXStrong Attack+5, Unaffected+3, Mounting+2, Buchigire, Critical Eye +3, Recoil Reduction+2

    You do not really need to use this set. You mainly want it for the Hunter Navigation tasks and to have it if you need it later. Unaffected+3 combines the highest levels of Quake Res, Hearing Protection and Wind Pressure. For the other skills see Armour Skills.

    Tasks 8 to 14: the Samurai set

    Tasks 8 to 13 each ask you to beat a specific monster. As before, turn on tracking and the right quests are chosen for you (which quests the Navi picks here was not checked).

    Unlike the earlier sets, think carefully about which version of this armour to commit to. The tasks give materials for 5 pieces in total, so you cannot make both a Blademaster and a Gunner set: pick one. Task 14 unlocks when you have finished all the others, and opens the G Rank Series 3 tasks.

    G Rank Series 3

    Tasks 1 to 5: Burst gear

    Tasks 1 and 2 only need you to read the descriptions. They give you GRP tickets, plus materials for Gou Toridcless legs and for all upgrades to G Supremacy Lv1. Gou pieces upgrade to Origin level, which buffs all ranged weapons, and any Burst or Origin Blademaster weapons, by a lot (see Gou equipment below).

    Task 3 is hunting Burst Zerureusu: Flashing Lancer of the Sky (G Rank Quest Counter > G Hunter Quests > ※ Burst & Origin Quests). It is a fixed G★7 quest that always applies a -900 Defence debuff, and you cannot lower the difficulty. If you struggle, use the Halk Potions and the other help items from earlier rewards.

    Zerureusu adapts to the damage type that hurts it most, and strengthens its weakest parts against that type: Cutting strengthens the legs and tail, Impact the head, and Shot the wings. Change your target spots as needed. It only strengthens a part against that damage type: when the head has adapted to impact, impact on the head is weak, but cutting on the head is unchanged. This task gives the materials to upgrade the Gou Toridcless armour to Burst (GF) Lv1.

    Task 4 is upgrading the Toridcless piece you have been working on, with those materials.

    Task 5 is making any Burst weapon. Start with a G Supremacy weapon, which needs Weapon Souls. It specifically needs Weapon Top Souls, which come from hunting any HR5 Goushu monster with HC on. To find which monsters are Goushu, look at the top of HR5 quest cards for the "Hardcore Quest" label. You also need GHC carves: the 2% carves from G Rank Hardcore monsters. Which monster you need depends on the weapon: see how to identify materials in Items and Materials.

    Always make G Supremacy weapons at G Rank level: making them at Gou level and upgrading costs more materials. Bowguns can be crafted straight at Origin level, skipping every upgrade. Make them with the smith's Create Weapon option, in the G Supremacy list.

    To have a G Supremacy weapon in the list, you need some of the Supremacy monster's materials. Do the repel of that monster under General Quest Counter > Hunter Quests > ★6. The six repels there are [Repel] Blazing Bloom (Teostra), [Repel] Radiant Glory (Goruganosu), [Repel] Heaven-Shaking Thunder (Doragyurosu), [Repel] Endless Mad Feast (Pariapuria), [Repel] Calamitous Hour and [Repel] Unsinking Cannon (Odibatorasu). You need about 10 repels to craft a weapon, but even one makes all that monster's weapons appear in the menu. Chances and items are in Supremacy quests.

    Tasks 6 to 8: Voljang and Origin

    Task 6 is hunting a Voljang, for example Knower of Primeval Volcano (G Rank Quest Counter > G Hunter Quests > ※ Burst & Origin Quests; which quest the task tracks was not confirmed). Like Zerureusu it has a fixed G★7 difficulty with a -900 Defence debuff. It is one of the newer monsters and quite challenging. Use every tool you were given, including Halk Potions, if you struggle. The reward is the materials for the Toridcless Legs Origin (GX) Lv1 upgrade. You still need to farm all the materials to push it from Burst (GF) Lv2 to Lv7.

    Task 7 is upgrading the Toridcless Legs to Origin Level 1. As above, you farm the bridging materials from GF Lv2 to Lv7. It rewards Origin Bloods: low-percentage carves needed to max out various Origin armour and weapons. They are meant for Task 8.

    Task 8 is maxing out a Gou weapon to Origin levels. Not every Gou weapon has Origin upgrades, so be careful when choosing a G Supremacy weapon to upgrade: you could end up capped at Burst instead of Origin. The guide's sentence listing the safe weapons is cut off after "All Varusaburosu, Teostra, Gureadomosu".

    Tasks 9 to 13: GR100, Zinogre and GR200

    Task 9 is to reach GR100, which you do by grinding.

    Task 10 is hunting a G Rank Zinogre: Swift Thunder in Great Forest (G Rank Quest Counter > G Hunter Quests > ※ G Exotic Quests). It has a fixed G★8 difficulty, which costs you 1,050 Defence. G Rank Exotics all have one-shot attacks and hit fairly hard. As always, use whatever the game gives you to ease it.

    Task 11 is making and equipping a piece of Zinogre armour at G Lv1. Exotic armour comes with a free extra skill, switched on by wearing a single piece (only the helmet, for example), and it depends on the monster. Zinogre's gives Thunder Attack (Large).

    Task 12 is making and equipping any Zinogre weapon, using the materials from the earlier tasks. Exotic weapons start at HR5 for normal weapons and at G Rank for Tonfa and Switch Axe. A weapon made at HR5 upgrades twice, and one made at G Rank upgrades once. All Exotic weapons, Zinogre's included, automatically give Speed Eating, so you swallow consumables (Potions, Seeds, Meat and so on) much faster. They also have combined Sigil and Decoration slots, so you can fit decorations or sigils. You can replace a sigil by inserting another directly over it, but you must remove sigils to insert a decoration, and the other way round.

    Task 13 needs every other task in the series done, and also GR200. It gives a booster set of consumables to help with the Zenith content at GR200 (see Zenith).

    Different. Hunter Navi is on (older saves fixed 2 Nov) and the tasks work the same. The GCP shop here holds only Diva Defense items, no materials, so farm the materials from the quests. Series 3 Task 13 still asks for GR200, the game's own number, but Zenith opens at GR 300 here (since 17 Nov). The whole G Rank path, step by step: Progression: HR1 to end game.

    Easy equipment

    Beyond the Hunter Navigation rewards, some equipment is easy to make compared with normal G Rank pieces.

    G8 event weapons

    Three weapons from the G8 cycle: Dual Swords, a Tonfa and a Great Sword. All end up with Cyan sharpness and are very powerful for how easy they are to get. The Dual Swords have Attack 651, Affinity 0%, 3 sigil slots and Sharpness +1.

    The DS and GS need HR2 and HR5 content, then a G★1 Pokaradon hunt for the final upgrade to G Rank. The Tonfa only needs G Rank Akura Jebia hunts, but you must have unlocked Tonfas from the Diva questline. Only the Tonfa quest is offered here (see below).

    WeaponBase questMaterials
    G8 Dual SwordsHR2 Rathalos in the Tower: G8 Survey Weapon: Dual Blades (not offered here)3rd weapon materials
    G8 Great SwordHR2 Hypnocatrice in the Great Forest: G8 Survey Weapon: Great Sword (not offered here)2nd weapon materials
    G8 TonfaG Rank ★7 Akura Jebia in the Swamp: G8 Survey Weapon: Tonfa (offered)1st weapon materials
    All three (Guild quest)G Rank ★4 Midogaron, with compulsory Back and Head Breaks: G8 Survey Weapons: 3 Types (may not be offered)All three weapons' materials

    The two HR quests were in the "Special Material" list. No Special Material list is offered on Hunsterverse, so those two quests are not offered here. The Tonfa quest is under G Rank Quest Counter > Gear Acquisition Quests. The all-three Guild quest is a Guild Quest (General Quest Counter > Clan Quests > Quest Orders, which needs a Guild Ticket); we have not confirmed it is offered.

    The DS and GS need PawCoin・Lead and HC Pawprint Medals to upgrade.

    • HC Pawprint Medals: on the official servers these were bought with GCP. That does not work here: the GCP shop holds only Diva Defense items. By the game's data they come from the exchange in the Series Quest Counter instead (see the note below).
    • PawCoin・Lead: by the game's data, 5 Quest Complete Tkts trade for 10 coins in the Series Quest panel's Item exchange tab (not tried in game). The Paw Pad Coin quests Paw Pad Coin - Lead 2 and Paw Pad Coin - Lead 6 under General Quest Counter > Special Quests > Paw Coins/Yearly Benefits give the coins as a reward. Many trades look alike, so choose carefully.

    The G upgrade for the DS and GS only needs Pokaradon materials and GR1 Gems. The Tonfas start fully maxed.

    The all-three Guild quest gives all three weapons' materials at once, but it is much harder than the single quests: a Midogaron with compulsory Back and Head breaks is easy to fail, and most materials depend on luck, so you would repeat it anyway. It is only mentioned for completeness.

    Prep weapons

    From G9 on, new monsters came with events to "prepare" for them, often with weapons. Three of these weapons come from the Hunter Navi tasks, and there are more. The quests are easy, mostly killing one large monster and some small ones. The small monsters change per quest and need not match the large one (for example Kut-Ku with Hermitaurs). On Hunsterverse they are the G★1 "Obtain It!" quests under G Rank Quest Counter > Gear Acquisition Quests, matched here by the large monster: Obtain It! Anti-Chameleos Gear!     Attack Ceiling and SR skills

    The Attack Ceiling is one of the most important things to upgrade.

    Frontier has a cap on the maximum attack: by default 800 true raw. That sounds high, but after a decade of power creep it is very low by mid to late game standards. A single skill can add up to +300 true raw, ranking GSR adds +100, and standard weapons have up to 700 true raw by themselves. If you do not raise the cap you deal far less damage.

    You raise it with My Missions: set tasks where you kill certain numbers of monsters under certain conditions. See your current one in the Book of Secrets (Status > SR Status). Both the General Quest Counter and the G Rank Quest Counter have a My Missions Quests row for these missions. You still need to turn HC on yourself if the mission needs it. These missions also raise other passive stats. The multiplier table, examples and recommended levels are in Hunter Rank and Style Rank: Attack Ceiling.

    SR skills

    At G Rank there is practically no reason not to have the Affinity Up SR skill on. It is a flat +20% to +24% Affinity, equal to Critical Eye +2, completely free. The full list and what each does is under Style Rank skills.

    Works the same. The Attack Ceiling, My Missions and SR skills are client mechanics and work the same.

    Poogie cuffs and whetstones

    Poogie Costumes and Skill Cuffs

    For the best G Rank set you need a Poogie with a Poogie Costume and a Skill Cuff. They work as two extra decoration slots that take only special Poogie decorations or cuffs. You can get the G Rank License from GR50, which lets you use cuffs that come with G Rank skills. Get it as soon as you can after it unlocks.

    The full process is on House and AI Companions (House Facilities, then My Tore, from HR4). You need 120P in total to get your Poogie ready for G Rank cuffs: 30P for the Poogie, 30P for the Departure Facility and 60P for the G Rank License. You then farm materials for proper cuffs.

    The original guide lists 7 types of cuff in three groups (our data also has PZ cuffs, Premium Zenith ones such as Obscurity Up PZ1):

    • SC, SB, SA are Standard Cuffs.
    • SZ are Standard Zenith Cuffs.
    • PC, PB and PA are Premium Cuffs.

    The A and B cuffs have the most points in their main skills but take 2 fixed slots. The C cuffs take a single slot but have fewer points. In general, Standard cuffs come first, and Premium cuffs are added in the next major patch. For example, the skill Obscurity arrived in G10.1 with only the SA1 cuff, and a number of P cuffs came with Z:

    Standard cuffs are still good for their main skill, but P cuffs give more flexibility and more points in total. No new Premium cuffs have been added for a while: the last came with base MHF-Z. So for the newest skills (such as Ceaseless, Rush, Point Breakthrough) there is only a single SA cuff that enables the skill.

    The SZ cuffs work like normal cuffs but give one of the Zenith skills instead of a normal skill. They are standard cuffs, so they add value to slots without needing a premium outfit (the PZ cuffs are the premium kind).

    Hi-Speed Whetstones

    Hi-Speed Whetstones restore 75 Sharpness in a single swipe and are very useful at every level. When you reach G Rank you automatically unlock an SR skill that makes any whetstone use give full sharpness at once. Since a Hi-Speed Whetstone sharpens in one swipe, together they are a very efficient way to recover sharpness.

    To unlock them you need a level 2 combination facility in My Tore, Felyne slots in My Tore if you need them, and gifts for the My Tore manager (depending on which of the three you have). In short: get the combination facility to Level 2, give your manager her preferred type of materials, and when she gives you the Hi-Speed Whetstones you unlock them for combination. The recipe uses normal whetstones and Knife Mackerel (135z, from the Combination NPC), which you can buy without limit, so you get unlimited Hi-Speed Whetstones. The full guide is in House and AI Companions (House Facilities, My Tore).

    G Whetstones recover 200 Sharpness, compared with 150 for a Normal Whetstone and 75 for a Hi-Speed one. A weapon with a maximum-size sharpness bar has 400 sharpness, so one G Whetstone is 50% of it.

    Caravan Gem

    You can get a Caravan (Hunter) Gem as soon as you reach HR2: enter the Caravan area (Pallone) and approach the bonfire. You are made to take a Caravan Gem and choose your first colour. The gem has many passive skills, as strong as making well-timed blocks damage enemies, or a straight 5% attack increase. They take no skill slots and cost no skill points. Read Regular Events: Caravan.

    Works the same. Skill Cuffs, whetstones and the Caravan Gem are client content and work the same. How to get a Premium costume for the P cuffs here was not checked; the Caravan gem smith was not checked either.

    Hardcore and Supremacy reminders

    These systems are explained in full on Hunter Rank and Style Rank. The key numbers:

    • Hardcore (HC) monsters: turn HC on yourself on the final quest screen (the flame icon only shows it is possible).
    • HC carves are 5% for HC monsters and a flat 2% for GHC monsters. They come from outside the normal carve pool, so you roll the pool first and the item second. Your Partner can carve them too.
    • Weapon Souls have four levels: Low (HR1-2), Mid (HR3-4), High (HR5, not Hardcore), Top (HR5, Hardcore). Example: an HC Teostra at HR5 with a Tonfa gives a Tonfa Soul (Top). They are used to upgrade anything in a Gou weapon tree, such as G Supremacy weapons.
    • Supremacy monsters are required to get to G Rank, but you do not need their items. Their items only go into Gou armour and weapon trees. Repel quests give 2 stacks at about 38 to 40% per roll; slay quests are far better for Boiling Blood (the Supremacy blood). See the tables.

    Works the same. Hardcore, Souls and Supremacy fights are client mechanics and work the same. All quest reward multipliers are 1.0 here.

    Gou equipment in G Rank

    Gou equipment starts its tree at HR5. Armour and weapons both follow the usual upgrade pattern: they start at Gou in HR5 and can go all the way to G Rank Origin. The stages are Gou, Storm, Supremacy, G Supremacy, Burst, Origin. Weapons can be started at G Supremacy level, but armour must always be crafted at HR5 Gou level.

    Wearing Gou-tree armour with a Gou-tree weapon has special effects.

    Storm, Supremacy, G Supremacy and Burst armour

    With a weapon from a Gou path that is at least Storm or higher level, every piece of Storm, Supremacy, G Supremacy or Burst (our client calls it Fierce Species) armour adds +15 Attack, +2% Status and +2% Elemental. This stacks per piece, up to +80 Attack, +10% Status and +10% Elemental.

    With at least two pieces of these armour types, you also get Sharpness Level Up, Critical Distance Up +0.3x and Power Coatings Up +0.1x. Sharpness Level Up raises the colour grade of your sharpness by one full level. The Critical Distance multiplier greatly strengthens properly spaced shots for ranged weapons. Power Coatings Up improves power coatings further (for example 1.6x becomes 1.7x). These effects only work on Hardcore, Supremacy or G Rank quests, which is most of the content you play.

    Origin armour

    With a Gou-tree weapon, every piece of Origin armour (Origin Species) adds +20 Attack, +3% Status and +3% Elemental. This stacks per piece, up to +110 Attack, +15% Status and +15% Elemental.

    • Blademasters with any piece of Origin armour get Sharpness Level Up when using the right Gou weapon types.
    • Gunners with any piece of Origin armour get the Critical Distance Up and Power Coatings Up buffs on all G Rank weapons, not only Gou-tree ones. This is a huge buff for standard weapons. It is almost never worth wearing a standard armour piece rather than an Origin one, even with Zenith armour in the Z update. Note that the extra Attack, Elemental and Status is not applied to non-Gou weapons.

    The extra attack is added at the end. If you had an Origin weapon and armour piece you would get +20 attack. With 500 true raw this becomes 820, and with Adrenaline +2 it becomes 1,220 ((800 x 1.5) + 20) rather than 1,230 (820 x 1.5). That matters little at the low end of sets and even less later, with more multipliers.

    Works the same. Gou gear effects live in the client and work the same.

    GZeny

    To get GZeny, check first whether the quest Counterattack！Iodrome is up. It is an Iodrome that gives a 50k Gz Cheque as its reward. If available, it is under G Rank Quest Counter > Event Quests.

    If it is not up, use the Quest Search function on the G Rank Quest Counter and search by GZeny amount. You can pair this with the Log In Boost (see Basics: Log In Boost) to get 5x the GZeny you would normally get from quests.

    G Break Jewels and standard G Rank decorations are covered in G Rank.

    Works the same. GZeny and Quest Search are client features. The Login boost is on and quest reward multipliers are 1.0. Counterattack！Iodrome is in our live event list; whether it is posted at a given time was not checked.

    Gunner sets

    This is not needed right at the start of G Rank, but know it now: every proper gunning set should have a single Origin or Zenith piece. It is one of the biggest DPS increases available and is not optional.

    It raises your Critical Distance multiplier by +0.3x in any HR5 or higher quest with any Gou or G Rank weapon. That is about a 20% DPS increase for LBGs and 15% for HBGs, more than most standard skills, with no real downside. Zenith armour lets you keep the critical distance and other boosts and also gain extra skill slots, so it should be standard in all sets either way.

    Works the same. Critical Distance and Origin or Zenith piece effects live in the client and work the same.

    Guild Poogies

    You can only have one of these buffs active! You only need to feed once per log-in, or when you want to change your active Guild Poogie skill.

    The Guild Poogies give a bonus when fed. The Guild has three Poogie Costumes, and each gives a different skill when fed successfully. The easiest way to get the buff is to mass-feed them Poogie Crackers (10z) from the Guild Shop. When it works, the Poogie jumps up and down in a white flash. See Guild and Guild Hall for what each outfit does.

    If you feed more than one Poogie, only the last one you fed is active. The buff lasts the whole session and only needs renewing when you log in. All Poogie buffs stack with similar skills: you can use the Luck Poogie with Great Luck active and still gain from it.

    The Luck Poogie has a 12.5% chance to reroll a failed reward roll and a 12.5% chance to add extra reward slots. You can reach a maximum of 24 standard reward slots with Great Luck and the Poogie active. The Poogie only affects the top 24 Main Reward slots, and not the other reward types (Sub Objectives, Additional, Breaks, Partner and so on).

    Works the same. Guild Poogie feeding is a client mechanic and should work the same (not tested in game). The Guild Shop's Poogie Crackers were not checked.

    AI companions

    Serious G Rank players want a Partner, a Rasta and a Partnya. Partners are long-term investments. Partnyas are mostly good for extra break rolls. A decent Rasta can be set up soon after you start G Rank with little investment.

    Partners

    Get your Partner into G Rank equipment as soon as possible. G Rank gear has fixed values for Partners: choose any full set and the best sharpness you can find on a weapon, and give them to your Partner. Take part in Diva Defense to earn lots of GCP for working towards skills on your Partner. That is a much better use of GCP than buying materials. Do not confuse Partners with mainline's Felyne Partners. Frontier's Partners have all the standard skills used at endgame and elemental values better than normal humans can reach. They can easily outdo many G Rank players if you invest in them. More in Partner and Diva Defense.

    Partnyas

    Partnyas are support AI with very low DPS, barely enough to matter. The biggest reason to bring one is that it doubles all break rewards from a monster. With enough effort a Partnya can still be a solid support, with abilities such as Assistance, Encourage, Demon Flutes and special flutes that act like Hunting Horn buffs. See Partnya.

    Rastas

    Rastas are clones of other players, so you rely on them caring enough to invest a little. Both players gain when you have a Rasta contracted or contract yours to another player, and you naturally help them in any content they use a Rasta in.     Your first proper set

    Frontier content is not like mainline. Monsters are balanced for a baseline of power that depends on when they were released. Early G Rank monsters have about 40,000 Effective Health, and the newest content has up to 375,000 Effective Health. Your first proper 11 or 12 skill set is one of the biggest buffs you will get in Frontier, and makes a world of difference.

    The guide's example sets are baselines for tackling all non-challenge content without taking forever. They are not the best, and you can certainly build far better. The guide had Z1 sets for any weapon except Hunting Horn (with different decoration set-ups), a Z1 Hunting Horn set (core skills plus Encourage+2 for Evasion +2 and Horn Maestro for longer songs), a generic Bowgun set and a generic Bow set. Bows always want Auto-Reload, which is terrible on bowguns, so do not use bow sets on bowguns. Its links for these were pastebins that we do not copy. To build your own, use the Armour set builder, and see Armour, Decorations and Skills.

    Hiden sets

    Hiden is the catch-all word for Weapon Tech Skills. They raise damage by at least 1.20x everywhere and add extra effects by weapon type (for example higher coating multipliers for the Bow, and faster, more forgiving charge windows for the Great Sword). They are always among the five best decorations on a proper set. Rather than copy sets, the guide's author suggests learning MHSX2G (see Tools and References) and building your own. More in Hiden.

    Different. Hiden decorations are not handed out as course bonuses here. Instead the Hunting Road shop sells Hiden decorations from GR 500 (25,000 Road Points each) and True Hiden decorations from GR 500 (50,000 each). Building your own set and the Hiden decoration grind work the same.
