    Regular Events

    The Hunter Festival, Diva Defense and its Guild Interception, the Caravan and its gems, and Raviente: how each event works, what to hunt for and what the rewards are.

    Event roster

    Four things count as "events" in Frontier. Two are always available: Conquests and the Hunter's Road. Two run on a cycle: the Hunter Festival and Diva Defense.

    Doing all four gives you Event Cycle Equipment. It is mostly outdated, but the decorations have many uses, and the weapons are still solid choices for a Level 50 G Rank weapon to unlock Shiten Conquests.

    Event Cycle Equipment

    There are three series of weapons, and three series of armour and decorations. You earn them by taking part in all of the running events. Any single decoration or weapon needs 40 of each event's material. Armour pieces also need 40, but you should never need them.

    • Weapons: the Law (Light element), Sage (Wind element) and Truth (Darkness) series. All three have weapons in every category. They have high element and sharpness but fairly low raw by modern standards.
    • Armour and decorations: the Ninfa, Uiru and Anju series. You will never use the armour pieces in the current game. Ninfa and Uiru decorations are often needed in solid sets. Anju is less likely to be needed.

    Conquests

    Conquests are G Rank quests against levelled monsters. Conquest monsters come in Lv1, Lv200, Lv1000 and Lv9999 versions. See G Rank for more.

    Scheduled Quests

    On the Taiwanese servers, the Scheduled Quests changed every week after maintenance. Brand new monsters and content usually appeared as a Time Limited Quest before they joined the permanent quest rotation. Unique event weapons and armour were only available through Scheduled Quests. The guide's Google Document tracked live events, but it did not always cover smaller useful quests such as those for Gou Materials, so check what is on offer yourself. On Hunsterverse, see Events and the Quest catalog.

    Hunter Festival

    The basics

    The Hunter Festival is a regular event that gives unique weapons, decorations and access to a point store. It has three weeks: Sign Up, Soul Collection and Winners' Week. Two teams, Red and Blue, compete by collecting Souls.

    Sign Up Week

    Sign Up Week has two parts: guilds are assigned colours, and the Hunting Competition runs.

    Colour assignment

    A normal guild member does not need to do anything. The guild leader, or the second or third in command, can sign the guild up at any point during the week. The guild is given a random colour, Red or Blue, which it keeps for the rest of the event. The colour shows next to a hunter's name in town once the guild is signed up.

    Hunting Competition

    The Hunting Competition runs at set hours. The quests are available from 14:00 on Friday until 14:00 on the following Monday (Taiwan time). You find them on the main non-G Rank quest giver, the Guildie wearing black, in the Official Hunting Assembly section while it is up.

    The quests vary from contest to contest. These days they mostly mean fighting standard G Rank monsters in preset gear, with your choice of Origin weapon and three decorations for its slots. There is usually a Solo Quest and a Guild Quest.

    • Solo quest: taking part at all gives you materials for Pugi Cuffs and Partnyaa Decorations. High placings give tickets for unique helmets, which you can wear as real armour or use for a transmog.
    • Guild quest: mainly for boosting your team's points for the whole festival. It adds nearly a whole guild's worth of Souls to your guild's colour, which greatly improves your chance of winning, and adds one extra person's worth of Souls when Festival Point rewards are worked out. These quests often have a sub-objective, such as breaking a part or cutting a tail, so you may need to adjust your party to meet it.

    For both quests the armour set is preset by the quest. You can be flexible with your build by using the slots in your weapon. The decorations you want depend a lot on your weapon type, so adapt to your weapon, the skills on the sets and your access to decorations.

    The result of the competition is not shown until Winners' Week. It gives a booster pack of Souls to your guild and your guild's colour:

    Soul Gathering week

    About Souls

    Souls are the points you earn during Soul Gathering week. There are many sources, and you should use as many as you can every time you take part. After every quest, the messages show your current Soul count and how many the quest added. You can only carry 500 Souls at a time.

    Souls decide which colour wins the festival. They also decide how many Festival Points you get to spend in Winners' Week.

    If you are contributing, turn on auto-contribution in the options, or hand your Souls in often to the Festival Manager near the blacksmith. That way a disconnect or other problem does not lose them. To hand them in, talk to her while you hold Souls. To turn on auto-contribution, open the main menu, go to Options, then Other, and set the Soul auto-hand-in setting. (The guide says to toggle it to Off; check the setting in your own client.)

    If you are not contributing, do the opposite: never talk to that NPC and turn auto-contribution off. On the Taiwanese servers, a guild that signed up for the Cappuccino rank was kicked from it if it had less than 5,000 Souls on the Wednesday before maintenance, even if the Souls were handed in by accident. The guide says that rule had been dropped by the time of the closure, but it is still polite to do your best.

    Killing monsters

    Every monster gives a fixed number of Souls, typically between 1 and 17. For most monsters you get the Souls as long as you hunt them successfully, which includes both captures and slays. Some are set to capture-only or slay-only. For example, the G Exclusive Midogaron is usually capture-only. You can check each monster's Soul count and requirements on the main Festival website.

    Soul Gems

    Soul Gems are items you take on a quest. They give an extra fixed number of Souls when you complete it. There are three values: 20 Souls, 40 Souls and 80 Souls. Only one gem is used per quest, so even with 99 in your pouch you only get 20, 40 or 80 extra Souls.

    Where you could get them:

    • Hunter's Road: you can buy 5x each of the three gems every week. The minimum time between festivals is three weeks, so you get 15x of each for every festival, a total of 2,100 Souls worth. The Tower Medal store also sells them (see Tower Medal store).
    • Diva Defense: gives 25x 80-Soul gems for contributing on all 7 days of Prayer week, which the guide counts as 1,250 Souls worth. This may not line up with the festival cycle, but you should get 25 every other festival at the very least.
    • The festival itself: gives 5x of each gem for 700 free Souls during Soul Collection week.

    With all of the above, and using only half of the Diva Defense gems, you get 3,425 free Souls a festival. That makes it easy to finish with 5,000 or more using the daily options, without grinding secret quests.

    Diva Song

    Once you have finished Chapter 3 of the Diva questline, you can listen to a Diva Song. It gives at least +1 Soul on any quest, and +2 at maxed affection. That is not much, but it adds up with your other sources. Use a Diva Song before you start grinding a secret quest window.

    Weapon Sigil

    At G Rank you can put a special Soul Collection sigil in your weapon to add one extra Soul to any quest. You find the sigil on the White Monoblos B recipe at a 5% chance, and on any sigil that rolls outside its fixed percentage ranges. It is a small, fixed bonus that stacks with all other sources.

    Secret Quests

    Secret Quests keep the Soul totals of the two teams close until the end. There are always three Secret Quests a day. During the week they are only open to the team that is behind, and they open for two-hour windows at 06:00 / 14:00 / 22:00 Taiwan time. From 14:00 on Saturday until 06:00 on Monday, all Secret Quests are team neutral, so you can always use them. It is easy to reach 5,000 Souls just by joining one or two of these windows. They are also the surest way to get a secret, because from Monday on the secrets may all belong to one team if the score is lopsided.

    Secret quests really are secret, and players have to find them. There is some logic to which quests appear, but you will do best by watching the busiest servers and checking forums. There are only a certain number of quests, so later on you can work through them to identify the quest if nobody is posting. Forum posts usually appear within the first 15 minutes of a secret. A first-time quest, or an awkward window like 06:00, can take longer.

    In-game messages and chat for secret quests were in Chinese, so players sometimes needed their system locale set to Chinese to read them. Look for messages that state the Soul total. For example, 這裡! 逆轉99魂剛炎角火飯-3 means a secret quest worth 99 Souls (逆轉99魂) for HR5 Gou (剛) Varusaburosu (炎角火) with a food buff and 3 slots left (飯-3). The key parts are the monster name and the Soul total. If a monster has several quests at one rank, join one that is already posted to see which it is, or hope someone is spamming the quest name. You can also ask in global chat: 請問有逆任嗎? / 請問逆任是?. Ask on the most popular server.

    Dailies

    Each day you can take a daily quest that gives 200 Souls for killing two Mosswine. It needs one Purple Hunter Pass. You get 7 of them after you contribute at least a single Soul, from the Purple Festival NPC (Receive Rewards, then Personal Goals).

    The quest is on the HR quest NPC. It is her first option (or the second, if you have not done the Extreme Style demo quests), followed by the last option.

    Partnyaas

    Taking a Partnyaa on a quest has a chance to give you some Festival Souls back. It is usually a handful, and very rarely up to ten. You can also send a Partnyaa out each day to collect a fixed number of Souls, based on an item you give it. Do this at the Partnyaa board in the Halk and Partnyaa area of your house (heart icon). Souls arrive at 12:00 (Taiwan time) the next day. At G Rank, items that are highly recommended give 80 Souls and have no other use in the game. G Rank Weapon Gems are also good choices once you have a lot, as they give about 200 Souls a day.

    Winners' Week

    Personal targets

    While you contribute, a number of personal Soul totals act as achievements and give rewards. The first is 7x Purple Hunter Pass, given for contributing a single point. It is used for the Mosswine daily quest that gives 200 Souls. Claim your rewards at the Purple Festival NPC: Receive Rewards, then Personal Goals.

    Other rewards include the Ancient Hunting Books that level Transcend, and the Ceremony Lyrics items used to craft weapons and armour that need all four cycling events.

    Guild targets

    Guild targets work like personal ones, but they are based on the total points the whole guild contributes. They give extra items for crafting and upgrading Festival armour and weapons, and for taking Winners Quests. After 700 points are contributed, everyone gets enough items for 700 Souls (3x each of the three gems). With the Mosswine quest items, that is enough for 1,900 to 2,100 Souls with no real effort. Claim them at the Purple Festival NPC: Receive Rewards, then Guild Goals.

    Personal multipliers

    To stop people putting in 1 point and leeching, there are personal multiplier tiers. The guild's Soul pool is divided by the number of contributors, but you get more back by contributing more:

    Contributed pointsPersonal multiplier

    2,000 to 4,9992.0x
    100 to 9991.0x

    The Cappuccino rank needed at least 5,000 Souls, so if you took part you always had the 3.0x multiplier. That requirement has been removed, but you should still get 5,000 Souls for the best return.

    Festival Points

    In the third week, your guild's total Souls give you a large amount of Festival Points. You can spend them on a huge range of items, including most consumables and materials in the game. Your share is your guild's total Souls divided by the number of contributing players. Every extra contributor divides the pool further.

    Example: 6 people contribute 50,000 points in total. You contributed only 1 Soul, so you receive 4,166 Festival Points ((50,000 / 6) x 0.5). Someone who contributed 10,000 Souls gets 25,000 Festival Points ((50,000 / 6) x 3).

    If you had not contributed at all, the person with 10,000 Souls would have 5 contributors in the pool and get about 29,999 Festival Points ((49,999 / 5) x 3). Your 1 point would have cost them 4,999 points. The guide's advice: either do not contribute at all, or really try, because otherwise you make everyone else's share smaller. The numbers ignore some other factors, but they are accurate enough to show why you should avoid contributing if you will not try.

    You claim and spend points at the Festival Manager NPC. You can hold a maximum of 120,000 Festival Points, and they carry over between festivals, though the store only opens in Winners' Week. If the multipliers are high, spend any points left from earlier festivals while you claim, because you are likely to overflow.

    Winners' Quests

    If your team wins, you can go on Winners' Quests. You take them from the Cat NPC next to the Festival Manager. You need Festival Tickets or Festival Gems to take them. How many you receive depends on your performance in the previous week, and you can also buy both with Festival Points.

    Winners' Quests are the only way to get Festival equipment: Festival weapons, armour and decorations. Many are very good and can be the key to finishing a set, because they have higher than normal points in key skills such as Thunder Clad or Drawing Arts.

So a Festival Zenith Weapon costs only 20 Festival Gems. They are some of the best weapons available, and the only place Capcom consistently puts hybrid elements and easy access to Skill Slots Up.

    These weapons need the same materials as other Zenith Weapons after their first tier. The final upgrade needs 2x of a Zenith's Rare, and 3x each of the semi-rare and GR800 breaks. So you still need to grind the related Zeniths quite a lot to finish them.

    Diva Defense

    The basics

    Diva Defense is a lot like the Hunter Festival. You choose a Prayer Gem and hunt monsters to get Song Spheres, which power up server-wide Prayers. You pick one colour of Prayer Gem to contribute to each day, and you are locked to it until noon Taiwan time the next day. Your Song Spheres go to your chosen gem automatically. The total you contribute directly affects how much GCP you earn.

    The event has two main parts: the Prayer Chapter (week one, collecting Spheres) and the Song of War Chapter (week two, using the Prayers). The Guild Interception runs alongside the Song of War Chapter and a final Greeting Song Chapter follows. The Diva Defense menu has these entries: Choose Prayer Gem, View Prayer Totals, View Monster Values, View Bonus Tasks, Utilise Prayer Gems, GCP Shop and View/Claim Rewards, Rankings, Event Explanation and Notices.

    Prayer Gems

    The actual buffs only work in the second week. How many times you can use them depends on the global Song Spheres on the server. When a Prayer is maxed out, you must choose another colour and you cannot contribute to the maxed one.

    Each Diva Defense has up to four colours of Prayer Gem. Each colour has a different effect, based on the skills assigned to it. Which effect belongs to which colour changes every cycle, so check in game.

    You choose your colour each day by talking to the Blue Guild Girl, who also handles things like Arena quests. You can change it once a day after 12:00 Taiwan time. Choosing your first colour does not count as a change, so on the first day you can contribute to two colours.

    Prayer levels

    Each day after 12:00, the most popular prayer is ranked up. This is based on server-wide contributions, so you cannot steer it towards your own preference. It also means the total level of all prayers cannot exceed 7. For example, with four prayers up that each have three levels, the two most popular will likely get max levels, the third will get one level and the least popular will get none.

    Song Spheres

    Gathering

    You gather Song Spheres by hunting monsters and breaking their parts. As in the Hunter Festival, each monster's reward is fixed but varies between events. The rank you hunt at also changes the Spheres you get, for both the kill and any breaks:

    Break Spheres

    Every break you make on a monster gives extra points. A break has a fixed starting value, and each later break adds 0.10x of that value. The default is 20 Spheres, so in a G Rank quest breaks are worth 40 Song Spheres (because of the GR 2.00x). With three breaks, the first gives 40, the second 44 and the third 48, for a total of 132 extra Spheres.

    Bonus Tasks

    Each day, within certain windows, monsters, quests, locations and day or night quests can be set to give extra Song Spheres. You can check them with the Blue Guild NPC. The monsters change daily, usually give at least triple the normal Spheres, and have four-hour windows. Unlike secret quests, they are based on return time: if a window ends at 16:00 and you take a quest at 15:59, you get no bonus if you return after 16:00.

    Rewards

    You will probably need to claim each reward type more than once to get everything. You can only claim 33 items at a time, so if you have 60, claim twice.

    The main rewards depend on your personal and your guild's contribution. Contributions are measured in raw Song Spheres and in Score. Score gain is doubled on a Premium Course, so it is separate from the actual Song Spheres. The reward menu has: Diva Defense, GCP Shop and View/Claim Rewards, View Contributions, Reward Requirements, Claim Rewards and GCP Store.

    • Prayer Chapter: you can only claim Milestone and Daily rewards.
    • Song of War Chapter: you can claim any unclaimed Milestone and Daily rewards, plus Rank, Guild Rank and GCP rewards, and use the GCP shop.

    Milestone rewards

    As in most events, you get rewards for your total contribution. This is the main source of Mirage Powder since the Caravan Festival was dissolved, and of all items tied to Diva Defense itself. They can include the materials for the Prayer Weapons, a fixed amount of GCP, and the items unique to the Caravan Festival and Diva Defense.

    Daily rewards

    You can claim daily rewards on every character on your account, so each cycle you can get up to three of whatever the daily cycle includes, including Diva and Prayer Weapon Gems. That needs extra character slots. For every day you contribute points you receive a set of Daily Rewards, and a chat notice tells you when you complete them. They do not change from day to day: there are 7 different rewards, and to get the last one you must contribute some Song Spheres on every day. They can include materials for Caravan equipment and for the Diva Weapons, which you can only get from the Diva questline and these rewards.

    Rank rewards

    • Personal Ranking Rewards: rewards related to your total score contribution on the server.
    • Guild Ranking Rewards: rewards related to your guild's total score contribution on the server.
    • GCP Rewards: meant to give GCP based directly on your personal score. The guide's author says this had never worked for them as of the third Diva Defense.

    Song of War Chapter

    In the Song of War Chapter you can activate Prayers (Listen to the Prayer Song), based on the Song Spheres the colours collected in the previous week. How many times you can use the buffs depends on the total Spheres the server gathered.

    All effects you contributed to are active at the levels they reached across the server as soon as you activate the prayer. You then have 60 minutes to use them and the Prayer Weapon buffs (see Prayer Weapons). After you activate a buff, the Diva Defense Event Icon pulses and gets a timer. When the timer runs out, you lose all Prayer effects, and any Prayer Weapon you use returns to its default form without the extra skills or sharpness.

    GCP store

    There are two kinds of GCP store. The standard one can also be reached at the Blacksmith and on the Pink Store NPC. The Diva one depends on the level of the Ringing Prayer Gem, and you can only open it if you contributed to its colour while it is one of the available Prayer Gems. Stock includes items from discontinued events, and things like HC and GHC carves. The Convert Items option works like it does at other NPCs, and turns GP tickets, Shiten Tickets and Gou Tickets into points.

    GCP skills

    GCP skills work like other extra skill sources such as Guild Food or Exotic Armours: they use none of your skill slots. You buy the skill you want, and it is active for a number of quests depending on how many you bought.

    • In most cases you can only queue one skill type. If you buy 10 uses of Lone Wolf and then one of Focus+2, you lose all your Lone Wolf uses.
    • Like Carving Talismans and Lucky Bags, these skills are only used up if you finish the quest. If you abandon the quest you do not lose a use.
    • Level 0 skills such as Focus+2 are usually always available. They do not need an active Prayer or even a contribution.

    Prayer Weapons and Guild Interception

    Guild Interception

    Guild Interception extends Diva Defense and runs alongside the Song of War Chapter. In practice it is a series of huntathon quests on the Interceptor's Base map. You fight a number of monsters in a row, based on pools defined by the quest route.

    One of the Felynes from the Diva questline runs the event. It stands next to the Blue NPC who gives information on the Song of War Chapter. You take all Guild Interception quests there, claim rewards, check status and manage everything else about the event. The quests take place on the Interception Base map, a fortress.

    You can carve each monster you slay, and it fades out gradually. There is time to carve three times, because you can still carve while it fades. The Diva herself appears at the basecamp. You can give her items, and she returns them when the quest ends.

    The quests contribute to claiming grids on a hex board that guilds fight over. If you hold more than a certain number of hexes, you get special functions in your Guild Hall in the Song Chapter week, and flat material rewards for everyone in the guild, based on the hex count.

    Menus

    The menu entries are: Interception Quests, Bonus Tasks, Interception NPCs, View Interception Board, Confirm Stats, Confirm and Receive Rewards, View Interception Ranking and View Manual.

    The Interception NPCs option lets you hire an extra Hunting Troop NPC who comes on all G Rank Interception Quests. You can customise the weapon class they use and how strong they are. Changing them costs GCP, but you can only do it once per day. Each hunter in a quest can have a Hunting Troop with them, up to 4 hunters and 4 troops in a quest.

    The Interception Board shows how many hexes your guild has taken, the path the event will take, and when the next update of the board is.

    The Branching Path to treasure was added in the Z2 update. When you route past a branching path that leads to treasure, you get a new type of quest option in the same section as standard quests. Finishing it and getting the points shown in the Board status on that hex gives an extra reward, which can include things like GCP, Guild Hall Cooking materials and Transmog Tickets. You claim these bonus rewards in the same place as progression rewards. You must complete the quest to claim them, so you can miss them if heavy grinding goes on while you are offline and the hex page moves on before you do the quest.

    Quests

    Interception quests are laid out like any other NPC quest. You can see the names and icons of the monsters involved, and the quest shows needed information such as the star rating for defence reduction.

    Keoaruboru

    Keoaruboru is a huge Elder Dragon with urgent interception quests. It has the same body structure as Akantor and focuses on fire attacks. It triggers in a similar way to Duremudira and needs you to have earned 35,000 points, or a low-chance random roll.

    It has breakable parts: Left Claw, Right Claw, Head, Back and Tail. You should always have a ranged user focus on the back, because every decoration needs 5 back breaks. Each urgent is cleared per person, so with four players you can chain four in a row. A proper hunt with all breaks gives around 5,000 points, so you only need to earn about 15,000 in the gaps between each set of four.

    Keoaruboru is notable for its decorations and armour pieces, which give points in the new skill Rush. With Rush active you gain up to 130 attack, and you can run with your weapon drawn without using stamina, just by attacking or guarding continuously. The armour starts at GX level immediately and only needs common materials for Lv1, but the decorations need materials from all the breaks on the monster.

    Caravan

    Caravan Area

    The Caravan Area, or Pallone, is next to the town entrance. You need to be Hunter Rank 17 to enter. It holds everything to do with Caravan Quests, Caravan Gems and Raviente.

    Before you do anything Caravan-related, get a Caravan Gem. Enter the Caravan area and talk to Kashira, the woman near the bonfire. You then get a new icon of a random colour next to your name: that is your Caravan Gem.

    Caravan Quests

    The main Caravan Quests play like standard quest progression in the mainline games. You must complete all the courses in a star rating, then an urgent quest, to move on to the next star rating.

    They are like the Epic Hunting Quests in other games. The difference is that you fight each large monster in a separate zone, and you may be able to take a break between hunts. Doing anything notable in a quest earns you CP: carving, gathering, killing monsters and breaking parts.

    Compared with standard quests, monsters usually have a lot less health but hit much harder. All the quests in a sequence must be done with the same equipment and item pool. The only ways to get more items are to return to town when you are offered the chance, or to have a mission on the Pallone Hot Air Balloon. There you can talk to the man on the deck and spend the CP you earned on items such as potions.

    Caravan Gems

    The basics

    You can get a Caravan or Hunter Gem as soon as you reach HR2. Enter the Caravan area (Pallone) and approach the bonfire. You are made to take a Caravan Gem, and your first colour is assigned.

    To equip your Caravan skills, use a box that lets you change equipment, then choose Change Equipment, then Equip Hunter Gem Skills. This opens the skill equipping menu. You can equip up to 3 skills worth a total of 10 points. Active skills turn orange.

    Gem colours (PP)

    Your gem can be one of eight colours, and seven can be assigned to monsters: Peach, Brown, Yellow, Green, White, Purple/Blue, Cyan and Rainbow. Each day at 12:00 Taiwan time, the game rolls your gem colour and the monster colours, choosing one of the previous colours at random. Only your own gem can be Rainbow.

    On quests you get extra points in these colours (PP), based on the monster's colour, your own gem's colour, and those of anyone who joins you. Every colour adds to its own pool, except Rainbow, which adds to all colours. Before G10 only one Rainbow gem counted and they did not stack. The guide's author had no information on this since G10 launched.

    Obtaining colours

    You earn colours just by having a Hunter Gem and hunting. The colour you gain depends on the species of monster you fight. You also gain a single point in your assigned colour from any gathering quest. To see the colours currently assigned, open the Hunter Gem information board next to the blacksmith in town.

    If you have the colour assigned to the monster that day, you get double PP. If you have a Rainbow Gem, you get PP in all colours. So you should always try to have Rainbow active when grinding colours.

    Efficient grind

    The most important thing for grinding colours efficiently is a Rainbow Gem. Points in all colours cut the number of quests needed by a huge amount. Even if you min-maxed to get double on every quest, you would still need 3.5x as many quests as with a Rainbow Gem.

    You can switch to a Rainbow Gem at any time by talking to the Gem Smith in the Caravan Hut, choosing Change Gem Colour, then Change to Rainbow. It costs 40,000 CP, or is free on Premium. That pairs well with a Hiden grind, where you want Premium anyway. On Hunsterverse every account has the Premium course (not tested in game).

    Other things that multiply the PP you get per quest:

    • Colour Charm: the easiest and most immediate. You get it from the Combination NPC for Medals. You earn 1 Medal every time you kill 10 of a single monster type, and the charm needs 40 Medals. Use it with one of the Impatience charms to cut the wait between quests while grinding colours.
    • Elder Medicine Buff: a further 1.5x multiplier on the colours you gain. It applies automatically once a month on a schedule, but you can force it at any time by paying 100,000 CP to the elder at the top of the Caravan Hut.
    • Hunter Soul Sigil: adds +1 to colours when used on a quest. You find it on the White Monoblos B and Yian Garuga recipes, and it can appear on any sigil by random chance. The gain is small, but it adds up with a Rainbow gem, and it is included in the pool for the extra colours from the buffs above.
    • Premium Course: a flat 2x multiplier on all colour gain, and the Rainbow Gem is free.

    The best way to grind colours is to stack as many of these buffs as you can and do either map deliveries or iron ore deliveries. There are map deliveries in many sections. The Iron Ore delivery is in the HR1 quests section (named "Hunter Basics, Mining!"). Iron Ore lets you carry a stack of 99 and do the quest 33 times in a row without a break, or even visiting the supply box. It also counts towards unlocking the Green Armour Colour, and is probably the most efficient way to get it. You can buy Iron Ore for 5 GCP or 5 Festival Points.

When all 7 colours are at the cap (for example, 259 in each colour at level 1), visit the Caravan Gem Smith to rank up.

    CP farming

    The basics

    The Normal Caravan Routes give a good chunk of CP. Finish the 7-star Caravan Quests to unlock the Limited Routes. Only the person posting needs to have done this, and anyone can join a limited quest as long as someone has it available to post.

    Limited routes rotate on a fairly fixed schedule, which the guide's Google Document showed. It could drift out of sync after a large patch. Days start from 12:00 midday: a route listed for the 21st is available from 12:00 on the 21st to 11:59 on the 22nd.

    One Limited Route quest line has all three types of Fatalis. It is the best choice for a CP grind. Make a set with Carving and High Speed Gathering, and use a G Finesse weapon if you have one. Be careful when killing the Fatalis, because after their death animation you can end up with several carve zones clipping into the terrain.

    G Rank Caravan Routes were added in Z2.3. They are a good alternative if you do not want to grind the normal quests and just want bursts of CP. They average between 50,000 and 90,000 CP per run, with variable times. The most profitable ones take longer than a Fatalis Trio.

    Fatalis Trio

    A Fatalis Trio takes around 10 minutes and nets about 100,000 to 150,000 CP per run. The total depends on how many carves you get and what you roll per carve.

    • A G Finesse weapon multiplies CP by 1.20x.
    • Premium multiplies CP by 3.00x, and also multiplies damage dealt by you and your Legendaries by 1.25x. That makes kills faster, so you can carve more, which raises your total after the 3.0x multiplier.
    • If you roll an hour on the Daily Bonus, grinding the Fatalis quest for CP is a solid use of it. That comes to around 600,000 to 900,000 CP an hour, or 1.8 to 2.7 million an hour on Premium.
    • The Log In Boost does not add to the CP gained in Caravan. But the extra carves at higher levels add CP to your final total. It could also give you extra carves if a carving hitbox clips into the terrain, though it is probably better used elsewhere.

    Raviente runs

    The other easy source of CP is joining Raviente hunts. It is not ideal unless you can find a group that wants to farm Raviente materials or CP. Do not try Berserk Raviente without proper gear, which means already having a decently ranked Caravan Gem. A Raviente earns 140,000 to 200,000 CP per kill and takes about 30 minutes, so around 280,000 to 400,000 CP an hour, with extra benefits. The guide's author is not sure Premium boosts this, but believes it is at least 2.0x. See Raviente.

    Caravan Gem Smith

    Maintain Gem
    Gems need regular maintenance to keep their skills active. Choose Maintain Gem on the Gem Smith. It is free, but you must do it every 30 days to avoid decay and skill deactivation.
    Upgrade Gem
    Once you hit the cap for your current level in every colour, choose Upgrade Gem. Your gem goes to the next level and you can gain colour PP again. New skills unlock as you raise all colours, and you keep the old ones.
    Change Gem Colour
    Two choices: Randomised Gem Colour changes your gem's active colour, and Rainbow Gem Colour forces it to Rainbow, which gives points in all colours.

    Berserk Raviente (G Rank)

    G Rank Ravientes are usually organised much more tightly than the HR2 and HR5 ones, but they follow the same pattern.

    Expected gearing

    Everyone in a Raviente group is expected to have Hiden in the weapon they use. If you do not have Hiden, or even something as optimised as the cookie-cutter sets in the armour section, you should not be doing G Raviente at all. It is wise to wait until you have several Hiden setups and a good variety of Level 50 weapons before thinking about the Raviente grind. If you will not do the Hiden grind, pretend Raviente does not exist and ignore it.

    • To follow the meta for groups without Determination, you want Hiden in Hammer, LBG, DS and/or Hunting Horn. Groups going for the meta are easy to spot, because they advertise only for specific weapons. If a host only posts -3 / -2 / -1 or similar, they probably do not mind about the Hammer / HH / DS / LBG standard.
    • Groups always use SR Adrenaline food. Make sure you are comfortable with Adrenaline runs before even considering Berserk Raviente.
    • The Cycling Event Equipment is excellent for Raviente. It has high Cyan Sharpness and the Light element, so you can boost both Fire and Thunder with Blazing Grace+2 and a Zinogre armour piece.
    • Raviente takes less damage from the types dealt to it. If your group had 4 DS users, it would soon take much less Cutting damage, which lowers your chances of breaks. So the ideal team has 1x Ranged, 1x Cutting, 1x Impact and 1x Hunting Horn, even outside the meta.

    Skill setups: get all the DPS skills you can, plus Hiden. The vague starting meta is GS, Hammer, LBG and Hunting Horn groups, varying for real endgame groups. In short: GS > SnS, Lance, HBG > GL, LS > Tonfas, Bow.

    Recruitment. Do not join Ravientes or Raviente trains that are already running unless they have at least 4 slots and do not fill up within a couple of minutes. If a running Ravi is at 31/32, someone has probably disconnected and you would be taking their place, which will get you shouted at.

    The first phase of any Raviente is recruiting the 24 to 32 players needed. For Berserk Raviente, almost all groups recruit Support Teams before any hunters. Support is the most vital role, and this stops anyone sniping a Hunter spot before hunter sign-ups open. If a Berserk Raviente sits at 4 to 5 players, a support team is almost certainly organising itself.     If you cannot follow the Chinese, watch for the number rising quickly past 5. Busier towns have a small window to join and fill up fast, which is why support teams always join first. At weekends the window can be tiny, so watch chat and be ready in the menu if you want to join a weekend Ravi train.

    Roles

    There are two main roles: Supporters and Hunters. Parties almost always want 4 Support members, and everyone else is a Hunter. The guide generally does not recommend trying to be a Supporter. It takes a lot of communication with the organiser, and it is very important to do it right so the hunt goes faster.

    Supporters

    Supporters keep on top of Raviente's rage, trigger special part destructions, and provide carts for the hunter group. Efficiency matters a lot. Each time Ravi enters rage mode, it lowers the weakness value of all hitboxes by 10. That is enough to push some hitboxes out of exploit weakness range, and it is one of the biggest DPS losses short of missing a member. So you almost always want a team that stays on top of Sedating Ravi. Resurrection and Destruction support also need good management, so hunters can cart without failing the quest, and to add to the point pool for all hunters.

    The guide suggests leaving this role to native speakers, because you must coordinate with the organiser. It says a guide to Support would be written later.

    Hunters

    The generally accepted best setup is 1x Hunting Horn, 1x Impact, 1x Cutting and 1x Shot weapon. The usual best set is HH, Hammer, Dual Swords and a Light Bowgun. A Great Sword works fine as the cutting weapon, and HBG has its own niche.

    The reason is that Raviente gradually builds up resistance to any type of damage you do to it. A party of four of one type ends up with much less damage over time. Most parties like Hammer, Dual Swords or Great Sword and a Light Bowgun, which are the best choices for a normal party for DPS and for guaranteeing the most points from part and crystal breaks.

    Another niche is a party of four Heavy Bowguns. They spam compressed Elemental Shots. These do next to no shot damage, so the usual downside of stacking one weapon does not apply, and HBG users often go in fours. The downside is that you will almost never get Crystal breaks, so you lose points.

    Other setups can clear the quests, but with random players they will most likely follow the guidelines above, even if they use other weapon types. However much you like Tonfas, they are not valid replacements for an HH or Hammer user in KO output or sheer added DPS.

    Hunting phases

    Without Determination you are unlikely to get more than three breaks in a G Raviente. That matters little when you grind decorations, but it makes weapon grind slower. If you never get extra Gg gems, you will need to fight roughly 80 additional Ravientes per Lv100 weapon.

    Berserk Raviente has five phases. They all happen in the same arena, which Raviente damages across the five, so it turns from an underground volcano into a lava-filled wreck, and Raviente gains new attacks.

    Breaks

Each shows a +20,000 contribution point message when destroyed. The message appears noticeably earlier for the current arena host, so if that is you, signal when a break happens so people can switch targets. It is usually obvious what breaks, but if your party splits its focus and you are unsure, compare with these pictures. All breaks leave some frills tattered.

    Head is basically Ravi's ears, directly behind his jawline.

    Tail is at the very tip of his tail and fairly obvious.

    Weapon conduct

    LBG
    Bowguns should focus on the shell from the moment Raviente enters the arena until it breaks. Do not try to help break the crystals. If you are the area host, which is typical, always signal when you see a +20,000 points message (not counting crystal staggers), so people can move efficiently and not waste DPS on broken parts. Shoot along the length of Ravi's shell, because the centre of his body is the Belly hitbox and does not count towards breaking the shell.
    Hunting Horn
    Ideally bring a HH with Paralysis Immune, Attack Up Large and Infinite Stamina. Buff people, hit the parts that need hitting and help KO the head. Whenever Ravi does the circling attack, trigger Paralysis Immune and Infinite Stamina if you can. It is also good to hit the Crystals if your group composition allows it.
    Dual Swords
    Focus on shredding the shell and tail when needed while keeping up your sharpen chains for DPS. Bring plenty of whetstones. It is hard to manage and play well with a proper setup that includes Combat Supremacy.
    Hammer / Great Sword
    As Ravi enters by circling the arena, he makes a ramp in the middle-left of the map, marked by rapidly exploding rocks. Use this ramp to climb onto Raviente's back. On its back is a green/yellow crystal that erupts with paralysis about every 20 seconds. These crystals are the priority target for all the hard hitters (HH, Hammer, GS). Breaking one gives +20,000 points and staggers Raviente for a good while. If you stagger Raviente, go to its head at once as it falls. This is where Hammers and HH matter most, because they can KO Raviente, which makes it repeat the stagger at once. Also break the head while you hit it for the KO (+20,000). Then it is time to go back to the Shell, or to the Tail if it is already broken.
    Heavy Bowguns
    The HBG meta is to spam compressed Elemental Shots. You deal next to no shot damage, so your group composition does not matter, and HBG users often all go together. These groups are generally banned in the serious player servers. 7 HBG users doing the meta can tear through a Raviente in about 17 minutes, which robs most teams of most breaks.
    Others
    Other weapons are mostly about copying their closest equivalent above. With SnS you want as much element as possible, but it is basically a worse DS. Bow sits between HBG and LBG but is far less effective than either, because it cannot use Combat Supremacy. LS, Lances and Gunlances are mediocre in general and lack the burst damage of GS or Hammer and the DPS of DS. Tonfas lack the burst damage and high KO of a Hammer and the party-wide buffs of a Hunting Horn, so they are the weakest impact choice.

    Phase notes

    Phase 1
    Ravi always starts with a spiral around the arena, which leaves the crystal vulnerable almost at once. Hard hitters should climb on and break it as soon as it is safe.
    Phase 2
    Raviente always emerges from the ground just above the centre pool of water, causing a tremor. You can evade this to fill Thunder Clad meters, then dive into Raviente's body for a second fill. Both also trigger Stylish Assault if you have it. After emerging, Raviente goes into his rest position and hits his tail against the arena. Water geysers erupt across the arena, then the whole arena explodes and is covered in water balls that home in on anyone still there. The eruption launches you and makes Raviente eat you. This gets past all forms of one-shot prevention and cannot be avoided. It is common to Farcast out after the first geysers start, or after the eruption, to be in position to attack the shell once Ravi lands from the eating animation.
    Phase 3
    Raviente starts already coiled around the arena and at once uses Explosive Powder from under his head, with the usual fireballs. You can evade both the powder explosions and the fireballs to get Thunder Clad and Stylish Assault charges. From Phase 3 Ravi can slam his head into the ground, causing an AOE around it. If it is charged for a long time, it is a single hit. If he does it quickly, it makes multiple pellets erupt outwards and can kill you through chip damage. Always dive this one.
    Phase 4
    Raviente emerges just above the centre pool of water again, causing a tremor, the same as Phase 2. It still fills meters and triggers evasion-based skills. After emerging, Ravi floods the arena with lava by lifting himself up and slamming down. You avoid it by standing on any crater filled with water. It does nothing to stop projectiles for gunners. You can evade into the lava as it disappears to trigger evasion-based skills, just like the tremor. From this phase on, Ravi can hit the arena with his tail to rain down Lava Balls, the same as in the next phase's intro. They cause damage over time on hit. Sprinting at full speed with Absolute Defense active avoids them killing you, and Small Barrel Bombs can launch you clear past them.
    Phase 5
    Phase 5 copies the intro of Phase 2, and you can evade the eruption to trigger evasion-based skills. All the water-themed attacks are now lava-based. The big change is that, unlike the water balls that rain down after the arena explodes, the Lava Balls do damage over time. You can tank them with Absolute Defense if you run at full speed, and they will not kill you. If you use Determination, the best way to evade them is to launch yourself with Small Barrel Bombs and abuse iframes. Unlike the other phases, when Ravi coils around the arena he starts forming a giant ball of fire. If it finishes, he raises it and drops it into the arena, killing anyone who is not at the map edge behind his tail tip. While he does this, attack the head to help Support break it completely and to get points for the break. In this phase the Crystal is on top of Ravi's head, and breaking it cancels the giant ball of lava.

    Crystal notes

    The crystal on Ravi's back moves further along his back each phase, so you need to go further and have more efficient DPS each time to break it. In the final phase the Crystal is on top of Ravi's head. If you fail to stagger him, he drops a giant ball of flame into the arena and nukes it. You can avoid it by Farcasting, or by hiding behind the tip of his tail at the map edge. The final crystal is weaker than the others. While Ravi circles the arena, always use Dash or Mega Dash Juice and a Movement Speed Seed (CP, Caravan Store) to reach crystals quickly.

    Standard phases give 50,000 per clear and 20,000 per break, including the crystal on Raviente's back. That means up to 130,000 per standard run, with 90,000 as the baseline "good" value. This has almost no effect on the armour grind, but it means you only get 50 to 60 extra 100Gg gems per Raviente before you have the skill and can join such a group.

    After the carving quest, talk to Kieru again and choose the second option. This collects your rewards and lets you join future quests. You cannot join another Raviente until you do this.

    Additional breaks

    Even when the Raviente is dead, you can still break all its parts during a carving quest. This is a bit glitchy and the host of the carving quest should do it. Attack all the parts until you get the +20,000 message (for example with GS charges), then Farcast back to the balloon to trigger the breaks for party members. Everyone gets an extra 60,000 points, which helps you reach the million threshold. You need high damage for this. An endgame G Rank GS set with Hiden and Combat Supremacy does it within 2 minutes, but lesser sets probably cannot.

    Works the same. Berserk Raviente runs here with room for 32 players (8 for the small Berserk). The fight and gear are client content and work the same. How parties are organised and the reward odds were not checked.

    Raviente weapons and armour

    Raviente weapons

    All Raviente weapons start from a common source, crafted with HR2 Raviente materials. These bases upgrade into one of three HR5 upgrades, and each of those upgrades into any final weapon. Before the G Rank upgrade your choices do not really matter. But when you start a G Rank weapon you are locked to that single weapon, and you cannot change your mind later. You cannot switch between paths without starting a brand new weapon from HR2 Lv1. Choose your final upgrade carefully.

    Upgrading

    Unlike standard weapons, Evolution or Raviente weapons have levels and use g or Gg points to upgrade. You get these points by converting the types of gems you get from the different Ravientes. Three gems come from HR2 and HR5 and can only upgrade pre-G Rank weapons. Three others come from G Rank and can upgrade both G Rank and non-G equipment.

    Final weapons

    Each weapon has 4 final upgrades, and they differ in different ways for each weapon type, so check the final stats on a site like Ferias before you choose. For example, the Great Sword has Raw, Elemental, Balanced and Affinity-focused finals. By numbers, the Raw version is better than the Affinity version, even with Critical Conversion. But it is only Normal Length, while the Affinity one is Very Long. In many situations the Affinity weapon ends up better, just from the length. Similar trade-offs exist for all weapons, so think about the skills you will use and how you will fight.

    For usability, most of these weapons start to pass normal G Lv50 weapons in raw at about G Lv70, and at Lv100 they have the highest Raw values of any weapons.

    Raviente armour

    There are three kinds of Raviente armour, one for each tier of Raviente (HR2, HR5, G Rank). All three upgrade to GX level and can then be turned into G Rank decorations for any set. The main reason to make them is to get the skill Determination, a very powerful hybrid skill that opens up half a dozen armour skill slots, enabling sets that are impossible to make without it.

    The HR5 armours are not worth crafting for new players, because of the grind and because they are not very good. Zinogre, Brachy and Gou pieces have far nicer skill points for much less work.

    Grind scale

    A set of Raviente armour is one of the biggest time investments in the game. You need about 400 Berserk Raviente runs to finish each set, each taking about 30 to 45 minutes with a good group. Premium helps a lot with weapon upgrade gems and a little with materials.

    The biggest problem is the 24-player requirement, which means you need to be active during normal Taiwan hours to hunt these Berserk Ravientes. Raviente groups can run at any time, and were previously common from around 13:00 to 18:00 and 19:00 to 01:00 each day.

    The grind is at least 250 Ravis, so even if you get everyone into most Ravis, a single decoration set takes about 1 month. With no life and trains available, you can get a set in about 15 days.

    It is one of the longest undertakings in Frontier. It pays off in the long run, because with Determination you can drop Attack, Issen, Expert, Exploit Weakness, Elemental Exploit, Blazing Grace and Absolute Defense from any set. Raviente weapons other than LBG and HBG also have around 50 to 100 more True Raw than the next best equivalents.

    Determination

    Determination is a skill found only on Raviente armours and decorations, and it is the single strongest skill in the game. It gives Attack +100, Affinity +100%, Critical Multiplier 1.5x, Adrenaline +2, Sharpness +1, Critical Shot, and Exploit Weakness and Elemental Exploiter with no hitbox requirements.

    The lack of requirements on Exploit Weakness and Elemental Exploit is very strong. It lets you force an element to do damage to a monster that would otherwise be immune to it, and deal more damage when you hit any spot on a monster.

    The downside is that it disables all the usual one-shot protections: Guts, Great Guts, True Guts, Absolute Defense, Guts Tickets, Mega Guts Tickets and Soul Revival are all disabled. Divine Protection still works.

    The skill lets you drop these skills if you use it: Issen, Expert, Exploit Weakness, Compensation, Edgemaster, any source of Adrenaline+2, any lesser attack skill, Critical Shot or Precision+2, Dissolver. Some setups let you drop more or fewer skills. If you drop Compensation, you want Evasion+2 from somewhere: in parties you can get it from Encourage+2, but solo you need another source. Gunlances still want Blazing Grace if shelling or using Wyvern Fire. The extra 100% affinity and 1.5x Critical Multiplier is a massive buff. You may not want Critical Conversion despite the obvious synergy, because newer skills give more DPS than the added raw.

    Works the same. Raviente weapons, armour and their upgrades are client crafting and work the same. How easily the materials drop depends on Raviente runs, whose reward odds were not checked.

    Extreme Raviente

    Extreme Raviente is Z2.3 filler content. It adds a second set of Berserk Raviente armour with ten new decorations, and it extends the grind on (most) evolution weapons by another 100 levels, up to Z100 and 400 levels in total. These weapons all gain Skill Slots Up+1 but do not gain the Part Breaker effect of other Zenith Weapons.

    Fight changes

    They gave it more health. That is the only thing they changed.

    Reward changes

    You get up to 450 Gg gems for a perfect Raviente, up from 140. The bonfire NPC gives 80x extra Gg gems, up from 50x. There are two new low% gem carves, and existing carves come in stacks by rarity (2x for rares up to 6x for commons). It takes longer, but you get at least three times the rewards of a standard Raviente for perfect play, and even poor play gets more than a normal Berserk Raviente. This was before the introduction of 4 to 8 player Berserks, so efficiency may change.

    For the grind scale: finished Z100 weapons need 479,250 gems. With only perfect Ravientes and Premium running, that is 453 Extreme Ravientes. Expect about 700 hours of your life if you want to grind it without spending money.
